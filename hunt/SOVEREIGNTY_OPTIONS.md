# Sovereignty of what — options + questions

Average person consumes it. You are in the wall. Not a consultant. Pick later; first see the menu.

---

**A. Their yes.**  
The stamp. This may happen; this may not. They don’t rent you. Nothing moves without that yes.

**B. Their file.**  
The folder that has to travel. Title, record, packet. Still theirs when the next office asks.

**C. Their name.**  
This is me. Login, face, number. Not a seventh coin.

**D. Their money.**  
What they can spend, hold, send. Crowded. Still a real kind.

**E. Their lock.**  
Secrets, keys, “only I can open this.” Hygiene if it’s just SSH. Sovereignty if it’s the thing their life sits behind.

**F. Their time with a gatekeeper.**  
The Friday 8am slot. The appointment is the scarce object.

**G. Their exit.**  
They leave the app / bank / school and the hours, photos, save file come with them.

**H. Their stuff.**  
The phone, the washer, the bike — still theirs to open and keep alive.

---

Questions (answer in any order, skip what doesn’t bite):

1. What would make an average person feel *robbed* if it vanished Tuesday — not annoyed, robbed?
2. Do they already pay a priest for this (lawyer, bank, Apple, landlord), and you want to *be the wall*, or is it something they don’t know they can hold?
3. Daily, or rare-and-brutal (death, move, license)?
4. Would they open it without you explaining sovereignty — grandma test?
5. If a bigger company ships a “good enough” version, are you still catastrophic, or are you a feature?
6. Does this have to feel exciting to *you* on a Saturday, or only right?
7. Money: do they pay you, or do you live as the default they never invoice (and someone else pays)?
8. Which two options *repel* you? Crossing those off is progress.
