DESK A — what you learn (engine)
Subject = the technical field. Study = the exact source. One subject at a time, in order.
Not diligence mail. Desk B is not a stack.

1. Subject: Information theory
   Study: Thomas M. Cover and Joy A. Thomas, Elements of Information Theory (Wiley). Use the 2nd edition if you can.

2. Subject: Game theory
   Study: Martin J. Osborne, An Introduction to Game Theory (Oxford).
   Or: Drew Fudenberg and Jean Tirole, Game Theory (MIT Press).
   Pick one book and finish it. Don’t split both at once.

3. Subject: Oligopoly and tacit collusion
   Study: Jean Tirole, The Theory of Industrial Organization (MIT Press) — oligopoly / collusion chapters.
   Learn: Δ = (π̄ − πN) / (πM − πN)
   π̄ = observed profit, πN = Nash (competition), πM = monopoly. Δ near 0 = competitive. Δ near 1 = collusive.

4. Subject: Focal points
   Study: Thomas C. Schelling, The Strategy of Conflict (Harvard, 1960).

5. Subject: Correlated equilibrium
   Study: Robert J. Aumann, “Subjectivity and Correlation in Randomized Strategies,” Journal of Mathematical Economics, 1974.

6. Subject: Algorithmic pricing collusion (before LLMs)
   Study: Emilio Calvano, Giacomo Calzolari, Vincenzo Denicolò, Sergio Pastorello, “Artificial Intelligence, Algorithmic Pricing, and Collusion,” American Economic Review, 2020.
   Study: Joseph E. Harrington Jr., “Developing Competition Law for Collusion by Autonomous Price-Setting Agents” (2018) and later Harrington notes on algorithmic collusion.
