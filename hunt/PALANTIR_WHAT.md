# What Palantir actually does (English)

Software. Not jets. Not 911. Not a clutch.

They sit on **other people’s data** and make it usable so analysts and operators can **see, link, and decide**. Unplug them and the cars still roll (unpluggable ~**3**). Painful to leave (sticky ~**7**).

## The products (names you’ll hear)

**Gotham** — the spy/cop/war one. Dump in messy intel, case files, sensors. People, places, events get linked. Someone sits in a dark room and asks “who / where / what next.” That’s why “see people coordinating” sounded like them.

**Foundry** — the company one. Factories, supply chains, hospitals, energy. Same trick: one picture of the operation so you can run it. Not a crime map. Still a map of the *business*.

**AIP** — Palantir with language models on that picture. Bootcamps. “Ask the ontology.” Still not the axle.

**Apollo** — how they push software into classified and unclassified rooms without it dying. Plumbing. Not the mission.

## What they do *not* do

They don’t send the cop car (that’s CAD). They don’t lift the drawbridge. They don’t make the plane. They don’t own “the shot doesn’t leave.” They don’t have to be the two-person yes **in the gear**.

That’s why they show up on every “see / decide / protect the principal / coded threat” idea: that’s their aisle. That’s why they don’t show up on “the kit will not go until the form.”

Not a how-to. Not a clone recipe. Occupancy map only.
