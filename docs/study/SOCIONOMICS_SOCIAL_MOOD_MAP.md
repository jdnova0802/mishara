Descriptive / institutional reference. **Collective psychology treated as a named causal claim — who said it, with which measurement tool, and whether a dated prediction hit.** Not a trading newsletter and not a vibe piece. This document inaugurates the series **Mind** category (collective psychology as putative cause). It is **not** a Branches (institutional/power) map and **not** a Mechanics (physical-instrument) map. Primary sources first. Wikipedia is a finding aid. OPEN rather than invented. Pull date **2026-11-14**. Companion to the Atlas and the Religion / Standards / War / Class / Pricing maps — **not** the same field grid. Standing rules: [`docs/study/STUDY_SERIES_RULES.md`](STUDY_SERIES_RULES.md).

Do not mash machines. **Socionomics** is not the **Elliott Wave Principle** (**EWP**). **EWP** is not a **Kondratiev** (Nikolai Kondratiev ~50–60-year) wave and not a **Benner** cycle. **EMH** (efficient market hypothesis) is not **RWT** (random-walk theory). **Prospect theory** is not socionomics. **Strauss–Howe** generational turnings are not Elliott degrees. **Robert R. Prechter** is not **Elliott A. Prechter**.

## How to read an entry

4. **Mechanism Basis** — the named tool/model (do not lump wave-count families)
5. **Predicted Outcome** — a checkable claim, not a mood
6. **Actual Outcome** — what happened, dated, sourced
7. **Hit / Miss / Partial / Pending** — judged against what was actually predicted, not a later rewrite

9. **What This Is NOT** — the adjacent, frequently-confused framework
10. **OPEN Flags** — unverifiable, contested methodology, or primary data not copied
11. **Falsifier** — the numbered condition that would prove this claim/cell wrong

### Core socionomic thesis (mood → events, not the reverse)

1. **Claim/Mechanism:** Unconscious, endogenously regulated waves of **social mood** are the primary regulator of social action; “Social mood determines the character of social events.” Causality is **unidirectional**; “there is no feedback loop of events back to social mood.” Brief emotional reactions to events are “transient and do not affect social mood.”
2. **Proponent & Primary Source:** **Robert R. Prechter**, *The Wave Principle of Human Social Behavior and the New Science of Socionomics* (1999); restated on his site as the “socionomic hypothesis.” Same unidirectional clause in Prechter & **Wayne D. Parker**, “The Financial/Economic Dichotomy in Social Behavioral Dynamics: The Socionomic Perspective,” *Journal of Behavioral Finance* (**JBF**) 8(2) 2007, pp. 84–108.
3. **Date Made:** Named as a science in the 1999 book; Prechter dates the postulate to the 1970s (Socionomics Institute overview). The 2007 *JBF* paper is the peer-reviewed restatement.
4. **Mechanism Basis:** Endogenous herding under uncertainty, patterned by **EWP** (next entry) — a **sociometer** (stock indexes as near-immediate mood meters; fashion, music, film as slower meters). **Not** a regression of **GDP** (gross domestic product) on news.
5. **Predicted Outcome:** After a large exogenous shock, the stock-index sociometer should **not** lastingly reverse its prior mood wave solely because of the news; the *character* of later events should follow the prior mood trend. Checkable form: a named shock does not rewrite the wave.
6. **Actual Outcome:** Socionomic retellings (John L. Casti, *Mood Matters*, 2010, as summarized in *Nature* 467, 31 (2 Sep 2010) and in Guillaume Deffuant’s *Journal of Artificial Societies and Social Simulation* review) use **9/11** and the **Kennedy** assassination as cases where **DJIA** (**Dow Jones Industrial Average**) / **S&P** (**Standard & Poor’s**) 500 did not show a durable mood rewrite. Those are **secondary** uses of Prechter’s thesis, not a controlled identification of causality.
7. **Hit / Miss / Partial / Pending:** **Partial.** The textual claim is stable across 1999–2007. A unidirectional causal identification (mood *causes* events with no reverse channel) is **not** established by the 2007 *JBF* dichotomy paper, which is a theoretical contrast with **EMH**, not an experiment.
8. **Mainstream Counter-Position:** Conventional macro and finance treat news, policy, and cash-flow news as **exogenous** inputs to prices and to measured sentiment (Fama 1970 “fully reflect”; later event-study design). Feedback (prices ↔ real activity ↔ sentiment) is the default, not a forbidden loop.
9. **What This Is NOT:** Not **EWP** itself (a price-pattern grammar that predates the 1999 hypothesis). Not **Kahneman/Tversky** prospect theory (individual choice under risk). Not **Strauss–Howe** saecula. Not astrology. Not “the news was bad so people felt bad.”
10. **OPEN Flags:** Operationalization of “social mood” independent of the stock index is thin; using the index as both cause-meter and outcome is circular unless other sociometers are independently scored. Casti’s 9/11 charts were not re-derived from primary index files this pass.
11. **Falsifier:** If Prechter’s 1999/site statement allowed a persistent events→mood feedback loop as part of the hypothesis, cell 1 dies. If a later Prechter paper withdrew unidirectionality, cell 2’s “restated” claim dies.

### Elliott Wave Principle as measurement tool (distinct from socionomics)

1. **Claim/Mechanism:** Aggregate prices trace a hierarchical fractal: a five-wave **motive** sequence (1–5) in the direction of the one-larger-degree trend, then a three-wave **corrective** sequence (**A–B–C**). Hard rules include: wave 2 does not retrace beyond the start of wave 1; wave 3 is never the shortest motive wave; wave 4 does not enter the price territory of wave 1 in a standard impulse (textbook form).
2. **Proponent & Primary Source:** **Ralph Nelson Elliott**, *The Wave Principle* (1938), with Charles J. Collins; *Nature’s Law* (1946). Modern teaching text: **A. J. Frost** & Robert R. Prechter, *Elliott Wave Principle: Key to Market Behavior* (1978). **Elliott Wave International** (**EWI**) Waveopedia restates motive vs corrective mode from that textbook.
3. **Date Made:** 1938 / 1946 (Elliott); 1978 (Frost–Prechter revival). Socionomics as a *named science* is **1999** — **61 years later**.
4. **Mechanism Basis:** **EWP** pattern grammar + Fibonacci relationships Elliott noted in *Nature’s Law* (5, 3, 8, 34, 144). Degrees from Grand Supercycle down to Subminuette. **This is a price-labeling tool**, not a theory of fashion or elections.
5. **Predicted Outcome:** A completed five-wave advance of a stated degree is to be followed by a three-wave correction of that degree, with the stated invalidation rules.
6. **Actual Outcome:** The grammar is in continuous use at **EWI** (Frost–Prechter lineage) **and** at other shops whose counts **disagree** (e.g. competing Grand Supercycle labels of the 1929 peak as wave 1 vs wave 3 vs wave 5). Disagreement is a property of the tool: multiple admissible counts.
7. **Hit / Miss / Partial / Pending:** **Pending** as a universal forecasting engine (no single official scorecard). **Hit** as a historical claim that Elliott published a five–three fractal in 1938, before socionomics existed.
8. **Mainstream Counter-Position:** Fama 1970: weak-form **EMH** tests whether historical prices (the **EWP** information set) are already reflected; mechanical rules based on past prices should not beat buy-and-hold. **RWT** is stronger (independent, identically distributed increments) and is **not** identical to **EMH** (Fama 1970, §II.C).
9. **What This Is NOT:** Not socionomics (mood→events). Not Kondratiev ~50–60-year economic cycles. Not Benner. Not **Dow Theory** confirmations (related folklore, different rules). Not a unique labeling of 1929.
10. **OPEN Flags:** Which 1978 Frost–Prechter Grand Supercycle labeling still matches **EWI**’s 2026 paid *Elliott Wave Financial Forecast* — not copied this pass. Competing public counts (non-EWI) are **other machines**.
11. **Falsifier:** If *The Wave Principle* (1938) did not state a five-wave / three-wave cycle, cell 1 dies. If Prechter dated socionomics to 1938 as the same object as **EWP**, the 61-year split in cell 3 dies.

### 2010 Prechter six-year collapse ending 2016

1. **Claim/Mechanism:** A multi-year deflationary collapse in **US** (United States) equities from a 2010 top into a 2016 cycle low, analogized to 1930–32, using **EWP** plus a **7¼-year** cycle (not Kondratiev).
2. **Proponent & Primary Source:** Three **unmerged** wordings, same months:
   - **Jim Puplava** / **Financial Sense Newshour**, 19 June 2010, excerpted by **EWI** (2 July 2010): Prechter’s *Elliott Wave Theorist* double issue; “six-year collapse that would end in 2016”; “next bottom is due in 2016”; 1930–32 analog “instead of taking two years, it’s going to take about six years.”
   - **Jason Zweig**, *Wall Street Journal*, 13 July 2010 (author reprint): Prechter — because the 1982–1999 and 2003–2007 mania “was so terrific,” “a complete retracement” “would put the Dow back to its levels in 1982, below 1000” “within the next six years.”
   - **Reuters**, 6 July 2010: Dow “could fall to between about 1,000 and 3,000 points over the next five to seven years” (Dow then ~9,754 in the interview).
3. **Date Made:** June–July 2010.
4. **Mechanism Basis:** **EWP** “multi-century top” + **7¼-year** cycle bottoms (1980, Oct 1987, Nov 1994, 2001/Oct 2002, Mar 2009 → next 2016). **Left-hand translation** of that cycle. **Do not** average this with Kondratiev or with later Grand Supercycle windows.
5. **Predicted Outcome:** Strict **Zweig** wording: **DJIA** below **1,000** by ~July 2016. Strict **Puplava** wording: a long decline **ending 2016** that wipes a “substantial portion” of the Dow and **S&P**, 1929–33 analog. Strict **Reuters** wording: **1,000–3,000** in **five to seven years** (through ~2015–2017).
6. **Actual Outcome:** **DJIA** close **10,465.94** on 30 July 2010 (**CNBC** (Consumer News and Business Channel) month-end). **DJIA** close **19,762.60** on 30 Dec 2016 (**FRED** (Federal Reserve Economic Data) series **DJIA**, **S&P Dow Jones Indices**). First close above **20,000** on 25 Jan 2017 (**20,068.51**, same **FRED** file). The index did **not** trade near 1,000 or 3,000 in 2010–2017.
7. **Hit / Miss / Partial / Pending:** **Miss** against all three wordings. Do not rescue the call by substituting “risk was high” or “deflation in other assets.”
8. **Mainstream Counter-Position:** Zweig’s own *Wall Street Journal* piece: earnings-and-multiple arithmetic to Dow 1000 is historically unmatched; **EMH**/random-walk view that a 90% path is not implied by a 1930s analog. Buy-and-hold vs a cash/T-bill prescription.
9. **What This Is NOT:** Not the 2022–2024 “biggest bear market ever recorded” *Theorist* line (later, separate). Not the Dec 2025 Fibonacci **46,880** cluster (later, separate). Not Bitcoin.
10. **OPEN Flags:** Full June 2010 *Theorist* double-issue **PDF** (Portable Document Format) not copied this pass; Puplava excerpt and Zweig interview are the public primary record used here. Intraday 2010–2016 **DJIA** low vs close — closes suffice to kill “below 1,000.”
11. **Falsifier:** If **FRED DJIA** showed a close below 1,000 between July 2010 and July 2016, cell 7’s **Miss** dies. If Zweig’s reprint misquoted “below 1000,” cell 5’s strict wording dies.

### Live Grand Supercycle top window (late 2025 count / 2026 liquidity year)

1. **Claim/Mechanism:** US equities are in a terminal **fifth** of a **Supercycle** that sits inside a **Grand Supercycle** advance from before 1800 (Kendall: supercycle from **1932**; cycle-wave landmarks **1835**, **1929**, **1966**). A long-term top is “right at the edge.” **2026** as “the year of the loss of liquidity.”
2. **Proponent & Primary Source:** **Steven Hochberg** & **Peter Kendall**, *Elliott Wave Financial Forecast* editors, **McAlvany Weekly Commentary**, **3 Dec 2025** (transcript). **EWI** public page “What Happens When the Mania Reverses?” last updated **15 Jan 2026**: “**DOW** near $50,000. **S&P** near $7,000.” Separate, **do not mash:** a Kondratieff-cycle site quoting Prechter’s *Theorist* that “**2022 through 2024** will witness the biggest bear market ever recorded.”
3. **Date Made:** 3 Dec 2025 (Fibonacci peak cluster + 2026 liquidity year); 15 Jan 2026 (mania-level snapshot); 2022–24 line is an **earlier** *Theorist* claim.
4. **Mechanism Basis:** **EWP** Grand Supercycle / Supercycle / Cycle labeling in the Frost–Prechter lineage, plus Fibonacci price relationships. **Not** Strauss–Howe 20-year turnings. **Not** Kondratiev (even when a third-party page prints both).
5. **Predicted Outcome:** Checkable numbers from the **3 Dec 2025** interview: Dow Fibonacci cluster **46,880** and **46,750** as the area where “the market has an opportunity to turn down”; “we’re right at the edge of one of those numbers now.” Broader claim: **2026** liquidity withdrawal and a significant long-term turn. **EWI** 15 Jan 2026 did **not** give an invalidation print; it described then-current **~50,000 / ~7,000** as mania context.
6. **Actual Outcome:** **FRED** **DJIA** **2026-11-13** close **52,347.55**; session **2026-11-04** **53,356.12** (above the Dec 2025 **46,880** cluster). **FRED** **SP500** **2026-01-15** **6,944.47** (near the Jan 2026 “near 7,000” snapshot); **2026-11-13** **7,561.67**; **2026-11-04** **7,688.62**. The 2022–2024 “biggest bear ever” window elapsed with new nominal highs afterward.
7. **Hit / Miss / Partial / Pending:** **Miss** on the **46,750 / 46,880** Dow cluster as a terminal peak (those prints were exceeded by thousands of points). **Miss** on Prechter’s **2022–2024** “biggest bear market ever recorded” if that line is read as a completed Grand Supercycle collapse in those calendar years. **Pending** on whether a Grand Supercycle-degree bear **begins** in late 2026 / 2027 — that is a **different**, still-open window, and **EWI**’s paid 2026 invalidation levels were **not** copied.
8. **Mainstream Counter-Position:** **EMH** / **RWT**: Fibonacci clusters from past waves are historical-price transforms and should not systematically time a Grand Supercycle turn. Valuation (Hochberg’s “Pluto chart”: **S&P 500** dividend yield **1.16%** vs 3-month US Treasury bill **3.8%**, **S&P 400** price-to-book **7.85**, 3 Dec 2025) can be high for years without implying a dated crash.
9. **What This Is NOT:** Not the 2010–2016 Dow-1000 call. Not Kondratiev. Not Howe’s 2026 “resolution.” Not Bitcoin **ETF** (exchange-traded fund) flows. Not a unique public **EWI** “must top in Q4 2026” print — that exact sentence was **not** found on elliottwave.com this pass.
10. **OPEN Flags:** Paid *Financial Forecast* / *Theorist* 2025–2026 issues (revised Fibonacci, official invalidation) not copied. Whether Hochberg later moved the **46,880** cluster after the break — **OPEN**. Third-party Grand Supercycle blogs are **out**.

### Hemline index (named sociometer, not the stock index)

1. **Claim/Mechanism:** Skirt **hemline** length co-moves with boom/bust: shorter in expansions, longer in contractions. A **fashion** sociometer **distinct** from using the stock price itself as the mood proxy.
2. **Proponent & Primary Source:** Urban-legend attribution to **George Taylor** (often “1926”). Taylor’s actual **1929** Wharton thesis *Significant post-war changes in the full-fashioned hosiery industry* treats skirt length as a **demand driver for hosiery**, not as a stock-market indicator. Empirical test: **Marjolein van Baardwijk** & **Philip Hans Franses**, “The hemline and the economy: is there any match?,” Econometric Institute Report **EI 2010-40**, Erasmus University Rotterdam (data **1921–2009**, *L’Officiel* hemlines vs **NBER** (National Bureau of Economic Research) chronology). Prechter lists “the clothes they select to wear” among sociometers (socionomic-theory page) **without** Taylor’s thesis as the proof.
3. **Date Made:** Folklore: 1926 (unsupported as Taylor’s market theorem). Thesis: 1929. Econometric test: 2010. Prechter sociometer list: 1999/site restatement.
4. **Mechanism Basis:** Coded monthly hemline from a fashion magazine vs **NBER** recession dummy — **not** **EWP**, **not** **DJIA** as the left-hand variable in the Franses test.
5. **Predicted Outcome:** Folklore: hemlines **lead** or **coincide with** the economy/stock market (shorter ⇔ boom). Franses/Baardwijk **testable** form: correlation with **NBER** cycle, with leads/lags.
6. **Actual Outcome:** **EI 2010-40:** the economic cycle **leads the hemline by about three years**; the reverse (hemline as predictor) is **not** found. Erasmus news restatement: “the hemline index is an urban legend” as a *forecasting* tool; long skirts now would reflect a past dip, not a coming one. Coefficient on a 36-month lagged **NBER** dummy: **0.073** (**HAC** (heteroskedasticity- and autocorrelation-consistent) s.e. **0.027**) in the monthly specification.
7. **Hit / Miss / Partial / Pending:** **Miss** as a **leading** market indicator. **Partial** as a **lagging** cultural correlate of the **NBER** cycle (the 2010 paper’s own claim). **Do not** score this as a Prechter **EWP** hit/miss.
8. **Mainstream Counter-Position:** Fashion production lags; *L’Officiel* is not the US closet. **EMH** has no hemline term. Standard leading indicators are **NBER**-class series, not skirt codes.
9. **What This Is NOT:** Not the stock-index sociometer (that *is* circular if used to prove mood→prices). Not **EWP**. Not Casti’s subtitle “rising skirt lengths” as if it were the Franses result. Not Taylor 1929 as a stock-forecasting paper.
10. **OPEN Flags:** Prechter’s own hemline *time series* (if any) in *Pioneering Studies in Socionomics* not copied. 2010 paper ends 2009; post-2009 hemlines **OPEN**.
11. **Falsifier:** If **EI 2010-40** found hemlines **leading** **NBER** by three years (instead of lagging), cell 6 dies. If Taylor 1929 contained a stock-index hemline theorem, cell 2’s correction dies.

### Academic / peer-reviewed engagement

1. **Claim/Mechanism:** Socionomics has a thin but **named** peer-reviewed footprint — not “legitimacy by newsletter.”
2. **Proponent & Primary Source:**
   - **Kenneth R. Olson**, “A Literature Review of Social Mood,” *JBF* 7(4) 2006, pp. 193–203. Reviews affect/motivation/traits and contagion as a **possible basis** for Prechter’s construct; does **not** validate **EWP** counts.
   - Prechter & Parker, *JBF* 8(2) 2007, pp. 84–108 (**DOI** (Digital Object Identifier) 10.1080/15427560701381028): finance ≠ economics; **STF** (socionomic theory of finance) vs **EMH**.
   - Prechter, **Deepak Goel**, Parker, **Matthew Lampert**, “Social Mood, Stock Market Performance, and U.S. Presidential Elections,” *SAGE Open* 2(4) 2012 (**DOI** 10.1177/2158244012459194): 3-year net **DJIA** change predicts incumbent popular-vote **margin**; **GDP** drops out in multiple regression; inflation/unemployment insignificant.
   - **John R. Nofsinger**, “Social Mood and Financial Economics,” *JBF* 6(3) 2005, pp. 144–160 — adjacent social-mood paper Olson cites; **not** an **EWI** product.
3. **Date Made:** 2005 (Nofsinger), 2006 (Olson), 2007 (Prechter–Parker), 2012 (elections).
4. **Mechanism Basis:** For 2012: stock-index **sociometer** as regressor of vote margin — **not** an Elliott count. For 2007: theoretical dichotomy, not a wave label.
5. **Predicted Outcome:** 2012 paper: net 3-year **DJIA** gain (loss) → higher (lower) incumbent vote margin; **GDP**/inflation/unemployment weaker. Olson: social mood is **rooted in** established personality dimensions (a literature claim, not a price forecast).
6. **Actual Outcome:** 2012 article is published, **SSRN** (Social Science Research Network) highly downloaded that year (authors’/institute claim). It is **SAGE Open** (author-pays open), not *Journal of Finance*. Olson 2006 is a **review**, not a new identification of unidirectional causality.
7. **Hit / Miss / Partial / Pending:** **Hit** as “papers exist in *JBF* / *SAGE Open* with the socionomic hypothesis in the abstract.” **Pending** as “the profession accepted **EWP** Grand Supercycle forecasting” — it did not; that is a different claim.
8. **Mainstream Counter-Position:** Economic-voting literature (e.g. **GDP**, unemployment) as the default incumbent model; the 2012 paper argues those lose a horse-race with the **DJIA**. Event-study **EMH** still treats news as information, not as a mood epiphenomenon.
9. **What This Is NOT:** Not a *Nature* research article (the 2010 *Nature* piece is a **book review** of Casti). Not replication of 2012 on 2016/2020/2024 (not copied here). Not proof of five-wave structure.
10. **OPEN Flags:** Socionomics Foundation working papers beyond the three named journal items — not inventoried. *SAGE Open* editorial standards vs *JBF* vs *Journal of Finance* are **different machines**; do not cite download rank as impact factor.
11. **Falsifier:** If *JBF* 8(2) 2007 were not Prechter & Parker, cell 2 dies. If the 2012 abstract did not claim **DJIA** dominates **GDP** in multiple regression, cell 5 dies.

### Efficient market hypothesis / random walk (mainstream counter)

1. **Claim/Mechanism:** Security prices “fully reflect” available information. **Fama 1970** tests by information set: **weak** (historical prices), **semi-strong** (public announcements), **strong** (private information). **RWT** = independent, identically distributed increments — **stronger** than the fair-game **EMH** model and **not** implied by it.
2. **Proponent & Primary Source:** **Eugene F. Fama**, “Efficient Capital Markets: A Review of Theory and Empirical Work,” *The Journal of Finance* 25(2) May 1970, pp. 383–417. Session chair **Burton G. Malkiel**. Popular statement: Malkiel, *A Random Walk Down Wall Street* (1973); restated in Malkiel, “The Efficient Market Hypothesis and Its Critics,” *Journal of Economic Perspectives* 17(1) 2003.
3. **Date Made:** 1970 (review); random-walk empirical work **preceded** the theory (Fama’s own historical note).
4. **Mechanism Basis:** Expected-return / fair-game model; weak-form tests of mechanical rules vs buy-and-hold. **Not** a mood fractal.
5. **Predicted Outcome:** Technical systems that use only past prices (including **EWP** counts) should not earn extra expected profit vs buy-and-hold after risk (Fama 1970 submartingale implication).
6. **Actual Outcome:** Fama 1970 concluded the model “stands up well” with “few exceptions.” Later anomalies and behavioral literature (including Fama’s own 1991/1998 revisits) **qualify** but do not replace the 1970 taxonomy. **EWP** remains classified as technical analysis under the weak-form heading.
7. **Hit / Miss / Partial / Pending:** **Hit** as the still-standard **counter-taxonomy** against which socionomics defines itself (Prechter’s **STF** “opposes **EMH** on ten major points,” robertprechter.com/financial-theory). **Partial** as a claim that markets have no predictable structure whatsoever — Fama already separated **EMH** from **RWT**.
8. **Mainstream Counter-Position:** This *is* the mainstream counter. Socionomics’ reply is **STF** (Prechter 2016 *The Socionomic Theory of Finance*): finance herding vs goods-market supply and demand.
9. **What This Is NOT:** Not prospect theory (micro anomalies can exist **inside** a debate about efficiency). Not “prices are a coin flip every day” (**RWT** iid is the extra assumption). Not a denial that volatility clusters.
10. **OPEN Flags:** Fama 1991 “efficient markets II” three-form relabel vs 1970 — cited as later, not copied as a table. Joint-hypothesis problem (efficiency + equilibrium model) is **OPEN** as a full restatement.
11. **Falsifier:** If Fama 1970 equated **EMH** with iid random walks in §II.C (he **distinguishes** them), cell 1’s split dies. If Prechter’s **STF** page accepted **EMH** for financial assets, cell 8’s opposition dies.

### Socionomics ≠ mainstream behavioral economics (Kahneman / Tversky)

1. **Claim/Mechanism:** **Prospect theory** describes **individual** choice under risk: value on **gains and losses** (not final wealth); **decision weights** ≠ probabilities; value function typically **concave for gains, convex for losses, steeper for losses** (loss aversion); certainty and isolation effects.
2. **Proponent & Primary Source:** **Daniel Kahneman** & **Amos Tversky**, “Prospect Theory: An Analysis of Decision under Risk,” *Econometrica* 47(2) Mar 1979, pp. 263–291.
3. **Date Made:** March 1979 (same decade Prechter dates socionomic postulates; **not** the same paper).
4. **Mechanism Basis:** Laboratory / survey choice problems vs expected-utility theory. **Not** **EWP**. **Not** unidirectional social-mood waves.
5. **Predicted Outcome:** Checkable: people reject expected-utility predictions in the paper’s named problems (certainty effect, reflection, isolation). **Not** a Dow target.
6. **Actual Outcome:** Prospect theory became the canonical micro foundation of **behavioral economics** / **behavioral finance** (with later cumulative prospect theory, 1992). It licenses **biases** and **limits to arbitrage**; it does **not** license a Grand Supercycle label.
7. **Hit / Miss / Partial / Pending:** **Hit** as a separate, peer-reviewed field. **Miss** if used as “Kahneman proved Prechter.”
8. **Mainstream Counter-Position:** Expected-utility / **EMH** defenders treat prospect-theory biases as **micro** and often **arbitraged**. Prechter **STF**: herding is **unconscious and endogenous** at the **aggregate**, not a list of individual biases about odds.
9. **What This Is NOT:** Not socionomics. Not **EWP**. Not Thaler’s *Misbehaving* catalog as if it were *The Wave Principle of Human Social Behavior*. Not loss-aversion as a 5-wave rule.
10. **OPEN Flags:** Whether any *Econometrica* / *Quarterly Journal of Economics* paper estimates an Elliott fractal as the aggregator of prospect-theory agents — **not found** this pass.
11. **Falsifier:** If Kahneman & Tversky 1979 derived a five-wave aggregate from the value function, cell 9 dies. If Prechter 2007 *JBF* claimed **STF** **is** prospect theory, cell 8’s distinction dies.

### Socionomics ≠ Strauss–Howe generational theory (Fourth Turning)

1. **Claim/Mechanism:** Anglo-American history moves in a **saeculum** (~80–100 years) of four **turnings** (~20 years): **High**, **Awakening**, **Unraveling**, **Crisis** (Fourth Turning). Recurring generational archetypes (Prophet, Nomad, Hero, Artist). Civic institutions rebuild after Crisis.
2. **Proponent & Primary Source:** **William Strauss** & **Neil Howe**, *Generations* (1991); *The Fourth Turning* (1997). 1997 timing sentence (quoted in later press from the book): “If the Crisis catalyst comes on schedule, around the year **2005**, then the climax will be due around **2020**, the resolution around **2026**.” Howe, *The Fourth Turning Is Here* (2023): Crisis **began 2008**; remaining ~decade; gate “sometime before the **mid-2030s**.”
3. **Date Made:** 1991 / 1997 / 2023 revision.
4. **Mechanism Basis:** Generational **cohort** location in a four-phase institutional cycle. **Not** **EWP** degrees. **Not** stock-index sociometers as the engine.
5. **Predicted Outcome:** **1997 text:** catalyst ~**2005**, climax ~**2020**, resolution ~**2026**. **2023 text:** climax/resolution **slipped** to the **2030s**. These are **two** dated machines — do not average.
