# Cycles of contention

**Subject:** *contentious politics* (Charles Tilly, Sidney Tarrow, Doug McAdam). Street phrase: social-movement waves. Feminism is one family inside this file, not the spine. Not Nisaba. Not a camp.

A **cycle of contention** is a temporally clustered rise and fall of collective claim-making — protest, strike, campaign, revolution, countermobilization — that diffuses across groups and sites, then exhausts or institutionalizes (Tarrow, *Power in Movement*).

A wave, operationally: law, money, or tech changes who can exit an old “you must” → a slogan names it → a medium (pulpit, print, TV, For You) purifies it → a counterwave sells restoration.

`Need(old institution) ≈ 1 / (new wage or law × exit option × frame × medium)`  *(heuristic, not a law of nature)*

Companion machines (do not mash): Perez techno-economic paradigms (`docs/study/TECHNO_ECONOMIC_WAVES.md`); cohort/age-structure (`docs/study/DEMOGRAPHIC_WAVES.md`); hegemonic cycles (`docs/study/HEGEMONIC_CYCLES.md`); medium/attention (`docs/study/MEDIUM_ATTENTION_WAVES.md`). Those ask what got cheap / how many bodies / who holds the table / what clips. This file asks **who revolts, with what orgs, under what opportunity, with what frame, with what receipt**.

Date every data pull `YYYY-MM-DD`. Do not renumber 19th-century crests because TikTok moved. Advocacy ≠ census. Wikipedia is a finding aid. Some table URLs in this map still land on Wikipedia, Wikiwand, or a recap — locator, not the load-bearing source. Swap to NARA/BLS/Oyez/Church Committee/census before a dissertation cites the number. A dissertation is **one numbered crest + one historiographic fight + one historical file + one dataset**. Mass splitter ≠ feed noise: electorates and comment sections are not the same animal.

**History first, tables second.** A cycle is a thing that happened in time — archives, statutes-as-events, monographs, oral history. Stats measure scale and the *live* splitter (prices, votes, density). Pew without Foner is a dashboard. Foner without BLS is a sermon. You need both. You are not tripping.

## 0. Theory and unit of analysis

**Mechanism stack:** political opportunity/threat (McAdam, Meyer) → mobilizing structures (McCarthy & Zald) → framing (Snow & Benford; Entman) → repertoires (Tilly) → diffusion/scale shift (Tarrow; della Porta) → countermovement (Meyer & Staggenborg) → outcome (law, collapse, co-optation, schism, or a feed).

**Unit of analysis for every numbered crest below** (Lasswell 1948 rebuilt for *acts*, not speeches — the grammar of a claim-making row, not the name of this file):

1. Who — role (sovereign, firm, union, church, party, PMC, cartel), not vibes
2. Does what — the act (evict, sanction, smear, shoot, tax, bless, certify)
3. To whom — target class / body / group
4. Under what authority — cover: flag / invoice / statute / procedure / docket · medium: pulpit / paper / TV / For You · threat: fear / gun / chaos · cloak: privacy / classification / NDA
5. Date — as-of + provisional vs final
6. With what effect — and who gets dinner after
7. Receipt — **history** (archive / primary / monograph) **and** **table** (scale / live splitter). Statute and docket count as history when they are the event.
8. Falsifier — which cell dies first if the row is wrong (usually a historiographic fight, not a poll)
9. Grain — event ≠ campaign ≠ cycle
10. Resources / org — roster + budget (an SMO, not a role-noun)

If 1–5 will not fill, the crest is a story. If 1–5 fill and 7–8 will not, it is a **clean story**.

## 1. Black freedom (US-weighted; rhymes elsewhere)

### 1.1 Abolition → emancipation (~1830s–1865)

1. **Who:** sovereign (Union) + parties (Liberty/Free Soil/Republican) + churches / free-Black societies. Not pamphlets alone.
2. **Does what:** certifies the end of chattel as property; shoots to enforce.
3. **To whom:** enslaved persons; slaveholding class.
4. **Authority:** cover = statute/flag (13th); medium = pulpit/paper; threat = gun/war.
5. **Date:** 1865 13th = final *on paper*. Provisional until armies occupy.
6. **Effect / dinner:** legal personhood of the formerly enslaved; planter capital haircut; Republican coalition eats Reconstruction offices.
7. **Receipt** (tables dated 2026-11-14 pull):
   - *History:* 13th Amendment https://www.archives.gov/milestone-documents/13th-amendment · Emancipation Proclamation https://www.archives.gov/milestone-documents/emancipation-proclamation · Du Bois, *Black Reconstruction* https://archive.org/details/blackreconstruc00dubo · James, *The Black Jacobins* · Davis, *The Problem of Slavery in the Age of Revolution* · LOC African American Odyssey https://www.loc.gov/exhibits/african-american-odyssey/
   - *Table — scale of the enslaved population at the moment of emancipation:* 1860 US census, enslaved population **3,953,760** (the free-population columns in the same census do not isolate free Black persons as a single national total in the table pulled here — flagged open, needs IPUMS/Census Bureau historical-statistics cross-tab, not invented). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1860_United_States_census · raw returns at NARA/IPUMS https://www.ipums.org/
   - *Table — the war-as-mechanism number, USCT enlistment:* **~179,000** Black men enlisted in the Union Army (United States Colored Troops + other units), of whom **nearly 37,000 died**; by war's end Black soldiers were about **10% of the Union Army**. https://www.battlefields.org/learn/topics/united-states-colored-troops (year-by-year enlistment breakdown not available on this source — flagged open; National Archives USCT compiled service records would give monthly musters if needed for a dissertation-grade table)
   - *Table — the economic-haircut number, compensated-emancipation estimates:* capital value of ~4 million enslaved people in 1860 was roughly **$3 billion** (Ransom & Sutch); Goldin's 1973 compensated-emancipation estimate put the cost of the federal government buying all US slaves in 1860 at about **$2.7 billion**; Gunderson's 1974 estimate ranges **$2.5–3.7 billion**, "the same order of magnitude as the war's costs." Ransom & Sutch (1990) note that spreading that payment over 25 years would have roughly tripled annual federal outlays of the era. https://eh.net/encyclopedia/the-economics-of-the-civil-war/
8. **Falsifier:**
   - The row's own test ("if emancipation were mostly moral suasion, the 13th and the war cell die") now has a number attached on each side: **~179,000 enlisted Black soldiers and ~37,000 dead** is the enforcement-mechanism cell; **3,953,760 enslaved as of the 1860 census** is the stock the mechanism had to move. A moral-suasion-alone account cannot explain why the stock only moved to zero after USCT enlistment scaled to 10% of the Union Army and after Union armies physically occupied Confederate territory — enlistment and occupation, not pamphlets, are what is doing the work in the timeline. This is consistent with the original row's claim; the new material is that the claim is now falsifiable against a specific enlistment count and a specific war-cost/compensated-emancipation cost comparison (the $2.5–3.7B compensated-emancipation estimate vs. actual Civil War cost is the number a committee would ask you to cite instead of asserting "the war was cheaper/costlier than paying for it" from memory).
   - **Open/unverified, flagged rather than invented:** a single authoritative year-by-year USCT enlistment curve (to test enlistment against the political-opportunity timeline sentence-by-sentence) was not found in this pass; NARA's compiled military service records or Dyer's *Compendium* would be the primary source to pull next.
   - Haitian Revolution (1791–1804) remains the prior Atlantic file, unchanged.
9. **Grain:** cycle (decades), war as terminal campaign. Not a tweet.
10. **Org:** Black churches, mutual aid, abolition societies, Union Army (via USCT, ~179,000 enlisted) as terminal SMO.

Fight: war as mechanism vs Garrison. Canon: Du Bois, *Black Reconstruction* https://archive.org/details/blackreconstruc00dubo · James, *The Black Jacobins*.

### 1.2 Reconstruction → nadir / Jim Crow (~1865–1950s) — counterwave

1. **Who:** Southern Democratic parties, Klan/White League/Red Shirts, courts.
2. **Does what:** evict from vote and land; shoot; certify caste (*Plessy*).
3. **To whom:** Black citizens (14th/15th on paper).
4. **Authority:** cover = statute/docket/"home rule"; threat = terror; cloak = local procedure.
5. **Date:** *Plessy* 1896 finalizes legal caste; nadir through mid-20th c.
6. **Effect / dinner:** Redemption governments, cheap labor, White primary.
7. **Receipt** (tables dated 2026-11-14 pull):
   - *History:* 14th Amendment https://www.archives.gov/milestone-documents/14th-amendment · 15th Amendment https://www.archives.gov/milestone-documents/15th-amendment · *Plessy v. Ferguson* https://www.oyez.org/cases/1850-1900/163us537 · Foner, *Reconstruction* · Woodward, *Strange Career of Jim Crow* · Wells, *Southern Horrors* · NAACP papers https://www.loc.gov/collections/manuscripts-from-the-naacp/
   - *Table — Black officeholding during Reconstruction (the number that directly tests Dunning):* a documented-records count of **at least 1,510–1,517 Black officeholders** across the former Confederacy during Reconstruction, by state: South Carolina 316, Mississippi 226, Louisiana 210, North Carolina 187, Alabama 173, Georgia 135, Florida 58, Arkansas 46, Texas 49, Virginia 85, Tennessee 20, DC 11, Missouri 1 — with historians estimating the true total closer to **2,000** once undocumented local offices are counted. https://freedomriders.facinghistory.org/sites/default/files/2023-10/Black_Officeholders_in_the_South.pdf
   - *Table — federal level:* **2 US Senators (Hiram Revels, Blanche K. Bruce) and 20 US House members** served in the Reconstruction-era Congress (1870–1901); the last of that cohort, George H. White, left Congress at the end of the 56th Congress (~March 1901) — no Black member sat in Congress again until 1929. CRS *African American Members of the U.S. Congress: 1870–2020* https://www.everycrsreport.com/reports/RL30378.html
   - *Table — dating the nadir precisely, Tuskegee Institute lynching counts by decade (Black victims):* 1882–89: 534 · 1890–99: 1,111 (decade peak; single-year peak **1892 with 230 total lynchings, 161 Black**) · 1900–09: 791 · 1910–19: 569 · 1920–29: 281 · 1930–39: 119 · 1940–49: 31 · 1950–59: 6 · 1960–68: 3. Totals as stated by Tuskegee: 3,445 Black victims, 1,297 white victims, 4,743 overall (1882–1968). https://archive.tuskegee.edu/repository/wp-content/uploads/2020/11/Lynchings-Stats-Year-Dates-Causes.pdf · EJI lynching project https://lynchinginamerica.eji.org/
8. **Falsifier:**
   - **Dunning School ("Reconstruction = corruption/Black incapacity for self-government") dies directly against the officeholding table.** ~1,500–2,000 documented Black officeholders, including 2 US Senators and 20 US Representatives within three decades of emancipation, is incompatible with a narrative built on the premise that Black Americans were incapable of, or marginal to, Reconstruction governance. The Dunning frame survives only as "a restoration plot" (per the original row), not as a historiographic fact.
   - **The nadir is datable, not vibes-based, against the lynching decade series.** The data forces a *correction* to a loose "nadir = mid-20th c." framing: the **single highest-violence decade is the 1890s (1,111 Black victims, peak year 1892 at 230 total)**, i.e., the immediate post-Redemption/pre-*Plessy* years, not the 1930s–50s. By the 1930s the annual count (119/decade) is already down an order of magnitude from the 1890s peak, and by the 1940s–50s it is down two orders of magnitude (31, then 6). "Nadir" as a *term* (coined later by historians, notably Rayford Logan) correctly spans roughly 1877–1920s politically and socially, but the *lynching-violence* peak specifically is the 1890s, which is the cell a committee would flag if the row implied a flat or late-peaking terror curve.
   - This is a correction to field 5/6 forced by field 7 data, flagged per the task instructions rather than silently harmonized: the original row's "nadir through mid-20th c." should read "legal/political nadir through mid-20th c.; lynching-violence peak specifically in the 1890s, declining sharply after."
9. **Grain:** counter-cycle, not one riot.
10. **Org:** Redeemer parties, paramilitaries, courts; Black churches + early NAACP (1909) as surviving structures.

### 1.3 Long civil rights (~1954–1968)

1. **Who:** churches, NAACP chapters, SCLC, SNCC, CORE; federal sovereign as absorber.
2. **Does what:** certify public accommodations, vote, formal equality; occupy streets and dockets.
3. **To whom:** Jim Crow state + private accommodations.
4. **Authority:** cover = docket/statute (*Brown*, CRA, VRA); medium = pulpit/TV; threat = state violence against sit-ins.
5. **Date:** *Brown* 1954; CRA 1964; VRA 1965; Fair Housing 1968 — statutes final; implementation provisional.
6. **Effect / dinner:** Black vote as a file; King-law vs post-1965 street split inside the coalition.
7. **Receipt** (tables dated 2026-11-14 pull):
   - *History:* *Brown v. Board* https://www.oyez.org/cases/1940-1955/347us483 · Civil Rights Act of 1964 https://www.archives.gov/milestone-documents/civil-rights-act · Voting Rights Act of 1965 https://www.archives.gov/milestone-documents/voting-rights-act · King, Letter from Birmingham Jail https://www.africa.upenn.edu/Articles_Gen/Letter_Birmingham.html · McAdam, *Political Process and the Development of Black Insurgency* https://press.uchicago.edu/ucp/books/book/chicago/P/bo5939918.html · Payne, *I've Got the Light of Freedom* · Morris, *Origins of the Civil Rights Movement* · SNCC Digital Gateway https://snccdigital.org/
   - *Table — the VRA test, Black voter registration % before (March 1965) vs. after (Sept 1967), US Commission on Civil Rights data by state:* Mississippi **6.7% → 59.8%** (largest swing; lowest pre-Act registration of any covered state) · Alabama 19.3% → 51.6% · Georgia 27.4% → 52.6% · Louisiana 31.6% → 58.9% · South Carolina 37.3% → 51.2% · North Carolina 46.8% → 51.3% · Virginia 38.3% → 55.6% · Arkansas 40.4% → 62.8% · Texas 53.1% → 61.6% · Florida 51.2% → 63.6% · Tennessee 69.5% → 71.7%. https://www.epi.org/chart/freedom-to-vote-figure-a-black-voter-registration-in-selected-southern-states-before-and-after-the-voting-rights-act-of-1965/ — cross-checked against Mississippi-specific history: fewer than 6% of eligible Black men registered in Mississippi by 1892 after the 1890 constitution's literacy test/poll tax, versus **>79,000 registered (≈97% of eligible Black men)** briefly in 1867 under federal Reconstruction oversight — i.e., the pre-1965 6.7% floor is itself a *restoration* of 1890s disenfranchisement, not a historical baseline. https://www.themarshallproject.org/2024/04/04/mississippi-voting-rights-history-discrimination
   - *Table — the "law alone did it" test, school desegregation compliance after Brown:* as of **fall 1964 — a full decade after Brown (1954)** — only about **2.25% of Black children in the South attended school with any white students**, and "in two-thirds of the region's biracial school districts, no blacks had been admitted to white schools" (Cascio & Washington, NBER working paper, using HEW/USCCR compliance data). Desegregation rose sharply only *after* the Civil Rights Act of 1964 (Title VI funding leverage), HEW's March 1968 guidelines, and *Green v. New Kent County* (1968); by 1966 "nearly all southern school districts had engaged in at least nominal desegregation," with Deep South district averages around 14% Black-student integration vs. ~47% in the rest of the South by 1967 — still a decade after *Brown* itself. https://conference.nber.org/confer/2005/si2005/dae/cascio.pdf
8. **Falsifier — tested directly against the numbers:**
   - **"Law alone did it" dies cleanly on the school-desegregation table.** *Brown* (1954) produced essentially no change in actual integration for a full decade — 2.25% in 1964 — until it was backed by a second lever (Title VI funding cutoff under the 1964 CRA) and federal enforcement machinery (1968 HEW guidelines, *Green*). This is exactly McAdam's point operationalized: the doctrinal "cover" (the *Brown* holding) needed political opportunity/enforcement capacity before it moved anything; the holding itself was not sufficient, and the Payne/Morris "local people, not only King/not only courts" cell survives.
   - **The VRA registration jump is the clean counter-case that something other than "law alone" can move fast once paired with direct federal registrars**, since Mississippi's 6.7%→59.8% jump (1965–67) happened in roughly two years, far faster than the decade-long school-desegregation lag — the difference is that the VRA deployed federal registrars directly into the counties (Section 4/5 coverage), while *Brown* relied on local district compliance with no comparable federal enforcement mechanism until 1964–68. That contrast — not "law matters" vs. "law doesn't matter" as a binary — is the falsifiable, gradeable claim the data supports.
   - *Shelby County v. Holder* (2013) remains the later legal counterwave against the VRA's preclearance mechanism, unchanged from the original row. https://www.oyez.org/cases/2012/12-96
9. **Grain:** campaign cluster inside a century cycle.
10. **Org:** Black churches, Black colleges, NAACP. Opportunity: WWII, Great Migration, Northern vote, Cold War embarrassment.

### 1.4 Black Power / nationalist (~late 1960s–70s)

1. **Who:** SNCC after 1966, Panthers, NOI; federal counter-intel as the other actor.
2. **Does what:** certify dignity without permission; survival programs; state smears/shoots (COINTELPRO).
3. **To whom:** police as immediate file; "colonies at home."
4. **Authority:** cover = party program; threat = gun both ways; cloak = FBI classification.
5. **Date:** late 1960s–70s; COINTELPRO 1967–71 as dated file (Black Nationalist-Hate Groups program specifically).
6. **Effect / dinner:** pride as program; org destruction; imprisonment pipeline starts as file.
7. **Receipt** (tables dated 2026-11-14 pull):
   - *History:* FBI Vault COINTELPRO https://vault.fbi.gov/cointel-pro · Internet Archive FBI-COINTELPRO-BLACK files https://archive.org/details/FBI-COINTELPRO-BLACK · Church Committee final report (Senate Select Committee, 1976) https://www.intelligence.senate.gov/resources/intelligence-related-commissions · Bloom & Martin, *Black against Empire* · Joseph, *Waiting 'Til the Midnight Hour* · Carmichael & Hamilton, *Black Power* (primary frame)
   - *Table — the state-destroyed-it number:* the FBI's "Black Nationalist-Hate Groups" COINTELPRO program ran **1967–1971**; by 1969, **233 of 295 authorized counterintelligence actions** under that program were directed specifically at the Black Panther Party and its allies — i.e., the Panthers alone absorbed about **79% of all authorized actions** in a program nominally covering the whole "Black Nationalist" category (SNCC, SCLC, NOI, Black Student Unions, etc.). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Panther_Party (Church Committee attribution; a direct Church Committee volume citation — e.g. Book III, "COINTELPRO — Black Panther Party" — would strengthen this for dissertation grade: https://www.aarclibrary.org/publib/church/reports/book3/pdf/ChurchB3_3_BlackPanthers.pdf)
   - *Table — the internal-factionalism number:* **peak Panther membership ≈5,000 (variously dated 1969/1970), with 38 chapters/branches by the end of 1968** across at least 21 cities. Decline: hundreds of members quit in **1971** amid the Newton–Cleaver split; the party closed dozens of chapters in **early 1972**, consolidating remaining members in Oakland; membership had fallen to **27 by 1980**. Deaths directly tied to police/FBI action (Bobby Hutton 1968; Fred Hampton and Mark Clark, Dec. 4, 1969 Chicago raid) are a separate, smaller count from deaths tied to internal Newton-Cleaver violence (at least 4 members) and the Jan. 1969 UCLA shootout with the rival US Organization (2 members killed) — these are mechanistically distinct kill-counts that should not be merged into one "the state killed the party" number. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Panther_Party
8. **Falsifier — "state destroyed it" vs. "internal factionalism destroyed it," run against the numbers:**
   - **Both cells have real data and neither is the whole story — this is the falsifying result, not a clean win for either claim.** The *external* cell is strong and dated: 233/295 (≈79%) of authorized Black-Nationalist COINTELPRO actions targeting the Panthers specifically, plus direct police/FBI killings (Hutton 1968; Hampton/Clark 1969) and mass prosecutions (Panther 21, Bobby Seale, Huey Newton's ~3-year imprisonment before reversal) is more than sufficient to falsify a "the Panthers simply burned out" story that omits state action.
   - **But the chapter-closure and membership-collapse timing (1971 mass resignations amid the Newton-Cleaver split; 1972 chapter closures; decline to 27 members by 1980) is also real and postdates COINTELPRO's formal 1971 termination** (the program was officially ended in 1971 after press exposure from the 1971 Media, PA FBI office break-in). A clean "COINTELPRO alone destroyed the party" claim has to explain why the steepest membership collapse (1971-72 resignations and chapter closures) coincides with the internal Newton-Cleaver rupture and comes right at/after COINTELPRO's formal end, not during its most intensive 1969 phase.
   - **Net falsifier:** the row survives as "COINTELPRO materially crippled the Panthers through 1969–71 (killings, mass incarceration, informants fueling paranoia) and that damage interacted with and accelerated a genuine internal factional split that then finished the organizational collapse in 1971–72" — it dies as either a pure external-destruction story or a pure internal-collapse story told in isolation from the other.

2. **Does what:** smash unions; certify markets as morality; tax-cut / privatize.

### 6.6 Sub-state separatism — Scottish independence referendum, 2014

1. **Who:** Scottish National Party (SNP, governing party of the Scottish Government under First Minister Alex Salmond) as the claimant; UK Government (sovereign, under PM David Cameron) as the certifying/granting authority; Better Together (unionist cross-party campaign — Labour, Conservative, Liberal Democrat) as the organized counter.
2. **Does what:** certifies a legally-binding, UK-sanctioned referendum on Scottish independence (not a unilateral declaration — the critical procedural difference from the Catalan case below); votes; the UK Parliament supplies the legal mechanism via an Order in Council under the Scotland Act 1998, s.30.
3. **To whom:** the Scottish electorate as decision-maker; the Union (rest of UK) as the entity whose territorial integrity is at stake.
4. **Authority:** cover = bilateral intergovernmental agreement, the **Edinburgh Agreement**, signed 15 October 2012 by Cameron and Salmond, which supplied the statute (Scottish Independence Referendum Act 2013, passed by the Scottish Parliament) and the UK-side Order in Council making the vote legally binding and its result recognized by Westminster regardless of outcome; medium = TV debates (Salmond-Darling debates) and print; threat = none required (contrast with Catalonia, where the Spanish state's threat/cloak cell is active).
5. **Date:** Edinburgh Agreement 15 Oct 2012; referendum held **18 September 2014**; result final and accepted by both governments the same night/next morning — no subsequent legal challenge to validity, unlike Catalonia.
6. **Effect / dinner:** No wins — Scotland remains in the UK. Exact result: 2,001,926 votes (55.3%) for No, 1,617,989 votes (44.7%) for Yes, on a turnout of 84.6% of the registered electorate — among the highest turnouts for any UK-wide or devolved vote in the democratic era. Cameron promises and subsequently delivers further devolution ("the Smith Commission," additional powers over tax and welfare, 2016 Scotland Act) rather than independence; SNP's bargaining position and seat count at Westminster surge afterward (2015 general election), so the party gets institutional "dinner" even in defeat.
7. **Receipt:**
   - *History* — Edinburgh Agreement text (UK Government/Scottish Government joint publication, 2012); Scottish Independence Referendum Act 2013 (legislation.gov.uk); Electoral Management Board for Scotland official results declaration (emb.scot); Centre on Constitutional Change retrospective, "What happened in September 2014?" (centreonconstitutionalchange.ac.uk).
   - *Table* — What Scotland Thinks / ScotCen survey series (whatscotlandthinks.org) on "most important reason to vote No/Yes"; cross-check: John Curtice's analysis finds economic evaluations played a central role in the 2014 result — 98% of voters who thought Scotland would be worse off under independence voted No, and 97% of those who thought it would be better off voted Yes; YouGov's final pre-vote poll found 47% thought Scotland would be worse off against 35% who thought it would be better off, and 45% thought the economy would be damaged against 37% who thought it would benefit.
8. **Falsifier:** the economics-vs-identity mechanism test is a real, run falsifier, not a slogan — the survey data shows that how economic perceptions aligned with votes "undoubtedly" gave the No side a decisive advantage, since the electorate who believed independence would hurt the economy broke overwhelmingly for No, which is evidence for the economic-grievance/economic-risk-aversion mechanism as dominant over a pure national-identity mechanism in 2014 — but this evidence shows *alignment* between economic belief and vote, not proof of *motivational primacy*; the data cannot distinguish "voters decided based on economics" from "voters rationalized a prior identity-based choice in economic terms," so a strict falsifier test would require panel data tracking belief-formation sequence, which the cross-sectional YouGov/Opinium polls here do not supply — note this limit rather than overclaiming the mechanism.
9. **Grain:** campaign (one bounded, legally-binding vote) inside the longer Scottish-devolution cycle (1997 devolution referendum → 1999 Scottish Parliament → 2014 independence referendum → ongoing SNP-pushed demand for a second referendum, "IndyRef2," not yet granted as of this pull). Do not conflate with the 2016 Brexit vote, which reopened the Scottish question as a *different* campaign with a different legal mechanism.
10. **Org:** Scottish National Party (SNP) — governing party, Holyrood seat-and-budget roster, led the Yes Scotland umbrella campaign; Better Together — the unionist cross-party campaign org (Labour/Conservative/Lib Dem joint structure, led by Alistair Darling), now defunct as a single org post-referendum; UK Government (Cabinet Office/Scotland Office) as the sovereign counterparty supplying the Edinburgh Agreement's legal mechanism.

### 6.7 United Kingdom — Brexit referendum (23 June 2016) as populist-sovereignty crest

1. **Who:** sovereign electorate exercising a one-off statutory referendum (not binding in strict constitutional-law terms, but politically treated as binding) vs. the Vote Leave/Leave.EU campaign coalition (UKIP, Conservative Eurosceptic wing, Nigel Farage) against the cross-party Remain campaign and the incumbent Cameron government.
2. **Does what:** electorate votes to leave the European Union; government subsequently invokes the EU exit mechanism; Parliament passes enabling legislation; UK formally exits.
3. **To whom:** the EU-27, UK Parliament's own sovereignty settlement, EU migrants resident in the UK, and UK nationals' free-movement rights — all re-negotiated by the result.
4. **Authority:** cover = European Union Referendum Act 2015 (statute authorizing the vote) → Article 50 TEU notification (international-treaty mechanism) → European Union (Withdrawal) Act 2018 and European Union (Withdrawal Agreement) Act 2020 (domestic statutes implementing exit); medium = tabloid press and a heavily contested official "£350m a week to the NHS" bus-slogan claim; threat = none (lawful vote), but "Project Fear" framing on both sides; cloak = official pre-vote Treasury/Bank of England economic forecasts later disputed by both camps.
5. **Date:** referendum held and result declared **23–24 June 2016**; Article 50 notice formally given by PM Theresa May's letter, **29 March 2017**; after extensions, UK formally left the EU **31 January 2020**, entering a transition period that ended **31 December 2020**. Final, not provisional, as a legal matter since January 2020, though the economic-effects debate remains live and is itself part of the falsifier below.
6. **Effect / dinner:** Leave won 17,410,742 votes (51.89%) to Remain's 16,141,241 (48.11%), out of 33,577,342 total votes cast, with turnout at 72.21% of 46,500,001 registered voters — a real but narrow (3.8-point) margin, not a landslide, which matters for any claim of an overwhelming "mandate." 7. **Receipt:** *History* — UK Electoral Commission official referendum results (the canonical primary source; cross-checked against en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_United_Kingdom_European_Union_membership_referendum for the vote totals cited above); House of Commons Library briefing CBP-7639 on the referendum; Theresa May's Article 50 notification letter (29 March 2017) and the European Union (Withdrawal) Act 2018 text on legislation.gov.uk. *Table* — British Election Study panel data (the dedicated academic vote-choice dataset for this case, whatukthinks.org/eu and the BES panel itself); ONS migration and regional-GVA series used in the economic-grievance literature.
8. **Falsifier:** the economic-grievance-vs-immigration/identity fight is explicitly unresolved in the leading academic treatment itself: one explanation holds Leave was driven mainly by economic "left-behinds" in struggling jobs or wage conditions, especially in the North of England, the Midlands, and Wales, while the competing explanation holds Leave reflected long-standing British suspicion of the EU, disgust with the Westminster political class, anxiety about the refugee crisis and migration from other EU countries, and opposition to austerity, with the author's own view being that the two may be complementary rather than rival — economic deprivation may have fueled resentment about immigrants and opposition to open borders, based on British Election Study panel surveys examining what separated Leave and Remain voters in 2016 and what predicted UKIP support from 2015 to 2017, with models controlling for age, sex, economic grievances, authoritarian/populist values, and attitudes toward the EU, immigration, and left-right ideology. The real falsifier: if, within the BES panel, local economic decline (wage/employment trend by constituency) predicts Leave vote independent of immigration attitudes and authoritarian-values scales, the pure-identity/sovereignty account dies; if immigration attitude and authoritarian-values scales predict Leave independent of local economic decline (which multiple BES-based studies, e.g. Fieldhouse & Prosser's BES-based work in the *British Journal of Political Science* 2020, generally find to be the stronger and more independent predictor), the pure-economic "left-behind" account dies first.
9. **Grain:** event (23 June 2016 referendum, one day) nested in campaign (2015 Referendum Act through the 2016 vote) nested in a longer cycle (UK-EU contention dating to the 1975 EEC referendum, running through 2016 and resolving — provisionally — on 31 January 2020, with ongoing contention over Northern Ireland Protocol/Windsor Framework terms as a live remainder).
10. **Org:** Vote Leave (official designated Leave campaign, led by Dominic Cummings/Matthew Elliott, with disclosed Electoral Commission spending returns) and the separate Leave.EU/UKIP track (Farage, Arron Banks funding — later subject to Electoral Commission fines for funding irregularities); Britain Stronger in Europe as the official Remain campaign — all with Electoral Commission-filed campaign-spending returns, a real disclosed-budget roster rather than a role-noun.

### 8.1 High imperialism + protonationalism (19th c.–WWI)

1. **Who:** the **East India Company** (chartered firm, Court of Directors, London — not "the West") as the extracting actor through 1858; the **Crown's India Office** (a specific ministry) after the Government of India Act 1858; separately, the **French Ministère des Colonies** and its federal administrations (AOF/AEF) in West/Equatorial Africa. Indigenous elite collaborators (zamindars collecting Bengal land revenue) are a distinct, named layer inside the extraction chain, not folded into "the West."
2. **Does what:** fixes and collects land revenue (Permanent Settlement, 1793); runs a trade monopoly, later a tariff regime that suppresses competing Indian manufacture; exports bullion into India in the 18th century, then reverses into a sustained trade/fiscal surplus extracted *out* by the 19th; funds its own garrison army off the same revenue it taxes.
3. **To whom:** Bengal peasant cultivators and zamindars (revenue base); Indian textile/silk producers (tariff target); in French Africa, villages subject to direct tax and (after 1912) forced-labor levies.
4. **Authority:** cover = Company Charter (renewed 1813, 1833, 1853) then Crown statute (1858); medium = company ledger/gazette; threat = the Company's/Crown's own sepoy army, whose upkeep the Charter Acts required Indian tax revenue to fund — i.e., the colonized population's own taxes financed the force used to hold the colony.
5. **Date:** Permanent Settlement 1793 — final as fiscal settlement (land revenue fixed at **≈£3 million** at 1789–90 prices, ~20% above pre-1757 demand); Company rule ends 1858 (final, Crown takeover after the 1857 rebellion); "high imperialism/protonationalism" conventionally 1870s–1914, with the Indian National Congress founded 1885 as the dated org-event. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_India_under_Company_rule
6. **Effect / dinner:** Company/Crown bondholders and officials eat dividends and pensions funded by Indian taxation; British manufacturers eat a captive market (British textile imports into India rose from **£5.2 million in 1850 to £18.4 million in 1896**); opium became **~40% of India's exports** by the second quarter of the 19th c.; zamindars who collected revenue ate a cut too — the dinner table has more than one "Western" seat and at least one Indian one.

### 8.3 Independence wave (~1945–1975)

1. **Who:** named nationalist parties/fronts — Convention People's Party (CPP, Ghana, Nkrumah); Front de Libération Nationale (FLN, Algeria); Rassemblement Démocratique Africain (RDA, multi-territory French West African federation) — as the claim-making actor; metropolitan governments (UK, France, Belgium, Portugal) as the granting/resisting sovereign; the UN General Assembly as a certifying body via Resolution 1514 (1960).
2. **Does what:** certifies sovereignty transfer (flag ceremony, UN seat) through negotiation (Ghana, most of French West Africa after de Gaulle's 1958 referendum) or armed force (Algeria 1954–62, Kenya's Mau Mau).
3. **To whom:** colonial subject populations as beneficiaries; settler populations (French *pieds-noirs* in Algeria) as a counter-target.
4. **Authority:** cover = UN Charter Ch. XI/XII + UN GA Resolution 1514, 14 Dec 1960 ("Declaration on the Granting of Independence to Colonial Countries and Peoples"); threat = gun where negotiation failed (Algeria, Kenya); medium = UN debate chamber as a new venue for the claim.
5. **Date:** Ghana, **6 March 1957** — first sub-Saharan African independence, the dated opening event; Resolution 1514, **14 Dec 1960**; trailing edge **1975** (Portuguese Africa) with Zimbabwe (1980) and Namibia (1990, closing the §8.2 Class-C-mandate loop) as codas.
6. **Effect / dinner:** new national political classes inherit the colonial state apparatus and army (see §8.4's falsifier); metropoles retain economic ties in many cases (CFA franc zone for ex-French Africa) — "formal sovereignty, continued informal economic dependence" is the dinner split Fanon's falsifier below names.

### 8.6 Latin America — Argentina's Dirty War and the human rights movement (1976–present)

1. **Who:** military junta (sovereign, 1976–83: Videla, Viola, Galtieri, Bignone as successive heads) running a state-terror apparatus (clandestine detention centers, death squads under Battalion 601 and Navy intelligence); Madres de Plaza de Mayo (mobilizing structure, mothers of the disappeared) and Abuelas de Plaza de Mayo (grandmothers, searching for stolen grandchildren) as the countermovement; CONADEP (Comisión Nacional sobre la Desaparición de Personas, created by civilian President Raúl Alfonsín, 1983) as the state's own truth-commission certifier; federal courts (Juicio a las Juntas, 1985) as the judicial sanctioning body.
2. **Does what:** disappears, tortures, and kills political opponents and suspected leftists without trial or acknowledgment ("Dirty War," Guerra Sucia); later, Madres occupy public space (weekly silent march around the Plaza de Mayo) to certify the disappeared as real, named, and owed an accounting; CONADEP certifies a documented casualty count; the federal courts sanction junta commanders criminally.
3. **To whom:** leftist militants, students, union organizers, journalists, and a wide net of suspected sympathizers (the junta's targets); later, the juntas themselves become the target of Madres' claim-making and the courts' prosecution.
4. **Authority:** cover = military-coup decree/national-security doctrine (junta seized power 24 March 1976, citing a prior leftist-insurgency threat as flag); threat = gun/disappearance/torture (clandestine detention centers — ESMA/Navy School of Mechanics the best-documented); cloak = total secrecy/denial (the regime denied the disappearances existed at all — this is the defining cloak of the period); later cover flips to docket/statute once CONADEP and the trials begin (Alfonsín's decree creating CONADEP, Dec 1983; Argentina's existing Criminal Code used at Juicio a las Juntas rather than any special tribunal).
5. **Date:** coup 24 March 1976; dictatorship 1976–1983 (final as a periodization — ends with return to civilian rule, Alfonsín inaugurated 10 Dec 1983); CONADEP report *Nunca Más* published 1984 — the casualty count in it is **final as a documented-case count, explicitly provisional/incomplete as a true total** (CONADEP itself said the real number was likely higher); Juicio a las Juntas opened 22 April 1985, verdict **9 December 1985** — final as a first-instance judicial determination (later overturned by pardons, then reopened — see falsifier).
6. **Effect / dinner:** junta commanders convicted get imprisoned (Videla and Massera received life sentences in Dec 1985); the democratic government gets a legitimating truth-commission document; the amnesty laws that followed (Full Stop Law 1986, Due Obedience Law 1987, and Menem's 1989–90 pardons) let most lower-ranking officers walk — until those laws were themselves annulled by the Argentine Supreme Court in 2005, reopening prosecutions that have continued into the 2020s; Madres de Plaza de Mayo, founded by a small group of mothers in **April 1977** and still marching weekly at the Plaza de Mayo as of recent reporting, remain an active organization rather than a closed historical episode.
7. **Receipt:**
   - *History* — CONADEP, *Nunca Más* (1984; official report, Spanish original at the Argentine government's archive and in English translation via Yale/Latin American collections); Juicio a las Juntas trial record (International Crimes Database case file, internationalcrimesdatabase.org/Case/1118); Madres de Plaza de Mayo organizational history (founding April 1977, documented at latinolife.co.uk and the organization's own continuing public marches, reported into the 2020s as "40/45 years on"); the **discrepancy fight**: CONADEP's *Nunca Más* registered 8,961 persons as forcibly disappeared, based on documented, individually-verified cases submitted to the commission; human rights organizations (led by the Madres/Abuelas-aligned movement) usually place the true figure at 30,000, a number that functions as a political-memory consensus figure rather than a case-by-case tally — the gap exists because CONADEP's number is a floor built from documentable testimony and surviving paper trail under a regime that deliberately destroyed records and denied its own camps existed, while the 30,000 figure extrapolates from survivor testimony about undocumented victims, missing case files, and the destruction of evidence. CONADEP itself stated the actual number could be higher than its own count, which is why the discrepancy is a known, named methodological fight (documented-floor vs. extrapolated-consensus) rather than a simple factual dispute.
   - *Table* — CONADEP's own case registry (digitized, Argentine government archive); later-era cross-checks via Argentina's Secretaría de Derechos Humanos ongoing trial-tracking data and the Centro de Estudios Legales y Sociales (CELS) prosecution database, which track how many of the ~30,000-scale claims have corresponding judicial case files as the post-2005 trials have proceeded.

The Full Stop/Due Obedience amnesty framework is a separate, later falsifier test: if impunity had been the true final state, the 2005 Supreme Court annulment and the subsequent (2006–present) wave of reopened trials would not have happened — they did, which falsifies "impunity closed the case" as the cycle's endpoint.
9. **Grain:** the junta period (1976–83) and Juicio a las Juntas (1985) are a bounded campaign; the Madres' continuous weekly marches since 1977 and the post-2005 reopened-trials wave extend this into an ongoing cycle that has not closed, unlike (for contrast) the 1954–68 US civil-rights campaign-grain crest in §1.3. Do not collapse "the Dirty War" and "the ongoing human-rights trials" into one dated event.
10. **Org:** military junta chain of command (Ejército/Armada/Fuerza Aérea joint command, Battalion 601 military intelligence, ESMA as the Navy's detention-center apparatus) as the perpetrating org with an actual budget line inside the state; Madres de Plaza de Mayo (founded April 1977 by a core group including Azucena Villaflor, roster of mothers of the disappeared, now an incorporated association with its own university — Universidad Popular de Madres de Plaza de Mayo — and public funding/donation structure) and Abuelas de Plaza de Mayo (separate org, founded 1977, focused on identifying stolen grandchildren via DNA — Banco Nacional de Datos Genéticos) as the two distinct, still-operating SMOs; CONADEP (1983–84, a time-limited state commission, not a standing org) and CELS (Centro de Estudios Legales y Sociales, founded 1979, still the leading legal-prosecution NGO) as the roster+budget orgs carrying the post-1983 legal fight.

## 9. Agrarian, land, caste

Who eats, who holds title. Weak on Western For You. Huge on body count.

### 9.1 Peasant / land reform

1. **Who:** Bolivia's **MNR** (Movimiento Nacionalista Revolucionario) revolutionary government under Víctor Paz Estenssoro, installed by the April 1952 insurrection; **sindicatos campesinos** (armed peasant syndicates, Cochabamba-centered) that in many documented cases began **seizing haciendas before** the government's own decree — a sequencing fact that is itself the falsifier test below; hacienda-owning landlords as the dispossessed class.
2. **Does what:** certifies redistribution by decree — the Agrarian Reform Law (Decreto Ley 3464, **2 August 1953**) — abolishing *pongueaje* (unpaid personal labor service) and granting peasant title, in many documented localities *after* peasant syndicates had already occupied the land rather than *before*.
3. **To whom:** indigenous and mestizo peasant cultivators (*colonos*) as beneficiaries; hacienda landlords as the target class.
4. **Authority:** cover = decree-law from a government that had itself just seized power by insurrection a year earlier; threat = armed peasant militias, organized partly with weapons surplus from the 1952 revolution (channeled via the miners'-union network); compensation = 25-year government bonds valued at the landlord's *own prior tax self-declaration* — a deliberately punitive mechanism, since landlords had chronically underdeclared value to minimize taxes.
5. **Date:** decree **2 August 1953** — final as statute; implementation provisional and uneven for decades: by **1970**, only **45% of peasant families** had received *formal* title — i.e., the paperwork ran roughly 17 years behind the fact of land occupation in many areas. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land_reform_in_Bolivia
6. **Effect / dinner:** peasant cultivators gain land access and the end of personal-service obligations; hacienda owners lose a near-monopoly — cultivable land in estates of **1,000 hectares or more** had stood at **92% before 1952**; the MNR government, in turn, gets a mobilized peasant base that becomes a durable corporatist pillar of Bolivian politics for decades.
7. **Receipt:**
   - *History* — Dunkerley, *Rebellion in the Veins*; Klein, *Bolivia: The Evolution of a Multi-Ethnic Society* and "Social Change in Bolivia Since 1952"; Moore, *Social Origins of Dictatorship and Democracy* · Wolf, *Peasant Wars* · Scott, *Weapons of the Weak* https://yalebooks.yale.edu/book/9780300036411/weapons-of-the-weak/ and *The Moral Economy of the Peasant* · Paige, *Agrarian Revolution*.
   - *Table, dated 2026-11-14 pull:* the two hard pre/post numbers available in this pass — **92% of cultivable land held in 1,000+ hectare estates before 1952**; **only 45% of peasant families formally titled by 1970**. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land_reform_in_Bolivia
   - **Open, flagged not invented:** a land-Gini coefficient series for Bolivia across 1950→1970s was **not found** in this pass (the one Gini series located, for China 1996–2013, postdates and is geographically irrelevant to this case — see falsifier note below). FAO https://www.fao.org/ and the World Inequality Database on land (WID) are the next direct pulls for a quantified concentration index rather than the percentage/title figures used here.
8. **Falsifier — Scott (moral economy) vs. Popkin (*The Rational Peasant*), tested against the actual sequencing:** Bolivia is a real test precisely because the *sindicatos* seized land **before** the 1953 decree guaranteed anyone individual title or payoff — i.e., peasants acted under collective, subsistence-insurance logic ahead of any state-guaranteed individual return, which favors Scott's moral-economy reading. A Popkin-consistent version of the same case would require peasants to have *waited* for the decree's title guarantee before acting individually; the documented chronology (law ratifying a fact already on the ground, not leading it) contradicts that version. This is a real, named case-level test, not a restatement of the theoretical dispute.
9. **Grain:** cycle at the national level (the hacienda system dismantled over roughly 20 years, 1952–1970s titling) containing one sharp campaign-grain event (the 1952–53 seizure/decree moment) — do not collapse "the Bolivian revolution" and "the land reform's completion" into one date.
10. **Org:** *sindicatos campesinos* (peasant syndicates, Cochabamba-centered, armed via 1952-revolution weapons surplus) as the actual mobilizing structure; MNR as the party-state; **COB** (Central Obrera Boliviana, the miners'-led labor confederation) as the allied urban SMO that supplied arms and an organizing template to the rural syndicates — named rosters, not "the peasantry" as a bloc.

### 9.2 Caste (India — different legal file)

1. **Who:** the **Second Backward Classes Commission** (the "Mandal Commission," chaired by B.P. Mandal, reporting 1980) as the certifying body; **V.P. Singh's National Front government** (announced implementation August 1990) as the executing sovereign; the **Supreme Court of India**, nine-judge bench, in *Indra Sawhney v. Union of India* as the adjudicating body (judgment 16 Nov 1992).
2. **Does what:** certifies a **numeric quota**, not an anti-discrimination statute on the US model — Mandal recommended **27% reservation** in central-government jobs (later extended to education) for Other Backward Classes (OBCs), stacked on top of the Constitution's pre-existing **22.5% combined reservation for Scheduled Castes/Scheduled Tribes** — i.e., a system that names categories and assigns fixed numeric shares by law, the exact structure the falsifier says a CRA-1964 analogy misses.
3. **To whom:** OBC applicants for central-government jobs/education as beneficiaries; "general category" (upper-caste) applicants facing a reduced open-competition share; SC/ST as a separate, older reservation category dating to the Constitution itself (1950), not created by Mandal.
4. **Authority:** cover = Constitution of India Arts. 15(4)/16(4) (reservation-enabling provisions) https://www.constituteproject.org/ + V.P. Singh's August 1990 executive implementation order + the Supreme Court's 1992 *Indra Sawhney* judgment; medium = gazette notification; threat = none directly from the state, but the 1990 announcement triggered nationwide anti-Mandal riots and self-immolations by upper-caste students — i.e., here the countermovement's street violence followed the *state's own* quota decision, the reverse of the usual "movement wins a statute" sequence.
5. **Date:** Mandal Commission report **1980** (provisional — shelved for a decade); V.P. Singh implementation announcement **August 1990** (final as a policy act, immediately litigated); Supreme Court *Indra Sawhney* judgment **16 November 1992** — final as controlling doctrine: upholds the 27% OBC quota, caps **total reservations (SC+ST+OBC) at 50%**, and introduces the **"creamy layer"** exclusion (wealthier OBC households excluded from the quota).
6. **Effect / dinner:** OBC households gain an enforceable numeric share of central-government jobs and (later) education seats; the **50% cap** becomes the binding constitutional ceiling every subsequent caste-quota fight has had to argue around (including 2020s Maratha-quota litigation); the "creamy layer" rule makes the quota caste-coded but income-tested at the margin — a mechanism with no Title VII/CRA-1964 equivalent.
7. **Receipt:**
   - *History* — Ambedkar, *Annihilation of Caste* (primary) · Constitution of India https://www.constituteproject.org/ · Jaffrelot, *India's Silent Revolution* · *Indra Sawhney and Others v. Union of India*, Supreme Court, 16 Nov 1992 https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indra_Sawhney_and_Others_v._Union_of_India.
   - *Table, dated 2026-11-14 pull — real socioeconomic-gap figures grounding the "legal file" claim:* from a 2019–2021 India DHS/NFHS-window sample (n=273,304, via PMC 2026-11-14, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11449120): **low-wealth (poverty) rate 22.9% among upper-caste adults vs. 47.4% among Scheduled Caste adults**; **higher-education attainment 23.2% (upper caste) vs. 10.8% (SC)**; Scheduled Caste membership carries an **odds ratio of ~2.26 (unadjusted)** for being in the low-wealth category. Secondary figures cited within the same source, from Thorat & Madheswaran (survey years not independently re-verified in this pass — flagged as secondary): monthly per-capita consumption **₹2,413 (higher caste) vs. ₹1,294 (SC)**; poverty rates **~9% (higher caste), ~20% (OBC), ~30% (SC)**; wealth ownership (2013) **~45% held by higher castes vs. ~7% by SCs**, though SCs are **~18% of the population**; wage-labor dependence (2012) **~44% (SC), ~26% (OBC), ~11% (higher caste)**; of the SC-vs-higher-caste wage gap, **~71.5% is attributed to measurable endowments** (education, capital) **and ~28.5% to caste discrimination** net of those endowments — the closest Indian-data analogue to a US "residual discrimination" coefficient.
   - **Open:** the Thorat & Madheswaran figures above are a secondary citation inside the PMC article, not independently re-pulled from NSS/NFHS-5 fact sheets in this pass — flag for direct verification against NFHS-5 district fact sheets or NSS consumption-expenditure rounds before treating them as load-bearing.
8. **Falsifier — "US race clone — the legal file is reservations + Constitution, not CRA 1964," tested against the instrument itself:** CRA 1964/Title VII is an anti-discrimination *statute* with no numeric quota attached. The Indian file is structurally the inverse: a numeric quota (27% OBC, 22.5% SC/ST, 50% total cap) written into policy and bounded by courts, with a wholly separate income-based "creamy layer" test layered on top of the caste category. A row that reaches for Title VII disparate-treatment case law as the analogy for Indian caste policy is wrong about which legal instrument is doing the work; the correct comparison class is numeric-quota/reservation jurisprudence (closer to the logic of *Bakke*/*Grutter*-style US quota litigation than to Title VII), which is exactly the distinction the original falsifier names and which the 27%/22.5%/50% figures above make concrete rather than asserted.
9. **Grain:** policy-campaign (1980 report → 1990 implementation → 1992 judicial settlement — 12 years) sitting inside a much longer cycle (reservations as continuous policy since the 1950 Constitution, 75+ years running, with the 50% cap and creamy-layer boundary still actively litigated into the 2020s via Maratha-quota cases).
10. **Org:** the Backward Classes Commission (Mandal Commission, time-limited body, 1979–80); V.P. Singh's National Front coalition government (1989–90, the executing political org); the Supreme Court's nine-judge constitutional bench (1992, the adjudicating body) — none of these is a social-movement org in the Tilly/McAdam sense. The closest SMO-type actors are OBC caste associations (pushing for implementation) and, on the counter side, the 1990 upper-caste anti-Mandal student agitation groups — the latter were short-lived and not a standing roster+budget org. **Open:** unlike Madres de Plaza de Mayo in §8.6, this crest's Org field is thin on roster/budget detail for the countermovement side — a named-organization pull (e.g., specific anti-Mandal student-union bodies, or specific OBC caste-association federations such as any Mandal-era SC/ST/OBC employee associations) is the next step for a future depth pass.

## 10. Fascism / springs

### 10.1 Fascism as a movement (not "people I hate") — NSDAP electoral trajectory vs. SA paramilitary escalation

1. **Who:** the NSDAP as party-organization (Hitler, Reichstag caucus) **plus** its violence organization, the SA (Sturmabteilung, under Ernst Röhm from 1931) — two linked but distinct rosters, not one "fascism" blob; then, from January 1933, the captured state apparatus.
2. **Does what:** contests Reichstag elections while simultaneously running street violence (meeting disruption, brawls with KPD/SPD paramilitaries, beatings) through the SA; after January 1933 certifies the palingenetic-nationalist myth via statute (Reichstag Fire Decree, Enabling Act) and absorbs/purges its own paramilitary wing once it becomes a liability (Night of the Long Knives, June–July 1934).
3. **To whom:** rival parties' organizers and voters (SPD, KPD, Centre), then — after the seizure of power — "marked internals" (Jews, leftists, the SA's own leadership under Röhm).
4. **Authority:** cover = party list on the Reichstag ballot, then Reichstag Fire Decree (Feb 1933) and Enabling Act (March 1933) as statute; medium = paper/radio plus brown-shirted street presence as a medium in its own right; threat = SA fists/pistols in the Weimar street-violence economy that also included the KPD's Rotfrontkämpferbund and assorted Freikorps remnants.
5. **Date:** interwar, 1928–1934, with the electoral climb 1928–1932 and the paramilitary climb 1930–1932 as two series that must be dated separately, not fused into one "rise of Hitler" line.
6. **Effect / dinner:** regime capture in January–March 1933 — but capture came via an **elite deal** (Hindenburg appointing Hitler chancellor on 30 January 1933, after NSDAP's vote share had already *fallen* from its mid-1932 peak), not via a rising vote share crossing a majority threshold on its own. Old NSDAP elites (Hugenberg, Papen) got cabinet seats first; the SA's own leadership got purged fifteen months later once the party no longer needed a street army it couldn't fully control.
7. **Receipt:**
   - *History* — Paxton, *The Anatomy of Fascism* (stages-as-process framework being tested here) https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/128540/the-anatomy-of-fascism-by-robert-o-paxton/ · Kershaw, *Hitler* · USHMM Holocaust Encyclopedia, "The SA" https://encyclopedia.ushmm.org/content/en/article/the-sa

8. **Falsifier — does the vote come before or after the paramilitary violence escalated, run against the actual numbers:**
   - **The SA's steepest growth phase (60,000 in 1930 → 221,000 by November 1931, a 3.7× increase) runs concurrently with and slightly ahead of the NSDAP's steepest electoral growth phase (18.3% in Sept 1930 → 37.3% in July 1932).** Paramilitary mobilization scaled up in parallel with, not strictly after, the vote share climb — this is consistent with Paxton's stage order (mobilizing a militant base and normalizing violence as a precondition for, not a consequence of, mass electoral breakthrough), and it falsifies a naive "they won votes, then got violent" sequencing.
   - **The cell that actually dies is the inverse naive story too — "violence straightforwardly bought more votes."** Between the July 1932 peak (37.3%) and November 1932 (33.1%), the NSDAP's vote share *fell* even as the SA kept growing (toward 445,000 by August 1932, continuing to grow into 1933). Electoral support **plateaued and reversed before the seizure of power**; what changed the outcome was not a renewed vote surge but Hindenburg's elite appointment decision in January 1933, with the March 1933 "landslide" (43.9%) occurring only *after* the Reichstag Fire Decree had already suspended core civil liberties and banned/intimidated the KPD press — i.e., the final vote number is downstream of the power seizure, not its electoral cause.
   - **Net falsifier result:** the row survives as "paramilitary scale-up and electoral growth rose together through mid-1932, then the regime was delivered by an elite bargain after electoral momentum had already stalled" — it dies as either "votes caused the violence" or "violence alone (without the elite deal) delivered the regime." This is exactly Paxton's point against a vibe-checklist reading of fascism: the hinge is elite legitimation at a moment of *electoral plateau*, not a monotonic mass-mandate curve. ### 10.2a South Korea — Gwangju Uprising (18–27 May 1980)

1. **Who:** sovereign/junta (Chun Doo-hwan's martial-law command, 7th and 11th Airborne Brigades, "New Military" faction) vs. mobilizing structures — Chonnam National University + Chosun University student councils, Gwangju taxi/bus drivers, the Catholic Priests' Association for Justice (Gwangju diocese clergy), and the opposition-party network around Kim Dae-jung (New Democratic Party).
2. **Does what:** martial law troops beat/bayonet/shoot demonstrators (18–21 May); citizens seize armories and hold the city as a self-governed "Absolute Community" (21–26 May); army retakes the Provincial Office by force (27 May, "Operation Sangmu Chungjeong"); regime certifies the episode a Communist-backed "riot."
3. **To whom:** Gwangju's student and civilian population; Kim Dae-jung, charged as the alleged instigator and sentenced to death for sedition (commuted in 1981 after US pressure and an appeal reportedly carried to Pope John Paul II).
4. **Authority:** cover = Emergency Martial Law Decree No. 10, extended nationwide 17 May 1980, later National Security Law-style sedition charges; medium = state broadcasters (KBS/MBC) blackout vs. underground leaflets and loudspeaker trucks; threat = M16s, bayonets, armor; cloak = official "North Korea-instigated riot" classification that stood until 1988 hearings.
5. **Date:** event 18–27 May 1980 (provisional death count at the time: the junta's own figure). Re-designated "democratization movement" by the 1988 National Assembly special hearings; archives inscribed to UNESCO Memory of the World in 2011. The constitutional *exit* crest is downstream: National Assembly ratifies direct-election constitutional amendment 12 October 1987, approved by referendum 27 October 1987 (≈93% yes), direct presidential election held 16 December 1987.
6. **Effect / dinner:** the South Korean government claimed 165 people were killed, while current scholarship estimates 600 to 2,300 victims — the stated discrepancy is the crest's own evidentiary fault line, not a rounding error. Chun's junta ate seven more years of executive power (1980–88); Kim Dae-jung's death sentence became the opposition movement's living martyr case and he was elected president in 1997. The 1987 election itself: Roh Tae-woo (Democratic Justice) won 8,282,738 votes, 36.64%; Kim Young-sam (Reunification Democratic) 6,337,581, 28.04%; Kim Dae-jung (Peace Democratic) 6,113,375, 27.05%; Kim Jong-pil (New Democratic Republican) 1,823,067, 8.07%, with turnout at 89.15% — Roh won on a split opposition vote, not a landslide mandate, which is itself a data point against "the movement swept the old order away in one election."
7. **Receipt:** *History* — May 18 Memorial Foundation / UNESCO Memory of the World archive inscription (2011); Chun regime's own casualty count vs. post-1988 National Assembly hearing testimony and subsequent scholarly tallies, per the South Korean government's claim of 165 killed against current scholarly estimates of 600 to 2,300 victims (Gwangju Uprising overview, wikiwand.com/en/Gwangju_Uprising); 1987 referendum/election record (museum.nec.go.kr/eng/eexh/eexh_1987.html; en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1987_South_Korean_presidential_election). *Table* — V-Dem liberal-democracy index for South Korea (sharp 1987–88 discontinuity, v-dem.net); National Election Commission vote returns above; ACLED/UCDP do not reach back to 1980 — this crest is pre-dataset and runs on archive + NEC returns, not a cross-national panel.
8. **Falsifier:** "students did it alone" dies first if (a) the taxi/bus-driver strike and convoy actions of 20 May (drivers' organizational mobilization, not a spontaneous crowd), (b) the Gwangju Catholic Priests' Association for Justice's documentation and shelter role, and (c) Kim Dae-jung's New Democratic Party network's prior organizing in the Jeolla region all show up as coordinated inputs rather than post-hoc narrative glue. 9. **Grain:** event (Gwangju, 18–27 May 1980, ten days) nested in campaign (Korean student/dissident opposition, 1979 Yushin collapse through 1987) nested in cycle (authoritarian-to-democratic regime transition, Third through Sixth Republics).

### 10.2b Philippines — People Power Revolution (Feb 1986)

1. **Who:** sovereign incumbent (Ferdinand Marcos, martial-law-era KBL regime) vs. a triple coalition — military defectors (Defense Minister Juan Ponce Enrile + AFP Vice Chief of Staff Fidel Ramos and the Reform the Armed Forces Movement), the Catholic Church hierarchy (Cardinal Jaime Sin, Radio Veritas), and the Aquino opposition (Corazon Aquino, post-snap-election civil-disobedience call).
2. **Does what:** regime certifies a fraudulent snap-election win; opposition calls civil disobedience; military faction defects and barricades itself in Camp Crame; civilians physically block loyalist tanks/Marines; Marcos flees; Aquino is sworn in and issues a provisional constitution.
3. **To whom:** the Philippine electorate; AFP rank-and-file whose loyalty was contested; Marcos's own inauguration audience (which shrank to loyalists).
4. **Authority:** cover = COMELEC's own (disputed) proclamation of Marcos as winner of the 7 February 1986 snap election vs. Aquino's claim of a stolen mandate; medium = Radio Veritas and Church pulpits coordinating the crowd; threat = tanks, Marine units, armed loyalist troops; cloak = US ambiguity (Reagan called fraud reports "disturbing" but said fraud occurred "on both sides") until Washington's position flipped.
5. **Date:** snap election 7 February 1986; COMELEC/Batasang Pambansa proclaim Marcos winner 15 February; Enrile/Ramos defect and seize Camp Crame 22 February; EDSA crowds peak 23–24 February; Aquino inaugurated and Marcos flees to Clark Air Base **25 February 1986**, arriving Hawaii 26 February. Provisional at the moment of flight; final once Ramos's troops secure Malacañang shortly after midnight 26 February.
6. **Effect / dinner:** the infobox lists "2,000,000+ protestors," and the lead says the demonstrations involved "over two million Filipino civilians", with the February 16 Luneta rally drawing about two million people and EDSA crowds growing from "hundreds of thousands" to "over a million" by midday on February 24, with some sources putting the figure at 2 million — i.e., the crowd-size numbers themselves run from "a million-plus" to "2 million" depending on which day/source is cited, which is the live splitter, not a single clean figure. 7. **Receipt:** *History* — Wikipedia "EDSA revolution"/People Power Revolution chronology (en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EDSA_revolution), cross-checked against contemporaneous AP/Reuters wire timelines; Chenoweth & Stephan, *Why Civil Resistance Works* (cup.columbia.edu/book/why-civil-resistance-works/9780231156837) treats EDSA 1986 as a canonical nonviolent-transition case. *Table* — NAVCO dataset (navcodata.org / dataverse.harvard.edu/dataverse/navco) codes EDSA as a successful nonviolent campaign; V-Dem Philippines liberal-democracy index shows a discontinuity at 1986.

### 10.2d Poland — Solidarity (1980–89)

1. **Who:** sovereign (Polish United Workers' Party / PZPR, with Soviet backing) vs. mobilizing structures — the Gdańsk Shipyard strike committee (Lech Wałęsa, Anna Walentynowicz), the Catholic Church hierarchy (reinforced by Pope John Paul II's 1979 homeland visit), and the independent trade-union federation Solidarity itself once legalized.
2. **Does what:** regime fires a shipyard worker (Walentynowicz) → triggers strike → regime signs a legalization agreement under pressure → regime later bans the union by martial-law decree → regime negotiates it back into legal existence at a Round Table.
3. **To whom:** Polish industrial and (later) broader workforce; by 1989, the entire PZPR one-party monopoly itself, which the Round Table dismantled.
4. **Authority:** cover = the Gdańsk (August) Agreement, a negotiated quasi-statute signed by the state; then Martial Law Decree of 13 December 1981 banning the union outright; then the 1989 Round Table Agreement restoring legal pluralism; medium = underground press (bibuła), Radio Free Europe, church pulpits; threat = ZOMO riot police, internment (thousands detained under martial law), gun (nine miners killed at the Wujek Colliery, December 1981); cloak = state-of-war ("stan wojenny") legal cover for the crackdown.
5. **Date:** the Gdańsk Agreement was signed 31 August 1980, when the Communist government agreed to let Solidarity exist; martial law was imposed 13 December 1981 under General Wojciech Jaruzelski; the Round Table Talks ran 6 February to 5 April 1989, leading to the semi-free elections of 4 June 1989 — final transition date, provisional until the 1990 fully free presidential election (Wałęsa elected president December 1990).
6. **Effect / dinner:** Solidarity's peak membership was about 10 million, reached in September 1981 — roughly one-third of the country's working-age population, making it the largest independent social movement organization in the Soviet bloc's history at the time. The 1981 crackdown dissolved that membership base by decree; the 1989 Round Table restored it as a negotiating party and produced the 4 June 1989 elections that broke the PZPR's monopoly, triggering the broader 1989 Eastern Bloc cascade. PZPR elites who negotiated the Round Table largely avoided prosecution (the "amnesty" dinner), while Solidarity activists inherited state offices and, later, privatized state assets.
7. **Receipt:** *History* — Gdańsk Agreement text and Round Table Agreement record (IPN — Institute of National Remembrance, eng.ipn.gov.pl); Garton Ash, *The Magic Lantern*; the Solidarity overview at en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solidarity_(Polish_trade_union), sourced membership figure and dates as cited above. *Table* — V-Dem Poland liberal-democracy index (discontinuity at 1989); Polish GUS economic series on the 1979–80 contraction and ~$18 billion foreign debt load cited as the economic-grievance backdrop.
8. **Falsifier:** the economic-grievance-vs-national/Catholic-identity fight is explicit in the sourcing itself: food prices rose while wages stagnated through the 1970s, the economy contracted in 1979 for the first time since World War II, and foreign debt reached about $18 billion by 1980 — a materialist account anchored in the Gdańsk Shipyard firing of Walentynowicz. Against that, the page credits Polish nationalism, pro-American liberalism, and ties to the Catholic Church as central, citing Wałęsa's own view that Pope John Paul II's 1979 visit was a significant factor, and Leszek Kołakowski's underground philosophical influence. The real falsifier: if Solidarity's 10-million membership surge of 1980–81 is shown (via regional strike data) to track most tightly with price-shock/wage-stagnation localities rather than with dioceses most affected by the Papal visit, the identity-driven account dies first; if the reverse correlation holds, the pure-economic account dies first. Historiographically this is the Ost (*Solidarity and the Politics of Anti-Politics*, materialist/labor reading) vs. Osa/Kubik (Catholic-nationalist framing and symbolic-politics reading) fight.
9. **Grain:** event (August 1980 Gdańsk strike) nested in campaign (1980–81 legal Solidarity period, crushed by martial law) nested in cycle (the full 1980–89 contention arc ending in regime collapse) — do not collapse the 1980 strike and the 1989 Round Table into one undifferentiated "Solidarity" event; they are nine years and a sixteen-month ban apart.
10. **Org:** Independent Self-Governing Trade Union "Solidarity" (NSZZ Solidarność) — the ~10 million peak-membership roster cited above, led by Wałęsa's national commission structure, headquartered in Gdańsk; underground structures (after 1981) sustained by church-linked funding and Western labor-movement support (notably AFL-CIO channels) — a genuinely budgeted, rostered SMO, not a role-noun.

### 11.1 Disability — ADA (1990) passage context vs. actual post-ADA employment-rate data

1. **Who:** disability-rights SMOs (ADAPT — Americans Disabled for Accessible Public Transit — as the direct-action wing) + a cross-disability legislative coalition, against a Congress and White House (George H. W. Bush) that had already passed the bill in one chamber and stalled in the other.
2. **Does what:** direct-action organizations stage a physical demonstration to force a stalled bill off the House floor; Congress certifies broad civil-rights coverage reaching private employers (not just federal grantees, unlike the 1973 Rehabilitation Act's Section 504); President signs.
3. **To whom:** people with disabilities as a protected class; private employers, transit systems, and public accommodations as newly regulated targets.
4. **Authority:** cover = statute (ADA, Pub. L. 101-336); medium = direct action + press coverage of the demonstration itself as a medium; the bill had already cleared the Senate in September 1989 but stalled in the House through spring 1990.
5. **Date:** the **Capitol Crawl**, 12–13 March 1990 — ADAPT activists marched from the White House to the Capitol, some leaving wheelchairs/crutches to crawl up the Capitol steps (12 March), followed by a Rotunda sit-in and arrests (13 March); both chambers passed the ADA in July 1990 (Senate 91–6, House 377–28); **President Bush signed the ADA into law 26 July 1990**, on the White House South Lawn.
6. **Effect / dinner:** the statute passed with overwhelming bipartisan margins and created the accommodation/access litigation infrastructure that follows — but the *labor-market* effect is the crest's own falsifier target (see §8 below), and it did not arrive on the same timeline as the statute itself.
7. **Receipt** (tables dated 2026-11-14 pull):
   - *History:* Scotch, *From Good Will to Civil Rights* · Shapiro, *No Pity* · ADA text https://www.ada.gov/ · Capitol Crawl chronology, Boundary Stones/WETA, "The Capitol Crawl Demonstration Helped Pass the ADA" https://boundarystones.weta.org/2021/09/16/capitol-crawl
   - *Table, dated 2026-11-14 pull:*
     - **Early-1990s counter-evidence against a naive "the statute fixed employment" story:** economist Thomas DeLeire's analysis finds employment for people with disabilities **fell, and fell relative to people without disabilities, between 1990 and 1995** — i.e., the employment gap *widened*, not narrowed, in the years immediately following the ADA's passage (cited in Mercatus Center policy brief, "Boosting Employment for People with Disabilities: Reforms Beyond the ADA," https://www.mercatus.org/media/176676/download).
     - **2023, working-age employment-to-population ratio:** people with disabilities **37.1%** employed vs. people without disabilities **75.0%** — a gap of **37.9 percentage points**, still very large 33 years after passage. Same Mercatus brief.
     - **2014–2023 narrowing:** the employment-to-population ratio for people with disabilities **rose by more than 11 percentage points** over this decade — the gains are real but arrived roughly a quarter-century after the statute, concentrated in the post–Great Recession tight-labor-market years, not as an immediate ADA effect. Same source.
     - **Generational cross-check, July 2024 pull (Kessler Foundation/University of New Hampshire NTIDE):** among people under 35 (the "ADA generation," i.e., born after the law existed), employment-to-population ratio for those *with* disabilities was **45%** vs. **68.4%** for the same age band *without* disabilities (gap 23.4 pts); for the 35-and-over "non-ADA generation," **34.6%** (with disability) vs. **79.9%** (without) — gap **45.3 pts**. The ADA-generation gap is meaningfully smaller than the non-ADA-generation gap, which is the one piece of evidence that does favor a long-run statutory effect, filtered through cohort rather than calendar time. https://kesslerfoundation.org/press-release/ntide-july-2024-ada-generation-drives-employment-gains-people-disabilities
8. **Falsifier — "charity-as-the-whole-cycle" tested against the numbers:**
   - **"Charity-as-the-whole-cycle" (i.e., nothing but moral suasion/goodwill ever happened; no enforceable legal change occurred) dies immediately** against the statute itself — the ADA created justiciable accommodation and access claims that did not exist before 1990, and the bill's own passage mechanics (a 1,000-person direct-action march forcing a stalled bill off the floor, then 91–6 and 377–28 votes) is a textbook mobilizing-structures-to-legislation case, not charity.
   - **But the opposite clean story — "the statute closed the employment gap" — also dies**, against DeLeire's 1990–1995 finding that the gap *widened* immediately post-passage, and against the still-37.9-point gap in 2023. The honest result: the ADA secured *access and accommodation rights* quickly, but did **not** close the *employment-rate* gap on the statute's own timeline; the real employment convergence (11+ points, 2014–2023) shows up three decades later and tracks a cohort effect (people who grew up inside ADA-covered schooling/hiring systems) more than it tracks a calendar-year "statute passed, employment responded" relationship.
   - OPEN: a precise 1990 (pre-ADA baseline) national disability employment-to-population ratio, directly comparable to the 2023 figures above using the same definition/age band, was not found in this pull — BLS's modern disability labor-force series (LNU02374597 and related CPS tables, https://www.bls.gov/webapps/legacy/cpsatab6.htm) only becomes consistently comparable from 2008 onward (when BLS added a standardized disability question to the CPS); the ACS/CPS 1990–1995 figures DeLeire uses come from a different survey instrument, so the "did the gap close since 1990" question is methodologically harder to answer with one clean series than the compressed bullet implied. Treat this as an open measurement-continuity problem, not a settled number.
9. **Grain:** statute-passage event (July 1990) nested in a legislative campaign (1988–1990 House stall + Capitol Crawl) nested in a decades-long labor-market convergence cycle (1990s widening → 2010s–2020s narrowing) that is still running.
10. **Org:** ADAPT (direct-action SMO, Denver chapter prominent in the Crawl) + a broader cross-disability coalition lobby; no 990-style budget figures for ADAPT's 1990 operations were located in this pull (OPEN).

### 11.3 Drugs — opioid/fentanyl overdose curve vs. cannabis curve, plotted separately as the row demands

1. **Who:** illicit-drug supply networks (increasingly synthetic-opioid-dominated since ~2013) + prescribers/pharma (the 1990s–2000s prescription-opioid phase) vs. state cannabis-legalization regimes (a separate statutory track) vs. CDC/SAMHSA as the measuring apparatus.
2. **Does what:** supply shifts from prescription opioids → heroin → illicitly manufactured fentanyl (IMF) across roughly 2010–2023; separately, states certify legal cannabis markets (statute), an entirely different drug/legal-status track that the row warns against conflating with the opioid curve.
3. **To whom:** opioid-curve deaths are overwhelmingly tied to IMF/polysubstance use; cannabis-curve "deaths" are, per the data below, essentially a polysubstance-detection artifact, not an independent cannabis-caused mortality curve.
4. **Authority:** cover = CDC/NCHS mortality coding (ICD-10 drug-poisoning codes) for the overdose series; cover = state legalization statutes for the cannabis track — two different authority/cover mechanisms, reinforcing why they are "two files."
5. **Date, as-of 2026-11-14 pull:** total US overdose deaths by year: **2020: 92,500 · 2021: 107,500 · 2022: 112,582 (peak) · 2023: 110,037 · 2024: ~75,000 (provisional/tentative estimate, range 70,000–80,000)**. Synthetic opioids (fentanyl) specifically: **87,155 deaths in 2023 (78% of all US overdose deaths that year)**; fentanyl deaths fell to **72,776 in 2023** (1.4% fewer than 2022) and continued falling, from **79,815** (Sept 2022–Aug 2023) to **57,997** (Sept 2023–Aug 2024) on a rolling 12-month basis. Source: USAFacts, "Are fentanyl overdose deaths rising in the US?" https://usafacts.org/articles/are-fentanyl-overdose-deaths-rising-in-the-us/ · Progressive Policy Institute, "U.S. Drug Overdose Deaths Down 21.7% From 2023 to 2024" https://www.progressivepolicy.org/u-s-drug-overdose-deaths-down-21-7-from-2023-to-2024/
6. **Effect / dinner:** the decline from the 2022 peak (112,582) is "almost entirely" concentrated in synthetic-opioid deaths, per PPI's read of CDC provisional data — i.e., the opioid curve is doing essentially all of the recent movement, in both directions, independent of anything happening in cannabis policy.
7. **Receipt — cannabis curve, separated out as the row demands:**
   - *History:* Musto, *The American Disease* · Courtwright, *Forces of Habit* · MacCoun & Reuter, *Drug War Heresies*.
   - *Table, dated 2026-11-14 pull — CDC MMWR, "Notes from the Field: Cannabis Detection Among Overdose Deaths — United States, 2021–2025" (https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/volumes/75/wr/mm7534a2.htm):* of **209,166 total overdose deaths** January 2021–June 2025, cannabis was **detected** in **43,880 (21.0%)** — but cannabis was **listed as a cause of death in only 0.8%** of overdose deaths, and was the **sole drug involved in just 9 deaths out of 209,166**. Among the cannabis-detected deaths, **73.5% also involved illegally manufactured fentanyl** and **60.4% also involved stimulants** — the CDC authors themselves conclude cannabis detection is "primarily indicative of polysubstance use," not an independent overdose driver.
8. **Falsifier — "cannabis mashed with fentanyl are two files," tested with the separated curves:**
   - **The two curves are not just different in scale, they are different in causal mechanism**, which is exactly what the row's falsifier demands be shown, not asserted. The opioid/fentanyl curve is a massive, largely independent mortality series (92,500 → 112,582 → ~75,000, 2020–2024) driven by illicit synthetic-opioid supply shocks, with its own rise-peak-decline shape unrelated to any state's cannabis statute. The cannabis curve, by contrast, shows **essentially no standalone lethality** (9 of 209,166 deaths were cannabis-only) and its "21% detection" headline number — the figure a sloppy version of this row would cite as if it meant 21% of overdose deaths are cannabis-caused — collapses on inspection into a polysubstance co-detection artifact (73.5% also fentanyl, 60.4% also stimulants).
   - **Net result: the falsifier holds exactly as stated.** A row that tried to read rising cannabis-detection percentages as evidence of a parallel "cannabis overdose epidemic" tracking the fentanyl epidemic would be wrong and is falsified by the CDC's own cause-of-death vs. detection distinction; a row that treats them as two separate files — one real, large, supply-driven mortality curve (opioids) and one near-zero standalone-lethality co-detection statistic (cannabis) — survives.
   - OPEN: this pull did not separately verify whether overdose-death trends differ between cannabis-legal and cannabis-illegal *states* (a cross-sectional test distinct from the national polysubstance-detection finding above); some academic literature (JAMA Health Forum, "Cannabis Legalization Associated with Teen OD Deaths") finds a youth-specific association worth checking directly rather than citing secondhand — flagged open rather than resolved here.
9. **Grain:** supply-shock event (fentanyl's 2013 arrival in the illicit market) nested in an overdose-mortality cycle (2013–2022 rise, 2022–2024 decline) that is entirely distinct from the cannabis-legalization statutory cycle (2012–present, state-by-state) — the row's "two files" instruction is a grain instruction as much as a causal one.
10. **Org:** no single SMO; this is a supply-chain/public-health-surveillance crest (DEA scheduling + CDC/NCHS mortality coding + state legislatures on the cannabis side) rather than a rostered movement organization.

### 11.4 CJ / police / prison — BJS imprisonment rate paired against FBI homicide rate, 1990–2024

1. **Who:** the carceral state (DOJ/BJS as the measuring apparatus; state DOCs and county jails as the executing apparatus) vs. abolition/decarceration advocacy (Sentencing Project, Prison Policy Initiative) vs. "order"/tough-on-crime advocacy — overlaps §1.5–1.6's Black-imprisonment-rate cell but is being tested here at the aggregate national level, paired against homicide as the row explicitly instructs.

3. **To whom:** the incarcerated population (peaking above 2.3 million people in 2008) and, on the other side of the pairing, homicide victims — two different "whom"s that a sloppy version of this row would conflate into one undifferentiated "crime and punishment" story.

## 13. Sex-differentiated child governance

Who controls a minor female body/mobility before majority, and who contests that control — three crests: statute-vs-custom over *marriage age*, statute-vs-custom over *genital cutting*, and regime-vs-street over *adult dress/mobility* enforced downward onto girls too. ### 13.1 Child marriage (minimum-age statute vs custom/religious practice-holders)

1. **Who:** Girls Not Brides (UK-registered charity, "The Global Partnership to End Child Marriage," charity no. 1154230) + UNICEF + national legislatures setting statutory minimum marriage age, vs. customary-law/religious-authority practice-holders (family elders, qadis/clerics administering parallel marriage registers) who treat betrothal as a parental/kinship transaction outside civil registration.
2. **Does what:** certifies/raises a legal floor on marriageable age (statute), vs. solemnizes/registers marriage below that floor through customary or religious channels that sit outside — or openly defy — the civil registry.
3. **To whom:** girls under 18 (and under 15 in the "very young" sub-metric); counter-target is the registering authority (imam, chief, customary court) whose jurisdiction the statute tries to pre-empt.
4. **Authority:** cover = statute/civil code (state) vs. custom/personal-status law/religious procedure (practice-holder); medium = UN treaty language (CEDAW, CRC) and DHS/MICS survey instruments vs. village/mosque register; threat = none directly, but loss of custody/inheritance/registration access functions as the enforcement lever on the statute side; cloak = under-registration (births and marriages both go unrecorded, which is the falsifier mechanism below).
5. **Date:** as-of 2026-11-14. Global stock estimate **640 million girls and women alive today were married in childhood**; an estimated **12 million child marriages occurred in 2022** alone — provisional annual flow, UNICEF modeled estimate, not a census count.
6. **Effect / dinner:** formally, girls retain legal minority (school eligibility, inheritance claims) longer; informally, families that need the transaction (dowry/bride-price timing, honor/pregnancy management) route around the statute via customary or unregistered religious ceremony, and local religious/customary authorities keep the registration fee and the social authority to arrange matches.
7. **Receipt:**
   - *History* — Girls Not Brides global partnership and its advocacy history https://www.girlsnotbrides.org/ · UNICEF global data portal on child marriage https://data.unicef.org/topic/child-protection/child-marriage/ · statute examples: Honduras eliminated all exceptions to its 18-minimum in 2017 (theirworld.org/Plan International reporting, July 2017) https://theirworld.org/?p=11579 ; Malawi's parliament amended its Marriage, Divorce and Family Relations Act to set 18 with no exceptions, February 2017 https://www.unwomen.org/en/news/stories/2017/2/news-malawi-parliament-adopts-amendment-to-end-child-marriage ; Bangladesh moved the opposite direction in February 2017, passing a law that **retained a judicial/parental-consent exception allowing marriage under 18 in "special circumstances,"** the countermove case https://www.jurist.org/news/2017/02/bangladesh-passes-law-allowing-some-child-marriages/
   - *Table* — UNICEF/Girls Not Brides regional shares of the world's child brides: **South Asia 45% (India alone ≈ one-third of the global total), Sub-Saharan Africa 20%, East Asia & Pacific 15%, Latin America & Caribbean 9%** (UNICEF data via end-violence.org compiling UNICEF figures) https://www.end-violence.org/articles/progress-needs-be-20x-faster-end-child-marriage-2030-unicef-data ; over the past 25 years ~68 million child marriages were averted, driven mainly by decline in India, but COVID-19 is estimated to have added 10 million additional child marriages; UNICEF states progress must run "nearly 20 times faster" to hit the 2030 SDG target. DHS/MICS microdata underlying all of the above: https://dhsprogram.com/ and https://mics.unicef.org/
8. **Falsifier:** **does raising the statutory minimum age actually move measured prevalence, or just push the practice unregistered?** Collin & Talbot (Center for Global Development working paper, "Do Age of Marriage Laws Work? Evidence from a Large Sample of Developing Countries," using DHS/MICS data, ~63-86 countries, 1995–2012, testing for a bunching discontinuity at the legal cutoff) find the clean-story cell dies: countries that raised the legal minimum saw the share of women married **under** the new legal age rise by roughly 20 percentage points, and the statistical signature of enforcement (bunching at the cutoff) was **50–55% weaker** after a reform (75–93% weaker with adjacent-bin controls); only 44% of countries show any enforcement signal at all at the no-parental-consent cutoff. Their conclusion, stated plainly: "new laws will not, on their own, be enough to prevent early marriage" — enforcement tracks general rule-of-law/governance capacity, not the statute itself, and rural areas show weaker enforcement than urban. https://www.cgdev.org/sites/default/files/do-age-marriage-laws-work-evidence-large-sample-developing-countries.pdf . Girls Not Brides' own CRANK synthesis ("The Law and Child Marriage: Synthesis Report of Recent Evidence") and its companion Mexico-specific paper ("Will You Marry Me, Later? Age-of-Marriage Laws and Child Marriage in Mexico") independently confirm the underground-displacement mechanism rather than a clean law-reduces-prevalence story. https://www.girlsnotbrides.org/learning-resources/child-marriage-research-action-network/crank-research-tracker/ . This is the cell that dies first if the "statute ends the practice" row is wrong: not a poll, an actual discontinuity test against DHS microdata.
9. **Grain:** campaign-grain (multi-decade statutory-reform campaign, country by country) nested inside the longer cycle of international personhood/age-of-majority norms (CRC 1989 onward); each national statute passage is itself an event within that campaign.
10. **Org:** Girls Not Brides — UK charity no. 1154230, FY2024 gross income **£5.35m**, expenditure **£4.81m** (FY2023: income £9.41m/spend £4.08m; FY2022: £4.08m/£3.37m) per UK Charity Commission financial history https://register-of-charities.charitycommission.gov.uk/en/charity-search/-/charity-details/5040402/financial-history ; network of roughly 1,800+ member civil-society organizations across 100+ countries (Girls Not Brides self-reported network figures, per its About page https://girlsnotbrides.org/about-us ). Counter-org is diffuse/non-roster (customary courts, local religious authorities) — the asymmetry itself is part of why enforcement fails: one side is an NGO+UN-agency coalition with a budget line, the other is a decentralized institution with no 990 to pull.

### 13.2 FGM/C abolition (criminalization statute vs cultural/religious practice-holders)

1. **Who:** WHO + UNICEF + national criminal-code amendments (Egypt, Sudan, and ~26 other African states with national FGM bans) vs. cultural/religious practice-holders — cutters (traditional practitioners, in some countries now medicalized doctors/nurses performing it clinically), elders, and in some contexts clerics who frame cutting as a purity/marriageability precondition rather than a religious obligation per se.
2. **Does what:** criminalizes the act (prison terms, fines) vs. performs/arranges the cutting, increasingly medicalized (shifted to clinical settings to evade the "backstreet" framing and sometimes the law itself).
3. **To whom:** girls, overwhelmingly cut before age 15 and increasingly before age 5 in some countries (UNICEF notes a shift toward cutting at younger ages) https://www.unicef.org/somalia/press-releases/over-230-million-girls-fgm ; counter-target is the cutter/practitioner class whose livelihood and status the law criminalizes.
4. **Authority:** cover = penal code amendment (statute); medium = UN agency campaign literature (WHO/UNICEF joint statements, "A Global Concern" reports) vs. kinship/religious teaching; threat = prison sentence (Egypt: up to 20 years as of the 2021 toughening) vs. social threat of a daughter being deemed unmarriageable/impure if left uncut; cloak = clinical medicalization, which launders the act under a medical-procedure cover rather than a religious or customary one.
5. **Date:** as-of 2026-11-14. UNICEF, March 2024: **over 230 million girls and women alive today have been subjected to FGM**, up from a 200 million estimate cited in 2016 — the rise reflects both new data coverage and population growth in practicing countries outpacing the rate of decline. https://www.unicef.org/somalia/press-releases/over-230-million-girls-fgm
6. **Effect / dinner:** where decline is real, girls gain bodily-integrity standing and (per the Egypt cohort data below) marginally improved health outcomes; cutters lose income and status; where criminalization outruns enforcement, the practice medicalizes (a doctor performs it in a clinic, evading both the back-alley stigma and in some cases prosecution) rather than stopping.
7. **Receipt:**
   - *History* — statute dates: Egypt first criminalized FGM in 2008 (ministerial decree/penal code amendment following the 2007 death of an 11-year-old during a cutting procedure), toughened penalties in **August 2016** (Parliament raised prison terms), toughened again in **January 2021** raising the maximum sentence to **up to 20 years** https://www.pressherald.com/2016/08/31/egypt-makes-penalty-more-severe-to-deter-female-genital-mutilation/ · https://egyptianstreets.com/2021/01/21/egypt-20-years-imprisonment-for-female-genital-mutilation/ ; Sudan criminalized FGM by presidential decree amending its 1991 Criminal Act, ratified by the Sovereign Council in **late April/early May 2020**, with up to 3 years' imprisonment for practitioners https://www.moroccoworldnews.com/2020/05/70270/sudan-criminalizes-female-genital-mutilation/ · https://www.pbs.org/newshour/world/sudan-ratifies-law-criminalizing-female-genital-mutilation . Academic cohort history: van Rossem, Meekers & Gage, "The decline of FGM in Egypt since 1987: a cohort analysis of the Egypt Demographic and Health Surveys" (BMC Women's Health, 2020) https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7216476/
   - *Table* — Egypt birth-cohort Kaplan-Meier cutting-by-age estimates from the above paper: cohort **1987–1990** cut by age 15 = **77%** (by age 19 = 78%); cohort **1991–1995** = **66–67%**; cohort **1996–2000** = **54%** (by age 15); cohort **2001–2005** = **21%** (by age 10, the youngest fully observable window) — a real intergenerational drop, base cutting-hazard for the 2010 cohort running at roughly **30% of the 1987 cohort's hazard**. Country-level current prevalence: Sudan, women 15–49, **87%** (SDG indicator 5.3.2), rising to **98% in North Kordofan** (UNICEF Sudan country data https://data.unicef.org/wp-content/uploads/cp/fgm/FGM_SDN.pdf ); global stock 230M+ per UNICEF March 2024 https://www.unicef.org/press-releases/over-230-million-girls-and-women-alive-today-have-been-subjected-female-genital . Somalia remains one of the highest-prevalence countries in the world (widely cited at or near 98-99% of women 15-49 across DHS/MICS and FGM-focused NGO country profiles, e.g. 28toomany https://www.28toomany.org/ and fgmcri.org country pages https://fgmcri.org/country/somalia ) — flagged here as a round, oft-repeated figure rather than a single freshly-pulled DHS line, since UNICEF's own Somalia country PDF did not load cleanly on this pull; re-verify against data.unicef.org/wp-content/uploads/cp/fgm/FGM_SOM.pdf before citing the exact decimal.
8. **Falsifier:** **is the Egypt decline driven by law, by education, or by something else?** Van Rossem et al. explicitly reject a single-cause "law did it" story: the decline is too broad-based across social strata to be explained by rising women's status/education alone (which would concentrate the drop among already-modernized groups), and they describe the law's direct effect as "limited and unclear given weak enforcement" even while allowing it may matter cumulatively over the long run through norm-setting rather than prosecution. They attribute the trend instead to a diffuse mix of anti-FGM campaigning, international pressure, and legislation acting together — explicitly **not** reducible to the criminalization-statute cell alone. This is the falsifier: if "criminalize it and it ends" were the right row, Egypt's near-flat enforcement rate (prosecutions remain rare relative to the 2008-2021 legal escalation) would show up as noise against a real cohort decline — and it does. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7216476/
9. **Grain:** cycle-grain — multi-generational (1987 birth cohort to 2001-2005 birth cohort is a 15-20 year span), with each national criminalization statute as an event inside it; not reducible to a single campaign year.
10. **Org:** WHO (FGM policy brief/guidelines unit, part of WHO's Department of Sexual and Reproductive Health, Geneva) https://www.who.int/ · UNICEF Child Protection section, publisher of the 2024 "Female Genital Mutilation: A Global Concern" report (uni.cf/FGMreport2024) · 28 Too Many — UK-registered charity producing the country-law-report series cited above https://www.28toomany.org/ . Counter-side is non-rostered (individual cutters/practitioners, extended-family decision-makers) — same budget asymmetry as 13.1.

### 13.3 State-enforced dress/mobility codes differentiated by sex (Iran: Guidance Patrol/hijab law vs. Woman, Life, Freedom)

1. **Who:** Islamic Republic of Iran's Gasht-e Ershad ("Guidance Patrol," the morality police under Law Enforcement Command/FARAJA, state) enforcing compulsory-hijab law, vs. the decentralized **Woman, Life, Freedom** (Zan, Zendegi, Azadi) protest movement that erupted after Mahsa Jina Amini's death in Guidance Patrol custody on **September 16, 2022**.
2. **Does what:** detains/fines/beats women for "improper" hijab (state side); the movement burns/removes headscarves in public, strikes, and street-protests (opposition side).
3. **To whom:** women and girls as young as early adolescence subject to the dress code; secondarily, the protest movement's own participants become the target of state repression (arrest, prosecution, execution).
4. **Authority:** cover = statute — Iran's long-standing compulsory-hijab provisions plus the new **"Law to Protect the Family through the Promotion of the Culture of Chastity and Hijab"**, approved by Parliament **September 20, 2023** and by the Guardian Council in **September 2024**, set to run as a three-year trial https://www.hrw.org/news/2024/10/14/iran-new-hijab-law-adds-restrictions-and-punishments ; medium = state broadcast + the movement's own medium = decentralized phone-video/social clips (connective-action repertoire, Bennett & Segerberg frame, §0); threat = fines (Article 47: 20 million–500 million rials, ~US$31-790, rising to 1.5 billion rials/$2,380 plus a 2-year travel/online ban and up to 5 years' prison for repeat offenses; Article 48, nudity/semi-nudity: up to 10 years, 15 on repeat) and, for protesters, live ammunition and execution; cloak = none — this is an openly statutory, uncloaked enforcement regime, unusual for this file's cover-typology.
5. **Date:** as-of 2026-11-14. Amini died September 16, 2022; the Chastity and Hijab law passed Parliament September 20, 2023, cleared the Guardian Council September 2024, described by HRW as about to "come into force" — provisional, not final, as of the October 2024 reporting; status of actual enforcement rollout in 2025-2026 should be re-checked against current wire reporting before citing as settled.
6. **Effect / dinner:** HRANA (Human Rights Activists News Agency) reported, as of its January 2023 tally, **522 protesters killed (including 70 children)**, **19,400+ arrested (including 168 children)**, and **110 arrestees under threat of a death sentence** since September 2022 https://www.jurist.org/news/2023/01/human-rights-group-reports-522-iran-protestors-deaths-since-september-2022/ ; Global Voices/human-rights reporting documented an **80% spike in executions** in the two years after the protests began (to September 2024) https://globalvoices.org/2024/09/16/iran-sees-80-spike-in-executions-two-years-after-protests/ — the state's "dinner" is deterrence-by-execution rather than material gain.
7. **Receipt:**
   - *History* — HRANA rolling protest-casualty reports (English portal) https://en-hrana.org/ ; Iran Human Rights (Oslo-based NGO) annual death-penalty reports https://iranhr.net/ ; HRW on the 2024 hijab law https://www.hrw.org/news/2024/10/14/iran-new-hijab-law-adds-restrictions-and-punishments ; Library of Congress Global Legal Monitor on the proposed law, Sept 2023 https://www.loc.gov/item/global-legal-monitor/2023-09-05/iran-law-to-protect-the-family-by-promoting-the-culture-of-chastity-and-hijab-proposed/ ; Wikipedia as finding aid only, "Mahsa Amini protests" and "Compulsory hijab in Iran" — both need primary-source verification per §0's rule.
   - *Table* — HRANA's running casualty/arrest counters (cited above); Iran Human Rights annual execution statistics; ACLED Iran event data for protest-event counts over time https://acleddata.com/ .
8. **Falsifier:** **does enforcement intensity track regime stability/confidence rather than the statute itself?** Real-world test: the Guidance Patrol was effectively pulled off the street in the weeks after the December 2022 unrest (widely reported, inaccurately, as "abolition" of the morality police), then explicitly **resumed patrols July 16-17, 2023** once street pressure had cooled — a visible on/off cycle keyed to the regime's read of its own security position, not to any change in the underlying statute, which stayed constant throughout https://www.semafor.com/article/07/17/2023/irans-morality-police-are-back · https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/world/493891/iran-s-morality-police-to-resume-headscarf-patrols . If enforcement were purely statute-driven, the on/off pattern should not exist; it does, which is the cell that dies if "law = enforcement" is assumed instead of "regime confidence modulates enforcement of a constant law." The 2023-2024 escalation to a harsher, technology-enabled statute (CCTV-triggered fines, Article 42's 12-hour platform-takedown mandate) during a period of *lower* street mobilization than 2022 also cuts against a simple deterrence-of-live-protest story — it looks more like consolidation after the movement's acute phase passed, which the method-owed process-tracing (not a poll) would need to adjudicate against the regime-stability hypothesis directly.
9. **Grain:** event (Amini's death, Sept 16 2022) → campaign (Woman, Life, Freedom street phase, Sept 2022-ish through its de-escalation by mid-2023) → ongoing low-boil cycle (statutory counter-mobilization 2023-2026, still live). Do not collapse the three grains into one "the uprising."
10. **Org:** state side — FARAJA (Iran's Law Enforcement Command) and the Guidance Patrol unit within it; no public budget line (opaque security-state financing, not a 990). Movement side is deliberately thin-org/leaderless (connective-action repertoire per Bennett & Segerberg, §0 framing section) — HRANA and Iran Human Rights are documentation NGOs, not the movement's organizational core, which by design has no roster to pull; this asymmetry (documented opposition NGOs vs. an undocumented protest base) is itself a method note, not a gap in research.

**Method owed across 13.1-13.3:** all three crests pair a real falsifier against the "law/statute alone explains the outcome" story and all three come back with the same finding — statutory change is necessary-but-not-sufficient, and enforcement capacity/regime incentive, not the text of the law, is the live variable. That convergence across three unrelated legal domains (marriage-age, criminal-code FGM bans, dress-code statute) is itself worth flagging as a possible higher-order pattern for this file's method section, not just three separate footnotes.
