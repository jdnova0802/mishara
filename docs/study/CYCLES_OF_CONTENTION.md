# Cycles of contention — PhD research map

**Not Nisaba. Not a tweet. Not a camp. Not a replacement for the shop.**  
Owner study file. This is a **research map**: for each numbered crest you get operational fact, mobilizing plot, carriers, the **legal/primary file**, the **historiographic fight** (what dissertations actually argue), and **links that open**. It is not a finished dissertation. A PhD is one crest + one method + one table. Date every data pull `YYYY-MM-DD`. Do not renumber 19th-century crests because TikTok moved.

**Formal name of the subject:** *contentious politics*. Wave shape: **cycle of contention** (Charles Tilly, Sidney Tarrow, Doug McAdam). Street phrase: “social-movement waves.” **Waves of feminism** are one family *inside* this file, not the spine.

**Companion machines (do not mash):** techno-economic paradigms (Perez); cohort/age-structure; hegemonic cycles; medium/attention. Those ask *what got cheap / how many bodies / who holds the table / what clips*. This file asks **who revolts, with what orgs, under what opportunity, with what frame**.

Advocacy site ≠ census. If a stranger cannot open the table, it is a plot, not a refresh. Publisher / DOI / archive / statute first. Wikipedia is a finding aid, not the file.

**Street equation (heuristic, not a law of nature):**

`Need(old institution) ≈ 1 / (new wage or law × exit option × frame × medium)`

When **exit gets cheap**, the old “you must” dies. The slogan **names** it. The medium **purifies** it. The counterwave **sells yesterday**. Irina Dunn did not move the BLS.

**How to read every numbered crest below**

- **Fact:** what became optional or compulsory (statute, wage, death, vote).
- **Plot:** loyalty test (nuance = betrayal).
- **Carriers:** named org types, not a Grey Pope.
- **File:** primary law / case / archive URL.
- **Canon:** the books/articles a committee will expect.
- **Fight:** the live scholarly disagreement.
- **Data:** the table you open instead of vibes.
- **Live split:** electorate ≠ comment section.

**PhD habit:** one **theory** (Tarrow + McAdam + Snow/Benford), one **case** (one numbered crest), one **dataset** (ACLED or NAVCO or V-Dem or BLS). Do not “collect waves” as identity. If a book makes you feel **initiated**, you are in the plot. Put it down. Open a **table**.

## 0. What a cycle is (theory you actually open)

A cycle of contention is a **temporally clustered rise and fall of collective claim-making** — protests, strikes, campaigns, revolutions, countermobilization — that **diffuses** across groups and sites, then **exhausts** or **institutionalizes** (Tarrow, *Power in Movement*, ch. on cycles).

**Mechanism stack (do not skip):**

1. **Political opportunity / threat** — elite split, state capacity, repression, access. Opportunity is not “the people woke up.”
2. **Mobilizing structures** — churches, unions, campuses, parties, mosques, diasporas, Signal groups.
3. **Framing** — diagnosis, prognosis, motivation (Snow & Benford); Entman problem / cause / moral / remedy.
4. **Repertoires** — historically learned toolkit (barricade, boycott, occupation, hashtag) (Tilly).
5. **Diffusion and scale shift** — local → national → transnational.
6. **Countermovement** — restoration plot with its own orgs.
7. **Outcome** — law, collapse, co-optation, schism, or a feed.

## 1. Black freedom (US-weighted; rhymes in other empires)

Carriers across crests: Black churches, NAACP, Urban League, SCLC, SNCC, CORE, UNIA, Nation of Islam, Black Panther Party, later Movement for Black Lives orgs + phones as evidence. McAdam’s claim: **indigenous organizations + political opportunity**, not a sudden moral awakening.

Rhymes (same scarce goods: safety, vote, land; different carriers): Haitian Revolution; Caribbean/African independence; ANC; Brazilian *movimento negro*. Do not clone US periodization onto them.

### 1.1 Abolition → emancipation (~1830s–1865)

- **Fact:** chattel as legal property ends *on paper* (13th Amendment, 1865). War is the enforcement. Haitian Revolution (1791–1804) is the Atlantic prior file.
- **Plot:** all men created equal — enforced by armies, not pamphlets alone.
- **Carriers:** Black churches; free-Black mutual aid; Garrisonian societies; Liberty/Free Soil/Republican parties; Union Army as the terminal SMO.
- **File:** 13th Amendment (NARA) https://www.archives.gov/milestone-documents/13th-amendment · Emancipation Proclamation https://www.archives.gov/milestone-documents/emancipation-proclamation · Haitian Constitution lineage via LOC.
- **Canon:** Du Bois, *Black Reconstruction in America* (1935) — Black workers as agents, not wards. IA: https://archive.org/details/blackreconstruc00dubo · Foner, *The Fiery Trial*; Davis, *The Problem of Slavery in the Age of Revolution*; Drescher, *Abolition*; James, *The Black Jacobins*.
- **Fight:** moral-suasion (Garrison) vs political abolition vs **war as the real mechanism**. Du Bois vs Dunning school (the latter is the **discredited** Redemption historiography — know it to reject it).
- **Data:** trans-Atlantic slave trade database https://www.slavevoyages.org/ · US census slave schedules via NARA/IPUMS https://www.ipums.org/

### 1.2 Reconstruction → nadir / Jim Crow (~1865–1950s) — *counterwave*

- **Fact:** 14th/15th on paper; vote and land stripped in practice. *Plessy* (1896) legalizes caste. Convict lease, lynching, disfranchisement constitutions.
- **Plot:** “Redemption,” home rule, white civilization.
- **Carriers:** Democratic Party in the South, Klan/White League/Red Shirts, courts, “Redeemer” constitutions. Black carriers: churches, Mutual aid, early NAACP (1909).
- **File:** 14th https://www.archives.gov/milestone-documents/14th-amendment · 15th https://www.archives.gov/milestone-documents/15th-amendment · *Plessy v. Ferguson* https://www.oyez.org/cases/1850-1900/163us537 · Civil Rights Act 1875 then *Civil Rights Cases* (1883).
- **Canon:** Foner, *Reconstruction: America’s Unfinished Revolution* · Du Bois, *Black Reconstruction* · Woodward, *The Strange Career of Jim Crow* · Wells, *Southern Horrors* (lynching as the file) · Equal Justice Initiative lynching data https://lynchinginamerica.eji.org/ · NAACP papers (LOC) https://www.loc.gov/collections/manuscripts-from-the-naacp/
- **Fight:** Foner/Du Bois (Black political agency + betrayal) vs old Dunning (Reconstruction as corruption). The Dunning school lost in the academy; it still lives as a **restoration plot**.
- **Data:** voter turnout series (ICPSR/ANES later); lynching inventories (EJI; Tolnay & Beck).

### 1.3 Long civil rights (~1954–1968)

- **Fact:** *Brown* (1954); CRA 1964; VRA 1965; Fair Housing 1968 — public accommodations, vote, formal equality.
- **Plot:** King law-as-sermon vs post-1965 street. Loyalty tests appear *inside* the coalition after Selma.
- **Carriers:** Black churches + Black colleges + NAACP chapters (McAdam structures). SCLC, SNCC, CORE. Opportunity: WWII, Great Migration, Northern Black vote, Cold War embarrassment.
- **File:** *Brown v. Board* https://www.oyez.org/cases/1940-1955/347us483 · NARA lesson https://www.archives.gov/education/lessons/brown-v-board · CRA 1964 https://www.archives.gov/milestone-documents/civil-rights-act · VRA 1965 https://www.archives.gov/milestone-documents/voting-rights-act · King, “Letter from Birmingham Jail” (1963) https://www.africa.upenn.edu/Articles_Gen/Letter_Birmingham.html
- **Canon:** McAdam, *Political Process* (above) · Morris, *The Origins of the Civil Rights Movement* · Payne, *I’ve Got the Light of Freedom* (local people, not only King) · Branch, *Parting the Waters* (narrative; not the theory) · Garrow, *Bearing the Cross*.
- **Fight:** King-centered national narrative vs **local-people** historiography (Payne). POS vs resource-mobilization vs “Black church as the SMO.”
- **Data:** VRA coverage formulas (DOJ archive); Black voter registration series; *Shelby County v. Holder* (2013) as later **legal counterwave** https://www.oyez.org/cases/2012/12-96

### 1.4 Black Power / nationalist (~late 1960s–70s)

- **Fact:** pride as program; police as the immediate file; survival programs; COINTELPRO as state repertoire.
- **Plot:** dignity is not a permission slip; “colonies at home.”
- **Carriers:** SNCC after 1966; Black Panther Party; Nation of Islam; Republic of New Afrika; African liberation solidarity orgs.
- **File:** FBI Vault COINTELPRO https://vault.fbi.gov/cointel-pro · Black nationalist files IA https://archive.org/details/FBI-COINTELPRO-BLACK · Church Committee reports (assassination/intel) https://www.intelligence.senate.gov/resources/intelligence-related-commissions
- **Canon:** Joseph, *Waiting ’Til the Midnight Hour* · Bloom & Martin, *Black against Empire* · Carmichael & Hamilton, *Black Power* · Newton, *Revolutionary Suicide* (primary, not gospel).
- **Fight:** “Black Power killed the movement” vs “liberal historiography hid the radicals.” Empirical: COINTELPRO + urban risings + Vietnam as **opportunity/threat**, not a slogan inventing a decade.
- **Data:** riot commissions (Kerner 1968); BJS historical imprisonment (next crest).

### 1.5 Colorblind / neoliberal counter-frame (~1980s–2010s)

- **Fact:** mass incarceration, War on Drugs, urban policy, Section 8/HOPE VI, crack as the **file** under the plot. Formal equality stays; the carceral state grows.
- **Plot:** the race story is the hustle; merit is enough; “welfare queen.”
- **Carriers:** parties after the Southern realignment; think tanks; courts (*Croson*, *Adarand*, later *Students for Fair Admissions*).
- **File:** BJS corrections https://bjs.ojp.gov/ · Sentencing Project tables https://www.sentencingproject.org/ · Alexander, *The New Jim Crow* (pair with BJS, not as sole gospel) · Forman, *Locking Up Our Own* (the needed counter-book).
- **Canon:** Wacquant, *Punishing the Poor* · Western, *Punishment and Inequality in America* · Wilson, *The Truly Disadvantaged* · Omi & Winant, *Racial Formation*.
- **Fight:** Alexander (Jim Crow analogy) vs Forman/Fortner (Black support for tough-on-crime as **also** a file). Colorblindness as ideology (Bonilla-Silva) vs declining discrimination thesis (older Sowell/Herrnstein plots — treat as **contention**, test with tables).
- **Data:** BJS; FBI UCR/NIBRS https://cde.ucr.cjis.gov/ · NLSY; ACS https://www.census.gov/

### 1.6 BLM / after (~2013–)

- **Fact:** phones as evidence; police as the file. Floyd 2020 as scale-shift. *Qualified immunity* and local DA files as the operational bottleneck.
- **Plot:** say the name vs “order / crime / fatherlessness.”
- **Carriers:** local BLM chapters (uneven); Campaign Zero; churches again; then **connective action** (Bennett & Segerberg) more than a single SMO. Counter: FOP, DA races, “back the blue.”
- **File:** Mapping Police Violence https://mappingpoliceviolence.org/ · Washington Post fatal-force https://www.washingtonpost.com/graphics/investigations/police-shootings-database/ · Pew police/race https://www.pewresearch.org/topic/race-ethnicity/
- **Canon:** Taylor, *From #BlackLivesMatter to Black Liberation* · Francis, work on NAACP as professionalized SMO vs BLM as connective · Oliver, event catalogs of 2020.
- **Fight:** “new civil rights” vs “riot frame.” Empirical live split is **crime and schools vs police and repair**. Not “does racism exist.”
- **Live data:** BJS NCVS; CDC WONDER homicide; Gallup confidence in police https://news.gallup.com/

## 2. Labor and the state

Carriers: craft unions, industrial unions, socialist/labor/communist parties, Catholic social orgs, later NGOs, then **right-populist parties** speaking working-class vote.

### 2.1 Craft / early unions (19th c.)

- **Fact:** hours, shop control, the strike as repertoire. Combination laws repeal (UK 1824–25); US conspiracy doctrine slowly dies.
- **Plot:** respectability of the trades vs the mob.
- **Carriers:** craft lodges, Knights of Labor (later), friendly societies.
- **File:** UK Combination Acts historiography via UK Parliament living heritage; Haymarket (1886) Chicago History Museum.
- **Canon:** Thompson, *The Making of the English Working Class* · Webb & Webb, *The History of Trade Unionism* · Commons et al., *History of Labour in the United States* · Hobsbawm, *Labouring Men*.
- **Fight:** Thompson (experience/culture) vs structural Marxism vs commons-institutional US labor history.
- **Data:** strike series (US Commissioner of Labor historical; later BLS).

### 2.2 Industrial / mass labor / socialist (~1890s–1940s)

- **Fact:** factory, eight-hour day, welfare experiments, the **party**. Internationals as **orgs**, not a ghost king.
- **Plot:** workers of the world vs national labor aristocracy.
- **Carriers:** IWW, CIO, SPD, Labour Party, SFIO, PCI, unions in the Second International then Comintern split.
- **File:** Second International congresses; Wagner Act / NLRA (1935) https://www.nlrb.gov/guidance/key-reference-materials/national-labor-relations-act · ILO founding 1919 https://www.ilo.org/
- **Canon:** Silver, *Forces of Labor* (Cambridge, 2003) — world-scale unrest database.  
  https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/forces-of-labor/24F7A6E01DA0617159FCCC7A6F68FC40 · DOI https://doi.org/10.1017/CBO9780511615702  
  Polanyi, *The Great Transformation* (1944) — double movement.  
  Hobsbawm, *Age of Extremes*.
- **Fight:** Leninist vanguard vs social-democratic incorporation. Silver: Marx-type unrest (new working classes) vs Polanyi-type (against commodification).
- **Data:** ILOSTAT https://ilostat.ilo.org/ · Silver/WLF labor unrest catalogs (book appendix + Braudel Center lineage).

### 2.3 Social democracy / New Deal compact (~1930s–1970s)

- **Fact:** union density peak, pensions, mixed economy. The state **absorbs** the crest (Wagner, Beveridge, Rehn-Meidner, *Les Trente Glorieuses*).
- **Plot:** class peace as patriotism; later New Left says the compact sold out.
- **Carriers:** CIO-AFL, Labour/SPD/SAP, Christian Democrats as **absorbers** in parts of Europe.
- **File:** Social Security Act 1935 (NARA) · Beveridge Report (UK Parliament/National Archives) · Taft-Hartley 1947 as US **legal counterwave**.
- **Canon:** Esping-Andersen, *The Three Worlds of Welfare Capitalism* (1990) — decommodification.  
  Korpi, power-resource theory.  
  Lichtenstein, *State of the Union*.  
  Piven & Cloward, *Poor People’s Movements* — disruption, not just union density.
- **Fight:** power-resource (labor did it) vs varieties-of-capitalism (firms wanted skills) vs Piven/Cloward (poor disruption, elite response).
- **Data:** OECD union density https://www.oecd.org/en/data.html · BLS union members https://www.bls.gov/cps/cpslutabs.htm · LIS inequality https://www.lisdatacenter.org/

### 2.4 Neoliberal smash (~1979–2008)

- **Fact:** capital mobility, union decline, inflation-first macro, privatization. Thatcher/Reagan as **opportunity for capital**, not a slogan inventing globalization.
- **Plot:** there is no alternative; markets as morality.
- **Carriers:** parties after 1970s inflation; central banks; IMF conditionality (Global South overlap §9.5); employer offensives (PATCO 1981 as US crest).
- **File:** Volcker; UK Employment Acts 1980s; WTO 1995 https://www.wto.org/ · IMF history https://www.imf.org/
- **Canon:** Harvey, *A Brief History of Neoliberalism* · Streeck, *Buying Time* · Blyth, *Austerity* · Hall, “The Political Power of Economic Ideas” (Keynesianism to monetarism).
- **Fight:** ideas (Blyth) vs class power (Harvey) vs capital-mobility constraint (Frieden). Do not treat “neoliberalism” as a cabal name; specify **policy instruments**.
- **Data:** BLS; ILO; Piketty/WID https://wid.world/ · IMF IFS.

### 2.5 After the crash / populist labor (~2008–)

- **Fact:** GFC, Occupy, then **right-populist working-class vote**, gig classification fights, industrial policy return (partial).
- **Plot:** elites vs people — left and right versions. Live split is **size of the state, who captured it, prices**, not the Internationale.
- **Carriers:** Occupy (connective, org-thin); then unions in specific sectors (logistics, Hollywood, UAW 2023); populist parties.
- **File:** Dodd-Frank; NLRB joint-employer/gig cases; BLS CPI https://www.bls.gov/cpi/
- **Canon:** Gitlin on Occupy; della Porta on anti-austerity; Cramer, *The Politics of Resentment*; Gest; Hochschild *Strangers in Their Own Land* (plot-adjacent — pair with ANES).
- **Data:** ANES https://electionstudies.org/ · BLS · OECD · Pew economic attitudes https://www.pewresearch.org/

## 3. Environment / climate

Carriers: Sierra-type conservation orgs, scientists, EPA-like agencies, Green parties, climate strikes, oil-state/consumer counter-orgs.

### 3.1 Conservation / wilderness (~1890s–1950s)

- **Fact:** parks, forests, game laws. Nature as cathedral **and** as timber reserve (Muir vs Pinchot).
- **Plot:** sublime wilderness vs wise use.
- **Carriers:** Sierra Club (1892), Boone and Crockett, Forest Service, National Park Service Organic Act 1916.
- **File:** NPS Organic Act history https://www.nps.gov/ · USFS history https://www.fs.usda.gov/
- **Canon:** Nash, *Wilderness and the American Mind* · Hays, *Conservation and the Gospel of Efficiency* · Cronon, “The Trouble with Wilderness” (*Uncommon Ground*) — the **fight** you must know.
- **Fight:** wilderness as elite anti-modern vs democratic public lands. Cronon vs deep-ecology plots.
- **Data:** park visitation; land-in-protection series (WDPA) https://www.protectedplanet.net/

### 3.2 Regulation / Silent Spring (~1960s–80s)

- **Fact:** Carson 1962; Earth Day 1970; EPA 1970; Clean Air / Clean Water / NEPA / ESA — air/water as **public**.
- **Plot:** science as sermon vs “jobs vs owls.”
- **Carriers:** new environmental SMOs (NRDC, EDF, FOE), scientists, then Green parties in Europe.
- **File:** EPA history https://www.epa.gov/history · NEPA; ESA https://www.fws.gov/law/endangered-species-act · Carson bibliographic LOC https://www.loc.gov/item/2021666185/
- **Canon:** Carson, *Silent Spring* · Gottlieb, *Forcing the Spring* · Hays, *Beauty, Health, and Permanence* · Dunlap & Mertig on US environmentalism as a social movement.
- **Fight:** public-health frame vs wilderness frame (they are **not** the same movement). Environmental justice (Bullard) as later split: who lives next to the plant.
- **Data:** EPA air trends; CDC biomonitoring; EEA Europe https://www.eea.europa.eu/

### 3.3 Climate science as politics (~1988–2010s)

- **Fact:** carbon as the file. Hansen Senate testimony 23 Jun 1988; IPCC 1988–; UNFCCC 1992; Kyoto 1997; Paris 2015.
- **Plot:** the science is settled vs the science is a hustle. Loyalty tests on both sides.
- **Carriers:** IPCC as **assessment org** (states + scientists); climate SMOs; fossil trade associations as countermovement.
- **File:** Hansen testimony (scan) https://image.guardian.co.uk/sys-files/Environment/documents/2008/06/23/ClimateChangeHearing1988.pdf · IPCC https://www.ipcc.ch/ · AR6 Synthesis https://www.ipcc.ch/report/ar6/syr/ · UNFCCC https://unfccc.int/ · Paris Agreement.
- **Canon:** Weart, *The Discovery of Global Warming* (AIP, often open) https://history.aip.org/climate/index.htm · Oreskes & Conway, *Merchants of Doubt* (countermovement of knowledge) · Hulme, *Why We Disagree About Climate Change* (disagreement is not only “denial”).
- **Fight:** knowledge-capture (Oreskes) vs cultural cognition (Kahan) vs political economy of carbon (Mitchell, *Carbon Democracy*).
- **Data:** NASA GISS https://data.giss.nasa.gov/gistemp/ · NOAA https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/ · Global Carbon Project · OWID CO₂ https://ourworldindata.org/co2-and-greenhouse-gas-emissions

### 3.4 Emergency vs hustle (~2010s–)

- **Plot A:** pause the world or the world ends (XR, school strikes).  
- **Plot B:** green elites / energy prices / private jets.
- **Fact:** Energiewende, IRA 2022, EU ETS — **measures vs cost** is the live splitter. Conservation (§3.1) is not this fight.
- **Carriers:** Fridays for Future; Sunrise; XR; then farmer/yellow-vest/AfD-adjacent energy-price countermobilization.
- **File:** IEA https://www.iea.org/ · Germany energy (BMWK/AGEB) · MIDEM polarization https://forum-midem.de/ · OWID energy https://ourworldindata.org/energy
- **Canon:** McAdam on climate as a **failed** mass US movement (opportunity/orgs); Fisher on youth climate; Malm (plot-adjacent — read as a frame, pair with IEA tables).
- **Live split:** measures vs household energy cost, high affect. Refresh Pew climate https://www.pewresearch.org/topic/environment/climate-energy/

## 4. LGBTQ

Carriers: homophile orgs (Mattachine, Daughters of Bilitis), Stonewall-era street, GLF, ACT UP, HRC-type law orgs, then intra-coalition splits.

### 4.1 Decriminalize / medical (~1950s–70s; Stonewall 1969 as crest)

- **Fact:** the state stops treating same-sex acts as crime (**uneven**). Wolfenden UK 1957 → Sexual Offences Act 1967; DSM drops homosexuality 1973; *Lawrence* US 2003 finishes the US criminal file (late).
- **Plot:** illness vs sin vs civil liberty.
- **Carriers:** homophile respectability orgs, then street after Stonewall.
- **File:** Wolfenden (UK Parliament) https://www.parliament.uk/about/living-heritage/transformingsociety/private-lives/relationships/collections1/sexual-offences-act-1967/wolfenden-report-/ · *Lawrence v. Texas* https://www.oyez.org/cases/2002/02-102 · APA DSM history (APA.org).
- **Canon:** D’Emilio, *Sexual Politics, Sexual Communities* · Duberman, *Stonewall* · Weeks, *Coming Out* (UK) · Chauncey, *Gay New York*.
- **Fight:** Stonewall as origin myth vs homophile **decades** of org-building (D’Emilio). Medicalization as oppression vs as the exit from criminal law.
- **Data:** ILGA world statutes map https://ilga.org/ · Williams Institute https://williamsinstitute.law.ucla.edu/

### 4.2 AIDS / ACT UP (~1980s–90s)

- **Fact:** death; FDA/NIH pipeline; CDC definition; the quilt. Operational: drug approval time + stigma as the file.
- **Plot:** silence = death vs “lifestyle.”
