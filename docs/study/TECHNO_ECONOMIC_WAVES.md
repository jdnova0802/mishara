# Techno-economic waves (Perez / Kondratiev)

**Not cycles of contention. Not a tweet. Not “4th industrial revolution” marketing. Not Nisaba.**

**Subject:** *techno-economic paradigms* (Carlota Perez); related older name: *long waves* (Nikolai Kondratiev). Schumpeter’s *creative destruction* is the mechanism story; Perez’s **great surges** are the diffusion story. Perez **breaks** with Kondratiev/Schumpeter on GDP upswing/downswing as the object. She dates **propagation of a technological revolution**, not a sine wave in aggregate output.

**Job of this file:** what became **cheap to build and run**. Movements ride this; they do not invent the railroad. Contention, hegemony, cohorts, and medium/attention are **other machines**. Villain classes, feminism waves, “globalists” are the wrong file.

Each surge (Perez): **irruption → frenzy → crash → synergy/deployment → maturity.**  
**Installation** = finance + hype + bubbles. **Deployment** = the thing in the **payroll**.  
**Crash rule:** the story dies in the trough; the **infrastructure** does not.

S-curve / hype cycle (Rogers; Gartner): **loud ≠ deployed.** Zip2 sat in ICT **deployment**. Money-on-the-rail was the next software primitive on that stack — a firm choice, not a sixth surge.

A dissertation is **one numbered surge + one periodization fight + one historical file + one cost/productivity table + one method**. Date live pulls `YYYY-MM-DD`. Do not renumber steam because a Substack landed. Skip Elliott Wave. Skip “Industry 4.0” as a crest name without a payroll.

## 0. Theory and unit of analysis

**Mechanism stack (this machine):** cheap key factor → cluster of innovations → finance falls in love (installation) → bubble/turning point → socio-institutional recomposition → deployment / golden age → maturity / next irruption. Perez, *Technological Revolutions and Financial Capital* (Edward Elgar, 2002). Site: https://carlotaperez.org/ · ch.1 PDF https://carlotaperez.org/wp-content/downloads/books/btr-en/PEREZ_TRFC_Ch%201.pdf · Elgar https://www.e-elgar.com/shop/gbp/technological-revolutions-and-financial-capital-9781843763314.html

**Unit of analysis for every numbered surge below** (not Lasswell acts — that lock is for contention. This lock is for **cheapness and payroll**):

1. **Key factor that got cheap** — energy, transport, materials, compute, tokens
2. **Carrier branch** — the industries that eat first (cotton, rail, steel/electrical, auto/oil, ICT)
3. **Infrastructure that remains after the crash** — canals, track, grid, roads, fiber/cloud
4. **Finance form** — canal companies, rail shares, trusts, Detroit paper, VC/NASDAQ
5. **Date** — Perez big-bang year + installation vs deployment as-of + provisional vs final
6. **Who eats** — new firms, old firms rebuilt, labor share, the state as demand
7. **Receipt** — **history** (monograph, archive, artifact) **and** **table** (prices, TFP, energy, employment). Pew without Mokyr is a dashboard. Mokyr without OWID is a sermon.
8. **Falsifier** — periodization fight (Perez vs Kondratiev dating; GPT vs “this isn’t a revolution”)
9. **Grain** — invention ≠ irruption ≠ frenzy ≠ deployment ≠ marketing name
10. **Institutions / firms / states** — not an SMO. Chandler’s firm, Hughes’s system, the central bank, the patent office.

If 1–5 will not fill, it is a story. If 1–5 fill and 7–8 will not, it is a **clean story** (hype with footnotes).

## 1. Steam / mechanization / canals (~1770s–1830s)

Perez big-bang: **1771**, Arkwright’s mill, Britain.

1. **Cheap:** water + mechanized cotton + wrought iron; factory discipline as the org form.
2. **Carrier:** cotton textiles, machine-building.
3. **Infrastructure that remains:** mills, canals, turnpikes. (Rail is surge 2.)
4. **Finance:** canal companies, country banks, merchant capital.
5. **Date:** irruption 1770s–90s; canal mania and crashes; Britain as core. Provisional until steam *locomotion* (next).
6. **Who eats:** mill owners; handloom weavers lose. State: poor law, combination laws — **contention rides this**, it does not cause it.
7. **Receipt:** *History* — Allen, *British Industrial Revolution in Global Perspective* · Mokyr · Landes · Arkwright/Cromford as artifact · British canal archive. *Table* — Crafts productivity · OWID GDP per capita https://ourworldindata.org/economic-growth · energy transition https://ourworldindata.org/energy
8. **Falsifier:** “coal did it overnight” (Wrigley: organic vs mineral energy is a **fight**). Pomeranz *Great Divergence* vs Allen high-wage Britain. If the key factor isn’t cheap **here**, the Who is China/India cotton, not Manchester.
9. **Grain:** inventions (Newcomen 1712) ≠ factory irruption. Luddism is **contention**, not the surge.
10. **Institutions:** patent, mill, canal company. Not a Twitter mob.

**Method owed:** real wages + energy prices in Britain vs the continent. Comparison: Belgium/France lag, not a morality tale.

## 2. Steam railways / coal / iron (~1830s–1870s)

Perez big-bang: **1829**, *Rocket*, Age of Steam and Railways.

1. **Cheap:** coal + steam traction + cheap iron → falling ton-mile.
2. **Carrier:** rail, coal, iron, machine shops.
3. **Infrastructure that remains:** track, stations, telegraph along the right-of-way. Crash kills the **story** (1847, 1866, 1873), not the rails.
4. **Finance:** rail shares, foreign bonds, joint-stock. Frenzy = railway mania (Britain 1840s).
5. **Date:** 1829–; national markets as the **fact**. US and continental catch-up is comparison, not a new number.
6. **Who eats:** rail capital, ironmasters; canals lose; farm prices integrate.
7. **Receipt:** *History* — Schivelbusch, *The Railway Journey* · O’Brien on rail and economic growth · Fogel, *Railroads and American Economic Growth* (**fight:** social saving) · *Table* — rail miles, freight rates · OWID https://ourworldindata.org/ · Mitchell historical stats.
8. **Falsifier:** Fogel vs Fishlow — if rail was “cheap” only as a counterfactual, the infrastructure cell still stands (the track remained). Do not confuse cliometric **social saving** with Perez **deployment**.
9. **Grain:** *Rocket* is an event. The network is the surge.
10. **Institutions:** rail corporations, land grants (US), Board of Trade. Telegraph as sister system.

**Method owed:** ton-mile series, not paintings of stations.

---

## 3. Steel / electricity / heavy engineering (~1870s–1910s)

Perez big-bang: **1875**, Carnegie Bessemer, Age of Steel, Electricity and Heavy Engineering.

1. **Cheap:** Bessemer/open-hearth steel; electrical energy at the socket; industrial chemistry.
2. **Carrier:** steel, electrical equipment, chemicals, heavy engineering, naval.
3. **Infrastructure that remains:** **the grid**. Hughes: a system, not a gadget. Crash (1890s) does not unbuild the wires.
4. **Finance:** trusts, investment banks (Morgan), German banks, Second Industrial Revolution paper.
5. **Date:** 1875–; US/Germany as core alongside Britain. Chandler’s big firm is the **org fact**.
6. **Who eats:** Carnegie, Edison/Siemens, IG Farben-line chemistry; skilled craft squeezed into the large firm.
7. **Receipt:** *History* — Hughes, *Networks of Power* · Chandler, *Scale and Scope* · Nye, *Electrifying America* · Misa, *A Nation of Steel* · Haber on chemicals. *Table* — steel output · kWh prices · electrification rates · OWID energy https://ourworldindata.org/energy
8. **Falsifier:** “Edison invented modernity.” Systems (Hughes) vs heroic inventor. If no grid, it is still installation.
9. **Grain:** light bulb ≠ electrified factory. Home electrification lags the plant — **deployment is payroll and kWh**, not a patent.
10. **Institutions:** holding companies, patent pools, municipal franchise, antitrust as the **state’s** answer (contention/hegemony adjacent — don’t mash).

**Method owed:** load curves and factory electrification dates, not Tesla memes.

## 4. Oil / auto / mass production (~1910s–1970s)

Perez big-bang: **1908**, Model T, Age of Oil, Automobile and Mass Production.

1. **Cheap:** oil calories + assembly-time of a car + (later) petrochemicals.
2. **Carrier:** auto, oil, rubber, highways, consumer durables.
3. **Infrastructure that remains:** roads, refineries, suburbs’ pipes. 1929 kills the **story** and a decade of payroll; it does not unpave America.
4. **Finance:** Detroit paper, consumer credit, then Bretton Woods / Keynesian demand as **deployment’s state**.
5. **Date:** 1908–; Fordist wage + suburb + state demand is the **deployment fact** (US golden age ~1945–70). Maturity / 1970s oil shocks = turning toward surge 5.
6. **Who eats:** Big Three + oil majors; mass-production labor **eats a wage** in deployment (this is why contention’s New Deal compact **rides** this surge).
7. **Receipt:** *History* — Flink, *The Automobile Age* · Chandler on GM/DuPont · Yergin, *The Prize* (oil politics — pair with IEA, not as gospel) · Gordon, *Rise and Fall of American Growth*. *Table* — vehicles per capita · real oil prices · BLS productivity https://www.bls.gov/ · Gordon’s TFP by era · IEA https://www.iea.org/
8. **Falsifier:** Gordon: the “special century” **ends** ~1970 — if you date mass-production deployment as forever, the TFP cell dies. Japan/Germany catch-up is comparison, not “the US invented the car” as the whole world.
9. **Grain:** Model T event ≠ Interstate deployment. “Consumer society” is a **lifestyle overlay**, not the key factor.
10. **Institutions:** multi-divisional firm, UAW as **labor institution on this stack**, highway agencies, Bretton Woods.

**Method owed:** TFP by sector 1910–1973 vs 1973–. Do not use a 2020s podcast as the periodization.

## 5. ICT / microelectronics / internet (~1970s–)

Perez big-bang: **1971**, Intel microprocessor, Age of Information and Telecommunications.

1. **Cheap:** transistors → compute per dollar; then bandwidth; then software **margins**.
2. **Carrier:** semiconductors, computers, telecom, then platforms.
3. **Infrastructure that remains:** fiber, data centers, protocols, phones. Dot-com crash (2000) killed pets.com, not TCP/IP. 2008 was Perez’s **second**, liquidity bubble on the same surge (read her double-bubble paper).
4. **Finance:** VC, NASDAQ, then FAANG cash-flow in **deployment**.
5. **Date:** installation ~1971–2000s (Solow paradox, then frenzy). Deployment: cloud, phones, platforms as the **default firm**. Still overlapping maturity while people shout surge 6.
6. **Who eats:** chip firms, platforms, cloud; routine clerical/manufacturing tasks automated; labor-share fight is **table**, not a villain class.
7. **Receipt:** *History* — Ceruzzi, *A History of Modern Computing* · Abbate, *Inventing the Internet* · Saxenian, *Regional Advantage* · Perez 2002 chs. on ICT · David 1990 dynamo/computer. *Table* — Byrne/Oulton ICT prices · BLS productivity https://www.bls.gov/ · OWID tech https://ourworldindata.org/technology-adoption · ILOSTAT https://ilostat.ilo.org/
8. **Falsifier:** Solow paradox vs later ICT-TFP (1990s US). If “the internet changed everything” in 1995, the **payroll** cell is still 2007–phones. Gordon: ICT did less than electricity/auto for living standards — **take the fight**, don’t smear it.
9. **Grain:** Intel 4004 is an event. iPhone 2007 is deployment hardware. “Digital transformation” is marketing.
10. **Institutions:** DARPA/CERN as **state irruption**, NSFNET, ICANN, platform firms, cloud regions.

**Method owed:** hedonic computer prices + employment by occupation (Autor/Acemoglu), not a TED talk. Zip2 = a firm **on this deployment**, not a crest.

## 6. Argued next (not settled): AI / green / bio

**Not a Perez-numbered sixth surge until a key factor is cheap *and* in the payroll across the productive structure.** Perez herself has treated AI as a **leap inside ICT**, not automatically a new revolution. Green and bio are **candidate clusters**. Do not smash them into one crest because a keynote did.

1. **Cheap so far:** **tokens** (inference cost down), sensors, some sequence costs (bio) — **not** robots-as-payroll, **not** grid-scale green as the universal key factor everywhere.
2. **Carrier (candidate):** foundation-model labs, cloud GPUs, maybe batteries/renewables (IEA), maybe process-bio. Three candidates, not one Who.
3. **Infrastructure that would remain after a crash:** weights + GPU clusters + data centers + (if green) wires and steel for the grid. A failed chatbot brand is pets.com.
4. **Finance:** 2020s capex/VC frenzy. This is **installation** until capex shows up as **broad productivity and jobs reconstitution**.
5. **Date:** as-of **provisional**. Live pull below. Agents = **irruption**. Control plane / stranger-openable receipts = boring **deployment** (your shop’s actual bet). World models / robots = **physical** installation, not “one more SaaS” by default.
6. **Who eats (so far):** chip vendors, hyperscalers, a few model labs. Labor: **plot** is abundance utopia or desk-death. **File** is BLS/ILO + AI Index, not a manifesto.
7. **Receipt:** *History* — this layer is thin on purpose (too new). Do **not** fake a Mokyr. Use papers as primary: Vaswani et al. 2017 https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.03762 · Kaplan et al. scaling 2020 https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.08361 · Hoffmann et al. Chinchilla https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.15556 · Perez on AI-as-ICT-leap (interviews/site) https://carlotaperez.org/ · *Table* — Stanford HAI AI Index https://aiindex.stanford.edu/ · Epoch compute https://epoch.ai/ · IEA energy-for-data-centres https://www.iea.org/ · BLS · OWID AI https://ourworldindata.org/
8. **Falsifier:** if tokens stay cheap but TFP and occupational reconstitution don’t move, you are still in **installation** (or a tool inside surge 5). If Perez is right that a revolution is a **cluster + infrastructure + common sense of production**, a chatbot wrapper is not surge 6. Gordon-style test: does living-standard growth match 1870–1970? Almost certainly not yet.
9. **Grain:** a model release is an **event**. GPU cluster buildout is **installation**. Copilot in the payroll of non-tech sectors would be the start of **deployment**. “4IR” is a crest name without a payroll — **skip**.
10. **Institutions:** labs, cloud, NIST/standards, energy regulators, export controls. Not a Grey Pope.

## Pocket terms

- **Techno-economic paradigm** — Perez: the new common sense of cheapest practice
- **Great surge** — Perez’s unit (not Kondratiev’s GDP wave)
- **Installation / deployment** — finance-led vs production-led halves
- **Turning point** — crash + institutional recomposition
- **Key factor** — the input whose falling price reorganizes production
- **GPT** — general purpose technology (Bresnahan/Trajtenberg)
- **Creative destruction** — Schumpeter
- **Solow paradox** — computers everywhere except productivity stats
- **Crash rule** — story dies; infrastructure remains
- **Clean story** — a named “revolution” with no price series and no payroll

One line: when a key factor gets cheap, finance installs it, the crash kills the story, deployment puts it in the payroll; the next slogan is not a surge.
