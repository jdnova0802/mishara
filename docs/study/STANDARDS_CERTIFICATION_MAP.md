# Standards & Certification Map

Descriptive / institutional reference. Who writes a standard, who may require it, who audits against it, who accredits the auditor — **not** a buyer’s guide. Primary sources first. Wikipedia is a finding aid. OPEN rather than invented. Pull date **2026-11-14**. Companion to the Peoples & Ethnicities Atlas and the Religion as Institution Map (`docs/study/RELIGION_AS_INSTITUTION_MAP.md`) but **not** the same field grid.

Do not mash machines. ISO’s own Geneva accounts are not a national accreditation body’s accounts and are not a private certifier’s 10-K. An ISO 9001 certificate is not issued by ISO. A CE mark is not an ISO certificate. FDA clearance is not a voluntary scheme. USDA Organic is not GLOBALG.A.P. PCAOB is not FASB (Financial Accounting Standards Board). A counterfeit UL mark is not UL’s revenue.

## How to read an entry

Every entry uses the same fixed **12-field** template (OPEN is field 12; the extra cells vs. the Religion map split “who certifies / who accredits” from “revenue”). Field labels stay in this form throughout. In any **Falsifier** line, “cell N” means field N on this list (so “cell 6” is Revenue/funding).

1. **Standard/body name** — self-designation vs. common name
2. **Governance structure** — who writes/approves the standard, how, how often it is revised
3. **Legal status** — voluntary vs. mandatory, who can require it, and where that varies by country
4. **Scope/jurisdiction** — what it actually covers and where it is enforced vs. nominal
5. **Who certifies + how certifiers are themselves accredited** — the certifier is not the standards body; name both
6. **Revenue/funding model** — membership fees, certification fees, government funding, named and sourced
7. **Defining institutional rupture** — a major revision, a scandal that triggered reform, a body losing recognition
8. **Internal subdivisions** — competing or overlapping bodies in the same space
9. **Common misconception corrected**
10. **Receipt**
11. **Falsifier**
12. **OPEN**

### International Organization for Standardization (ISO)

1. **Name:** Self: International Organization for Standardization; abbreviated **ISO** (Statutes Art. 1.1). Common: “ISO” as if it were a UN agency or a certifier.
2. **Governance:** Member-based association. **General Assembly** of members; **Council** is the core governance body (ISO Officers + **21** voting member bodies + chairs of the Policy Development Committees CASCO, COPOLCO, DEVCO — only the 21 vote) (Statutes Art. 7). **Technical Management Board (TMB)** (Chair + 15 member bodies) has delegated authority over the technical-committee structure and the ISO/IEC Directives (Art. 14). **Technical committees (TCs)** write International Standards (Art. 15). Central Secretariat in Geneva (Art. 16). Officers: President, Secretary-General, Vice-Presidents, Treasurer (Arts. 17–19). Development stages (ISO.org): proposal → working draft → committee draft (optional) → **Draft International Standard (DIS)** 12-week member vote → optional **Final Draft International Standard (FDIS)** 8-week vote → publication. DIS/FDIS approval: **two-thirds of P-members** (participating members of the TC/SC) in favor **and not more than one-quarter** of votes cast negative. Systematic review on a defined cycle (typically five years for many standards — confirm per standard).
3. **Legal status:** “A member-based association with civil personality in accordance with Article 60 et seq. of the Swiss Civil Code. It shall be not-for-profit and non-governmental.” Seat Geneva unless moved (Art. 23). ISO standards are **voluntary** unless a regulator, contract, or statute **incorporates** a given standard. One member per country (national standards body).
4. **Scope:** Object: promote standardization to facilitate exchange of goods and services (Art. 2). **ISO in figures 2024** (Jan 2025): **172** members (129 member bodies, 39 correspondent, 4 subscriber); **25,703** International Standards and standards-type documents at end-2024; **1,533** published in 2024; Central Secretariat **185** staff. https://www.iso.org/files/live/sites/isoorg/files/about%20ISO/iso_in_figures/docs/iso_in_figures_2024.pdf ISO does **not** run factories, hospitals, or banks.
5. **Who certifies:** **ISO does not perform certification or issue certificates**, and does not permit the ISO logo in connection with certification (ISO Certification page). Certification is by external **certification bodies (CBs)**. Accreditation of those CBs is a **third** layer (see next entry). CASCO (Committee on Conformity Assessment) writes the *rules for* conformity assessment (e.g. ISO/IEC 17021-1) — it still does not certify companies.
6. **Revenue:** ISO 2024 in review (kCHF): membership fees **22,646**; royalties from members selling ISO standards **14,936**; net sales **7,028**; funded activities **5,263**; **total revenue 49,873**; total expenditure **49,833**; operating result **40**; net result before funds **1,378**; **total assets 39,429**. https://www.iso.org/annual-reports/2024 Do **not** add this to UL Solutions’ $2.87 billion or to any CB’s audit-fee book.
7. **Rupture:** Founding conference London **1946** / operations **1947** (ISO’s own “since 1946” framing on iso.org/about). Modern: ISO 9001:2015 as the last major QMS rewrite (see next entry). ISO’s certification page now states that **Global Accreditation Cooperation Incorporated (Global ACI)** “has assumed the former roles of the International Accreditation Forum (IAF) and the International Laboratory Accreditation Cooperation (ILAC).” **IAF still published 2024 General Assembly resolutions** (e.g. 2024-30). Name **both**; do not treat the succession as a completed merger of every legal person.
8. **Subdivisions:** ISO ≠ **IEC** (International Electrotechnical Commission — electrotechnical, often dual-logo ISO/IEC). ≠ **ITU** (International Telecommunication Union — UN specialized agency). ≠ **CEN/CENELEC** (European regional). Member examples: ANSI (US), BSI (UK), DIN (Germany), AFNOR (France), JISC (Japan), SAC (China) — **one each**, not ISO branch offices. CASCO / COPOLCO (consumers) / DEVCO (developing countries) are ISO policy committees, not CBs.
9. **Misconception:** “We are ISO-certified” meaning ISO audited the plant. ISO says the opposite. “ISO is the UN.” It is a Swiss association of national bodies.
10. **Receipt:** Statutes PDF https://www.iso.org/files/live/sites/isoorg/files/archive/pdf/en/statutes.pdf ; iso.org/structure.html ; iso.org/stages-and-resources-for-standards-development.html ; iso.org/certification.html ; 2024 annual report and ISO in figures (URLs above).
11. **Falsifier:** If Statutes Art. 23.1 did not make ISO a Swiss Civil Code Art. 60 association, cell 3 dies. If 2024 total revenue ≠ 49,873 kCHF, cell 6 dies. If ISO.org stated that ISO issues ISO 9001 certificates, cell 5 dies.
12. **OPEN:** 2025 membership count if it moved from 172; audited statements behind the annual-report kCHF (the review page is ISO’s own, not a PDF audit pack copied here).

### ISO 9001 and the three-layer QMS stack (ISO ≠ CB ≠ AB)

1. **Name:** **ISO 9001**, *Quality management systems — Requirements* (current edition **ISO 9001:2015**, with the 2015 amendment for climate-change considerations in 2024 — confirm clause text in the ISO store, not a blog). Common: “ISO certification,” “we have ISO.”
2. **Governance:** Written by **ISO/TC 176** under TMB/Directives. Revision by the DIS/FDIS votes in the ISO entry. Systematic review can confirm, amend, or revise. A next full revision is often discussed as a 2025/26 DIS — **OPEN** publication date this pass.
3. **Legal status:** The standard is **voluntary**. It becomes de facto mandatory where a **purchaser, tender, or regulator** requires an accredited certificate. Some sector schemes (aerospace AS9100, auto IATF 16949, medical ISO 13485) **borrow** the 9001 chassis under other owners — those are **other** machines.
4. **Scope:** Requirements for a **quality management system**, not a product-safety test and not a license to sell drugs. Geographic: wherever a CB will audit; **recognition** of the certificate across borders is the IAF/Global ACI **MLA** (Multilateral Recognition Arrangement) story, not ISO’s.
5. **Three layers, named:**
   - **Layer A — standard:** ISO (does not certify).
   - **Layer B — certification body:** audits the organization against ISO 9001 and issues a certificate. Competence rules: **ISO/IEC 17021-1:2015** (management-system CBs).
   - **Layer C — accreditation body:** accredits the CB against ISO/IEC 17021-1, itself operating to **ISO/IEC 17011:2017**. National examples: **ANAB** (ANSI National Accreditation Board, US), **UKAS** (United Kingdom Accreditation Service), **DAkkS** (Germany), **COFRAC** (France). Peer recognition: historically **IAF MLA**; ISO.org now points complaints about false *accredited* claims to **Global ACI**. IAF MLA Annex 1 (end 2024) lists ISO 9001:2015 (QMS) as a Level 5 sub-scope under ISO/IEC 17021-1. https://iaf.nu/en/annex-1-scope-of-the-mla-2025/ ISO: accreditation “is not compulsory, and non-accreditation does not necessarily mean the certification body is not reputable.” Unaccredited certificates exist; they are **not** MLA-equivalent.
6. **Revenue:** ISO does not collect 9001 audit fees (ISO 2024 royalties are **standard sales**, kCHF 14,936, not audits). CB fees are private contracts — **OPEN** as a global total. AB fees (ANAB/UKAS schedules) are **another** machine — **OPEN** a 2024 integer this pass. Do not print “the ISO 9001 market is $X billion” from a consultant deck.
7. **Rupture:** **ISO 9001:2015** (superseding 9001:2008; IAF transition: 2008 certificates not valid after three years from 2015 publication). Climate amendment 2024 as a dated add-on. IAF→Global ACI succession as a **live naming rupture** (cell 5).
8. **Subdivisions:** ISO 14001 (environment), ISO 45001 (OH&S), ISO/IEC 27001 (information security), ISO 13485 (medical devices QMS) — **different** standards, often the **same** CB under different MLA sub-scopes. Sector overlays: IATF, AS9100, FSSC 22000. Competing unaccredited mills vs MLA CBs.
9. **Misconception:** “ISO certified us.” A named CB did, maybe accredited by a named AB. “An unaccredited ISO 9001 certificate is illegal.” ISO itself says accreditation is not compulsory. “IAF CertSearch is ISO’s registry.” It is an IAF (now also Global ACI-pointed) database of *accredited* certificates, incomplete by construction.
10. **Receipt:** iso.org/certification.html ; IAF MLA Annex 1; ISO/IEC 17021-1 and 17011 (purchase/ISO catalogue — titles and dates cited, full text not copied).
11. **Falsifier:** If ISO.org stated that ISO issues ISO 9001 certificates, the three-layer claim dies. If IAF Annex 1 did not list ISO 9001:2015 under ISO/IEC 17021-1, cell 5’s MLA sub-scope dies.
12. **OPEN:** Count of valid ISO 9001 certificates (ISO Survey is a **survey of reporting CBs**, not a census — do not treat a Survey integer as IAF CertSearch); ISO 9001 next-edition publication date; ANAB/UKAS 2024 fee totals.

### UL enterprise (Underwriters Laboratories → UL Solutions / ULSE / ULRI)

1. **Name:** Historical: Underwriters Laboratories / Underwriters Electrical Bureau (**1894**). Today **three** legal persons (UL Solutions 2024 Form 10-K): **UL Solutions Inc.** (TIC — testing, inspection, certification — plus software/advisory); **ULSE Inc.** (“UL Standards & Engagement”); **Underwriters Laboratories Inc.** (“UL Research Institutes”). Common: “UL listed” as if one nonprofit still did everything.
2. **Governance:** **ULSE** develops consensus standards via **technical committees**; publication after majority ballot return **and** at least **two-thirds** of voting members approve (ULSE Consensus Process Overview, May 2025 PDF). ULSE staff chairs/PMs **do not vote**; a UL Solutions representative gets **one** vote if on the TC, like any other member. ULSE accredited/audited as a standards developer by **ANSI** (American National Standards Institute), **SCC** (Standards Council of Canada), authorized by Mexico’s **DGN**. **UL Solutions**: Delaware corporation, board under Delaware law; IPO **16 April 2024** (38,870,000 Class A shares sold **by ULSE**, $28.00; company received **no** proceeds). ULRI is sole member of ULSE, which controls majority voting power of UL Solutions.
3. **Legal status:** UL *standards* are voluntary consensus documents unless a **code, OSHA rule, or purchaser** adopts them. UL *Marks* are **certification marks**. In the US, electrical products often need an **NRTL** (Nationally Recognized Testing Laboratory) mark under **OSHA 29 CFR 1910.7** — UL Solutions holds OSHA credentials (10-K). That NRTL slice is **regulatory recognition of a lab**, not ULSE’s committee.
4. **Scope:** Product safety/security/sustainability TIC in 110+ countries (10-K framing). A UL 94 flammability rating is not an ISO 9001 certificate. Canadian/Mexican adoptions follow SCC/DGN paths.
5. **Who certifies:** **UL Solutions** (and other NRTLs: Intertek, CSA, SGS, TÜV, etc.) certify products to UL *or other* standards. **ULSE writes** many of those standards. Accreditation of UL Solutions as a CB/NRTL is by **OSHA** (NRTL), and for management-system work historically by ABs — **do not** assume every UL Mark is ISO 17065 + IAF MLA. OSHA NRTL ≠ IAF MLA.
6. **Revenue:** UL Solutions **Form 10-K year ended 31 Dec 2024**: **revenue $2,870 million** (2023: $2,678 million); net income $345 million; attributable to stockholders $326 million. Segments 2024: Industrial **$1,254 million**, Consumer **$1,238 million**, Software & Advisory **$378 million**. https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1901440/000190144025000012/uls-20241231.htm ULSE 2025 annual report is a **mission PDF**, not this 10-K — do not add them. ISO’s CHF 49.9 million is **not** in this number.
7. **Rupture:** **1894** founding. **2022** enterprise split (TIC vs standards vs research) as described in UL Solutions reporting. **16 April 2024** IPO. **Gree 2016** (next fraud entry) as a *mark-misuse* rupture, not a rewrite of UL 94.
8. **Subdivisions:** ULRI / ULSE / UL Solutions. OSHA NRTL peers. IEC/ISO dual-logo vs UL standards. ETL (Intertek) as a competing mark.
9. **Misconception:** “UL is a government agency.” It is a family of private corporations/nonprofits; OSHA *recognizes* NRTLs. “UL the standards body is the same P&L as UL the certifier.” The 10-K is UL Solutions; ULSE sold the IPO shares and writes standards.
10. **Receipt:** 10-K (URL above); ulse.org consensus PDF https://ulse.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/UL_Standards__Engagement_Consensus_Process_Overview.pdf ; OSHA 29 CFR 1910.7.
11. **Falsifier:** If 2024 UL Solutions revenue ≠ $2,870 million, cell 6 dies. If the 10-K did not separate ULRI, ULSE, and UL Solutions, cell 1 dies.
12. **OPEN:** ULSE standalone 2024/25 audited revenue; current OSHA NRTL scope list snapshot.

### CE marking (EU conformity mark, not a quality certificate)

1. **Name:** **CE** marking (*Conformité Européenne*). Common: “CE certificate,” “CE certified by the EU.”
2. **Governance:** The mark is defined in **Union harmonisation legislation**. Model clauses: **Decision No 768/2008/EC** of 9 July 2008 (common framework for the marketing of products) — manufacturer draws up an **EU declaration of conformity**; CE affixed before placing on the market; followed by the **notified body’s identification number** only where that body is involved in production control (Art. R12). Accreditation/market-surveillance frame: originally **Regulation (EC) No 765/2008**, now largely **Regulation (EU) 2019/1020** on market surveillance. Product-specific **directives/regulations** (toys, machinery, medical devices, PPE, radio equipment, etc.) choose the **module** (self-assessment vs third-party).
3. **Legal status:** **Mandatory** to affix CE **only** for products covered by EU rules that require it; **prohibited** to affix CE where those rules do not apply (Your Europe). It is a **manufacturer’s claim**, enforced by **Member State market-surveillance authorities**, not by a Brussels certificate office.
4. **Scope:** Products for the EU/EEA market under listed harmonisation acts. Not a general “safe product” logo for furniture that has no CE directive. UK **UKCA** is a **post-Brexit other mark** — do not mash.
5. **Who certifies:** **No central EU body issues permission to use CE** (Your Europe). Default: manufacturer self-assesses, compiles technical file, signs DoC, affixes CE. **Notified body**: organisation **designated by an EU country** to do third-party conformity assessment when the applicable act requires it; listed in **NANDO**. Accreditation to EN ISO/IEC 17000-series is “important” for designation (Commission notified-bodies page) but designation is a **Member State legal act**, not IAF membership. Voluntary “CE certificates” from bodies **not** notified for that act are flagged by the Commission as misleading.
6. **Revenue:** Affixing CE **has no EU fee**. Notified-body fees are private. **OPEN** as a Union total. Do not treat NANDO headcount × a guessed fee as Commission revenue.
7. **Rupture:** **New Approach** (1985 Council Resolution) / **New Legislative Framework 2008** (765/2008 + 768/2008). **2019/1020** market-surveillance rewrite. Brexit/UKCA as a territorial rupture for Great Britain.
8. **Subdivisions:** Modules A–H in Decision 768/2008. Medical devices **MDR (EU) 2017/745** (notified-body-heavy) vs low-risk electronics under EMC/LVD (often self-declaration). **UKCA**, **UKNI**, Swiss **UK/CH** alignments — other marks.
9. **Misconception:** “CE means the EU tested it.” Often nobody outside the factory did. “CE is ISO.” Different legal order. “A Chinese ‘CE certificate’ from a non-notified body is the CE mark.” Your Europe: watch voluntary certificates.
10. **Receipt:** https://europa.eu/youreurope/business/product-rules-compliance/general-product-compliance/ce-marking/index_en.htm ; Decision 768/2008/EC https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/en/ALL/?uri=CELEX%3A32008D0768 ; Commission notified-bodies page; Blue Guide 2022 OJ C 247 (guidance, not the statute).
11. **Falsifier:** If Your Europe stated that a central EU office issues CE certificates, cell 5 dies. If Decision 768/2008 Art. R12 did not require the notified-body number only when that body is in production control, cell 2 dies.
12. **OPEN:** Count of NANDO notified bodies by legislation this pull; UKCA legal status for NI/GB as of 2026.

### FDA marketing authorization (non-voluntary US contrast)

1. **Name:** U.S. **Food and Drug Administration** pathways: devices — **510(k)** (premarket notification, FD&C Act §510(k), 21 CFR 807 Subpart E), **PMA** (premarket approval, §515 / 21 CFR 814), **De Novo** (§513(f)(2)); drugs — **NDA**/BLA under §505 / Public Health Service Act. Common: “FDA certified,” “FDA registered” (registration ≠ clearance).
2. **Governance:** Statute (FD&C Act) + FDA implementing regulations + user-fee commitment letters. Device user fees: **MDUFA V** (Medical Device User Fee Amendments), FY2023–FY2027, enacted **30 September 2022** as Division F of Pub. L. 117-180. Drug user fees: **PDUFA VII**, same FUFRA vehicle — **do not mash MDUFA dollars with PDUFA dollars**.
3. **Legal status:** **Prohibited** to market a non-exempt device or a new drug in the US without the applicable FDA authorization. This is **not** a voluntary ISO scheme. State law cannot generally waive it.
4. **Scope:** US market. A CE mark under MDR does **not** equal FDA clearance. FDA “clears” 510(k) devices as **substantially equivalent** to a **predicate**; it **approves** PMAs on a reasonable-assurance-of-safety-and-effectiveness standard. Those two words are not synonyms.
5. **Who certifies:** **FDA** issues the order. Optional **Third Party Review** for *certain* 510(k)s: a private “Recognized Third Party” reviews a file, **FDA still issues the clearance** (FDA 510(k) page). Facility registration and listing are **other** 21 CFR 807 duties, not a 510(k). ISO 13485 certificates from a CB are **not** FDA marketing authorization (they may be used in MDSAP — **another** machine).
6. **Revenue:** FY2025 MDUFA Performance Report (data as of 30 Sept 2025): FDA **net collections of $420 million** in medical device user fees in FY2025 (up $74 million vs FY2024). https://www.fda.gov/media/191127/download Detailed MDUFA financial report is cited there — **OPEN** the line-by-line appropriation split. PDUFA collections: **OPEN** this pass (separate report). Do not add $420 million to ISO’s CHF or UL’s $2.87 billion as “certification industry GDP.”
7. **Rupture:** **Medical Device Amendments of 1976** (28 May 1976) — 510(k)/classifications. **MDUFMA 2002** (first device user fees). **MDUFA V / PDUFA VII 2022**. Opioid/accelerated-approval controversies are **drug-center** ruptures, not device 510(k) law.
8. **Subdivisions:** CDRH (devices) vs CDER (drugs) vs CBER (biologics) vs CFSAN (food) — **do not** file infant formula as a 510(k). 510(k) vs PMA vs De Novo vs HDE vs EUA (emergency use — time-limited).
9. **Misconception:** “FDA certified like ISO 9001.” FDA is a regulator; ISO 9001 is a voluntary QMS. “Cleared means approved.” FDA’s own 510(k) page uses **clears**. “FDA registered” on Amazon is not a 510(k).
10. **Receipt:** https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/premarket-submissions-selecting-and-preparing-correct-submission/premarket-notification-510k ; FY2025 MDUFA Performance Report; Pub. L. 117-180 Division F.
11. **Falsifier:** If a 510(k) SE order were a PMA approval as a matter of statute, cell 4 dies. If FY2025 MDUFA net collections ≠ $420 million, cell 6 dies.
12. **OPEN:** FY2025 PDUFA net collections; PMA user-fee table FY2026; count of 510(k) clearances FY2025 from the same report if not copied here.

### USDA National Organic Program (NOP)

1. **Name:** **National Organic Program**, USDA Agricultural Marketing Service (AMS), regulations at **7 CFR Part 205**, under the **Organic Foods Production Act of 1990** (OFPA, 7 U.S.C. § 6501 et seq.). Common: “USDA Organic,” “certified organic” as if USDA inspected every farm.
2. **Governance:** AMS/NOP writes and enforces 7 CFR 205; **National Organic Standards Board (NOSB)** advises (OFPA). **Strengthening Organic Enforcement** final rule, 88 FR 3548 (**19 January 2023**), phased implementation (many provisions **19 March 2024**).
3. **Legal status:** **Mandatory** if you sell, label, or represent product as “100 percent organic,” “organic,” or “made with organic…” in the US, with listed exemptions (§205.100 / §205.101). The USDA organic **seal** is a certification mark of the Department.
4. **Scope:** Crops, livestock, wild crops, handling. Foreign operations may be certified by USDA-accredited agents **or** via **recognition/equivalency** (EU, Canada, etc.) — those are **other** legal paths. GLOBALG.A.P. is **not** this seal.
5. **Who certifies:** **Accredited certifying agents** (private or governmental) accredited by the **Secretary/AMS** for **five years** (§205.500). USDA **does not** typically inspect the farm; the certifier does (annual inspection + unannounced). Accreditation procedure: 7 CFR 205 Subpart F. Noncompliance/suspension/revocation of **certifiers**: **§205.665**. Revoked certifier ineligible for **not less than 3 years** (§205.665(g)(2)).
6. **Revenue:** Applicant certifier pays fees under **§205.640** (application includes a **$500** piece plus cost-recovery — AMS “Becoming a Certifying Agent”). Farm-level certification **fees are set by each certifier** (must file a fee schedule). **OPEN** NOP program-level appropriation vs fee total this pass. Do not use a trade-association “organic market size” as NOP’s budget.
7. **Rupture:** OFPA **1990**; 7 CFR 205 full program **2000–2002**. **SOE 2023**. Named certifier enforcement (cell 8 / fraud entry): **Texas Department of Agriculture** — AMS Administrator’s Decision denying appeal; **accreditation suspended in its entirety** (livestock/crops/handling) after 25 Feb 2022 proposed suspension (APL-037-22). https://www.ams.usda.gov/sites/default/files/media/NOPDecision_TDA_APL03722_Signed.pdf **Control Union Certifications–Turkey office** suspended **9 May 2019–9 June 2020** (NOP AP146). Historical AMS list (1 July 2015): **American Food Safety Institute, International** revoked **26 July 2006**; **California Crop Improvement Association** revoked **24 Nov 2008**. https://www.ams.usda.gov/sites/default/files/media/OrganicSusRevACAs07012015.pdf
8. **Subdivisions:** USDA-accredited agents vs **State Organic Programs** vs equivalency-recognized foreign systems. Private labels (QAI, CCOF, Oregon Tilth) are **certifiers under NOP**, not competing federal programs — unless they also run a **non-NOP** private standard (then name both).
9. **Misconception:** “USDA inspected this apple.” A named certifying agent did, under NOP accreditation. “Organic worldwide is one seal.” EU 2018/848 and JAS are **other** statutes.
10. **Receipt:** 7 CFR 205 https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-7/subtitle-B/chapter-I/subchapter-M/part-205 ; SOE 88 FR 3548; TDA decision PDF; AMS revoked-list PDF.
11. **Falsifier:** If §205.500 did not make AMS the accreditor of certifying agents, cell 5 dies. If the TDA Administrator’s Decision did not suspend TDA’s NOP accreditation in its entirety, the named-enforcement clause dies.
12. **OPEN:** Current Organic INTEGRITY Database certifier count; NOP FY2025 budget line.

### GLOBALG.A.P. (private farm-assurance scheme)

1. **Name:** **GLOBALG.A.P.**, scheme operated by **FoodPLUS GmbH** (Cologne); IAF 2025 endorsement agreement also names **Agraya GmbH**. Flagship standard: **Integrated Farm Assurance (IFA) version 6** (Smart and GFS editions). Common: “Global GAP,” “the supermarket’s ISO.”
2. **Governance:** GLOBALG.A.P. Secretariat / FoodPLUS sets **General Regulations** and **principles and criteria (P&Cs)**. IFA v6 launched late **2022**; **replaced IFA v5.2 on 1 January 2024** for fruit/vegetables, flowers/ornamentals, hops, aquaculture (GLOBALG.A.P. news on IAF endorsement). **IAF–FoodPLUS MoU**, GA resolution **2024-30** (Berlin): IFA endorsed as IAF MLA **sub-scope** under product certification **ISO/IEC 17065** — Level 4 General Regulations v6; Level 5 IFA (SMART and GFS) P&Cs v6. https://iaf.nu/resolutions/iaf-and-foodplus-operated-globalg-a-p-mou/
3. **Legal status:** **Private**. Mandatory only where a **buyer** (retailer, importer) or a government tender requires it. Not a substitute for USDA Organic or for EU official controls.
4. **Scope:** Primary production (plants from pre-planting to unprocessed product; aquaculture hatchery through postharvest by the same legal entity). **No processing/manufacturing** (GR Rules for CBs). IFA v6 GFS food-safety P&Cs are **GFSI-recognized** (Global Food Safety Initiative — a **consumer-goods-forum** benchmarking machine, not IAF).
5. **Who certifies:** Approved **certification bodies** accredited to **ISO/IEC 17065** by an AB that (GLOBALG.A.P. accreditation explainer): is an **IAF member**, is an **MLA signatory for product certification**, and has an **MoU with FoodPLUS**. FoodPLUS **approves** the CB (license + annual CB license fee) **on top of** AB accreditation — two gates.
6. **Revenue:** Producer certificate fees + CB license fees per GLOBALG.A.P. **fee table** (normative; GR requires payment). **OPEN** the euro line-items this pass (fee table is a living PDF). FoodPLUS/Agraya GmbH German company accounts exist — **OPEN** the 2024 Umsatz integer.
7. **Rupture:** EurepGAP → GLOBALG.A.P. rebrand (2007, scheme history). **IFA v6 / 1 Jan 2024** cutoff. **15 July 2024** IAF scheme-endorsement agreement; **16 July 2025** IAF–Agraya replacement agreement.
8. **Subdivisions:** IFA Smart vs IFA GFS; PHA (Produce Handling Assurance); CoC (Chain of Custody); CFM (feed); HPSS. Competing: **SQF**, **BRCGS**, **FSSC 22000**, national schemes benchmarked to GLOBALG.A.P. USDA GAP/GHP is **AMS**, not FoodPLUS.
9. **Misconception:** “GLOBALG.A.P. is ISO.” It is a private scheme that **uses** ISO/IEC 17065 CBs. “GFSI certification” is not a certificate — GFSI **recognizes schemes**.
10. **Receipt:** GR Rules for CBs v6.0 Apr 2025 https://documents.globalgap.org/documents/250401_GG_GR_Rules_for_CBs_v6_0_Apr25_en.pdf ; IAF resolution 2024-30; globalgap.org IAF endorsement news.
11. **Falsifier:** If IAF 2024-30 did not endorse IFA v6 under ISO/IEC 17065, cell 2 dies. If the GR covered manufacturing plants as in-scope IFA, cell 4 dies.

### Public Company Accounting Oversight Board (PCAOB)

1. **Name:** **Public Company Accounting Oversight Board**. Common: “the audit police,” “GAAP.” PCAOB does **not** write GAAP (that is **FASB** under the Financial Accounting Foundation).
2. **Governance:** Nonprofit corporation created by **Sarbanes-Oxley Act of 2002** (Pub. L. 107-204, **30 July 2002**), **§101**. Five Board members, including the Chair, appointed to staggered **five-year** terms by the **SEC**, after consultation with the Fed Chair and Treasury Secretary (pcaobus.org/about). Duties: register firms; set/adopt auditing, QC, ethics, independence standards; inspect; investigate and discipline. SEC approves PCAOB rules, standards, and **budget**.
3. **Legal status:** SOX makes PCAOB registration **mandatory** for firms that prepare audit reports for **issuers** and (post-Dodd-Frank) SEC-registered **broker-dealers**. Not ISO 9001. Not a federal executive-branch agency in the APA sense — SOX designed a **nonprofit** under SEC oversight.
4. **Scope:** Audits of issuers and broker-dealers. Does **not** set IFRS (IASB/IFRS Foundation) or US GAAP (FASB). Non-issuer private-company audits remain primarily **AICPA** / state boards — **other** machines.
5. **Who certifies:** PCAOB **registers and inspects** firms; it does **not** “ISO-certify” them. The firm’s **audit opinion** is the firm’s, under PCAOB standards. Discipline: PCAOB enforcement orders (SEC may review).
6. **Revenue:** SOX **§109** **accounting support fee**, allocated to issuers and broker-dealers, plus registration/annual fees credited to the next year. **SEC** (18 Dec 2024, press 2024-204): **2025 budget $399.7 million**; **accounting support fee $374.9 million** ($346.1 million issuers, $28.8 million broker-dealers). FR order 23 Dec 2024. 2024 budget PDF: ASF **$358.787 million** ($331.017 issuers, $27.770 broker-dealers). https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2024-204
7. **Rupture:** **30 July 2002** SOX (Enron/WorldCom). **Dodd-Frank 2010** broker-dealer audits. **2019** KPMG inspections-cheating enforcement (PCAOB Release **105-2019-003** and related SEC actions) as a dated integrity rupture — the Board inspects the inspectors’ targets, and those targets tried to game the inspection. **2024–25** SEC budget approvals as the live funding machine.
8. **Subdivisions:** PCAOB ≠ FASB ≠ GASB ≠ IASB ≠ IAASB (international *audit* standards, used outside PCAOB-land). State boards of accountancy. EU CSSF/CEAOB equivalents — **do not** mash.
9. **Misconception:** “PCAOB writes GAAP.” It writes **auditing** standards for issuer audits. “Big Four are ISO 9001 therefore PCAOB-compliant.” Different statutes.
10. **Receipt:** pcaobus.org/about ; SEC 2024-204; 2024 PCAOB budget PDF https://assets.pcaobus.org/pcaob-dev/docs/default-source/about/administration/documents/fiscal_year_budgets/2024-budget.pdf ; 15 U.S.C. § 7211 / § 7219.
11. **Falsifier:** If the 2025 SEC-approved budget ≠ $399.7 million, cell 6 dies. If SOX §101 did not create a five-member Board appointed by the SEC, cell 2 dies.
12. **OPEN:** 2025 audited actuals vs budget; foreign-firm inspection backlog integers.

### Misused marks and failed certifiers (named enforcement, not a hypothetical mill)

1. **Name:** Not a standards body. This entry collects **dated, primary enforcement** against (a) **false use of a certification mark**, and (b) **a certifier losing its accreditation**. Common: “fake ISO mill” as an unsourced urban legend.
2. **Governance:** Enforcement sits with **whatever statute owns the mark or the accreditation**: CPSA (UL mark as a registered certification mark), criminal counterfeiting (18 U.S.C. trademark/counterfeit goods), NOP §205.665 (organic certifiers).
3. **Legal status:** 15 U.S.C. **§ 2068(a)(12)** (CPSA) prohibits unauthorized use of a **registered safety certification mark**. FTC **16 CFR § 260.6**: deceptive to misrepresent third-party certification. USDA organic: selling as organic without a certifying agent is a separate OFPA offense.
4. **Scope:** US illustrations below. EU NANDO withdrawals and UKAS CB withdrawals are **other** dockets — OPEN as integers this pass.
5. **Who “certifies”:** In these cases the **mark was false** or the **certifier was stood down**. The standards body (UL, USDA, ISO) is **not** the defendant unless named.
6. **Revenue:** Penalties are **not** the certifier’s business model. Figures below are **orders**, not UL’s 10-K.
7. **Rupture / named actions:**
   - **CPSC–Gree, 25 March 2016** (release; settlement): Gree Electric Appliances (Zhuhai), Hong Kong Gree, Gree USA Sales agreed to pay **$15.45 million**. Staff charges included selling dehumidifiers **bearing the UL mark knowing they did not meet UL flammability standards**, plus failure to report and misrepresentations to CPSC. Settlement Agreement ¶¶11–23: knowing violation of CPSA §19(a)(12), 15 U.S.C. § 2068(a)(12); civil penalty **US$15,450,000**. https://www.cpsc.gov/Newsroom/News-Releases/2016/Gree-Agrees-to-Pay-Record-1545-Million-Civil-Penalty-Improve-Internal-Compliance-for-Failure-to-Report-Defective-Dehumidifiers ; https://www.cpsc.gov/s3fs-public/GreeCivilPenalty.pdf
   - **ICE HSI / C.D. Cal. indictment, 19 December 2019**: Zoulin Cai / Shenzhen Theseus Technology — alleged **$23.8 million** scheme; batteries bore **counterfeit UL certification marks** plus counterfeit OEM trademarks. ICE: https://www.ice.gov/news/releases/ice-hsi-arrests-chinese-national-238-million-scheme-sell-counterfeit-laptop-computer (indictment allegations, not a verdict).
   - **USDA-AMS, Texas Department of Agriculture**: Administrator’s Decision (APL-037-22) **denies appeal**; TDA’s NOP accreditation **suspended in its entirety** (must cease all certification; 1-year suspension then possible reinstatement petition). https://www.ams.usda.gov/sites/default/files/media/NOPDecision_TDA_APL03722_Signed.pdf This is **certifier failure**, not a fake UL hologram — still a named accreditation loss.
8. **Subdivisions:** Counterfeit **product marks** (Gree, Theseus) vs **accreditation revocation/suspension of a CB/certifying agent** (TDA, CUC-Turkey, AFSI 2006). Do not file KPMG’s 2019 PCAOB cheating here as a “fake ISO mill.”
9. **Misconception:** “No one ever gets caught for fake CE/UL/ISO.” CPSC and ICE dockets exist. “A suspended organic certifier means the farms were fake.” It means the **agent** failed §205.665; operations must **transfer**.
10. **Receipt:** CPSC release + GreeCivilPenalty.pdf; ICE 2019 release; AMS TDA PDF; 15 U.S.C. § 2068(a)(12).
11. **Falsifier:** If the CPSC settlement did not include a §19(a)(12) UL-mark count, the Gree clause dies. If the TDA decision did not suspend accreditation in its entirety, that clause dies. If the ICE release did not allege counterfeit UL marks, the Theseus clause dies.
12. **OPEN:** Cai trial/plea outcome after the 2019 indictment; Gree 2021 corporate guilty plea docket caption as a separate criminal machine (CPSC commissioner statement 17 Nov 2023) — full DOJ judgment not copied this pass.
