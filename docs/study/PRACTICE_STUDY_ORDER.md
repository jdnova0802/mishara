# Practice Curriculum — Plain English

Formal name of this *kind* of document: a practice curriculum (Sanskrit *sādhana*: a chosen, repeated discipline toward a defined end).

Academic placement: history of religions / comparative mysticism. The four families below are real. “Dual Throne Protocol” is a private working title, not a school, degree, lineage, or field. Do not file this note under that name.

(What it is: one historical tradition per function — wealth, awareness, dissolution, openings, love, protection, death, purification, and a map of ascent — in the traditions’ own names. The public household form is distinguished from initiation.)

The one sentence: each function is owned by a named tradition with texts and living practice. A private stack may sequence them. It cannot rename them, merge their authorities, or assign a job the source tradition does not give.

---

## 1. WHAT THIS CURRICULUM IS

A chosen, repeated discipline — not a brand, and not a reading list you never sit down for. (Sanskrit name: *sādhana*.)

Family — The historical religion or current that holds the practice: Hindu Śaiva / Śākta / Vaiṣṇava, tantric Tibetan Buddhism (Vajrayāna), Jewish Kabbalah, or Lucumí (the Cuban–United States form of Yoruba orisha religion).

The public form — What a householder already does without initiation. A Friday lamp for Lakṣmī is this.

The initiatory form — What requires a teacher, an empowerment, or a house: consciousness-transference at death (*phowa*); Lucumí priesthood (*asiento*); Śrīvidyā initiation (*dīkṣā*). This curriculum uses public forms until a real teacher exists. It does not fake the rest.

Hindu worship at an image — Seat, light, water or food, a name. The ordinary engine of the Hindu seats below. (Sanskrit: *pūjā*.)

The devotional-love path to a deity (called *bhakti*) — Distinct from the dissolution path and from the wealth function. Especially Vaiṣṇava.

Tantra, in this note — Hindu and Buddhist systems that use deity, mantra, and the body as the path, without requiring world-renunciation. Not a synonym for “edgy occult.”

The tantric vehicle of Tibetan Buddhism (Vajrayāna) — Holds the purification deity Vajrasattva and the death practices below.

Jewish mysticism (Kabbalah) — Holds the Tree of Life as a map of emanation and ascent. Not Hermetic ceremonial magic.

Lucumí / Santería — Orisha religion as organized in Cuba and the United States. Holds Èṣù / Elegguá. Upheld as a religion in *Church of the Lukumi Babalu Aye v. City of Hialeah*, 508 U.S. 520 (1993).

Wicca — The formal name of contemporary Pagan witchcraft as a religion in United States case law (*Dettmer v. Landon*, 799 F.2d 929 (4th Cir. 1986)). Academic field: Pagan studies. Not this curriculum.

Witchcraft as an English statute — A historical crime, later a fraud and consumer offence. Not a deity. Not Lakṣmī.

## 2. THE CURRICULUM (one tradition per function)

Wealth — Lakṣmī (also Śrī). Hindu. Inner current: Śrīvidyā. Public form: household worship of Lakṣmī, especially Friday (Śukravāra).

Awareness as ground — Śiva in the Trika / Kashmir Śaiva system. Hindu Śaiva. Public form: recollection and a *liṅga*; not a petition list.

Dissolution of false identity — Kālī in the Kālīkula / ten-wisdom-goddess current (Mahāvidyā). Hindu Śākta.

Removal of obstacles / beginning a work — Gaṇeśa (Vighneśvara). Hindu. Invoked first.

Choice of path / messenger at the crossroads — Èṣù (Yoruba) / Elegguá (Lucumí). Not Gaṇeśa. This is an initiatory house, not a candle color.

Love — Rādhā–Kṛṣṇa. Hindu Vaiṣṇava devotion. Not Lakṣmī’s job.

Protection — Durgā. Primary text: *Devī Māhātmya* (*Durgā Saptaśatī*) in the *Mārkaṇḍeya Purāṇa*. Not Kālī’s job.

Death and the interval after death — The intermediate state (*bar do*) and consciousness transference (*pho ba* / *phowa*). Tantric Tibetan Buddhism, Nyingma or Kagyu. Replaces any use of Thoth as a death-path.

Purification of residual karma — Vajrasattva, especially the hundred-syllable mantra in the preliminary practices (*ngöndro*). Tantric Tibetan Buddhism.

Map of levels / ascent — Kabbalah: *Sefer Yetzirah*, *Sefer ha-Bahir*, *Zohar*, Lurianic Kabbalah. Ten emanations (sefirot) from the infinite (Ein Sof) to the kingdom of this world (Malkhut). Dion Fortune, *The Mystical Qabalah* (1935), is an English exposition, not a source text. Hermetic “Qabalah” of the Golden Dawn (Isis-Urania, 1 March 1888) and Ordo Templi Orientis is a different institution.

Two aims this curriculum may hold at once, without inventing a new religion:

- Full engagement with livelihood and resources (Lakṣmī’s domain), without taking them as the self (Śiva).
- Preparation for death as recognition rather than shock (Kālī’s work in life; the intermediate-state and transference practices as the death-training).

## 3. EACH TRADITION, STATED IN ENGLISH FIRST

Lakṣmī / Śrī — The Hindu goddess of prosperity, auspiciousness, and right order. Marks: lotus, gold, rice (*akṣata*), lamp, cleanliness. Primary hymn: *Śrī Sūkta* (a *khila* associated with the Ṛgveda). It asks for Śrī. It asks for the removal of Alakṣmī. *Lakṣmī Tantra* is a Pāñcarātra text of the inner current. Kubera is a treasurer-yakṣa, not a substitute for Lakṣmī. Salt is not a canonical Lakṣmī attribute. Some regional Dīpāvalī customs honor salt. Others avoid buying it near Dhanteras by association with Alakṣmī. Neither custom is *Śrī Sūkta*. Do not import a Hoodoo salt-bowl into this seat.

Alakṣmī — The named inauspicious counterpart (poverty, filth, quarrel). The broom on Dīpāvalī in some houses is a folk enactment of driving her out. Cleanliness is the actual condition, not a metaphor.

Trika / Kashmir Śaivism — A Śaiva non-dual system in which consciousness (Śiva) is the ground of appearance, not an object of bargaining. Root works: *Śiva Sūtras*; *Spanda Kārikās*; the recognition school (Pratyabhijñā) of Utpaladeva and Abhinavagupta. A small *liṅga* is the ordinary aniconic support. This is not gymnastic “yoga.” It is not Śaṅkara’s Advaita Vedānta (a different Hindu system). It is not rDzogs-chen (a Tibetan parallel of similar depth, a different seat).

The Kālī-centered Śākta current (Kālīkula) — Includes the ten-wisdom-goddess frame (Mahāvidyā). Function here: time, the cutting of identification, rehearsal of death while alive. The image faces the practitioner. This is not Durgā (the protective mother of the *Devī Māhātmya*). It is not a costume Kālī. It is not gCod / Chöd, which is Tibetan.

Gaṇeśa — Remover of obstacles, patron of beginnings. Invoked before other Hindu work. Not the Yoruba crossroads orisha. Courtright’s *Gaṇeśa* (1985) is a modern monograph, not scripture.

Èṣù / Elegguá — Orisha of the threshold, speech, and the opening or closing of roads. In Lucumí he is greeted first among the orisha. Ifá is the parent divinatory and intellectual system in Yorubaland. A red-and-black candle is not initiation. Lucumí is not Wicca, Hoodoo, Haitian Vodou, or Palo.

Rādhā–Kṛṣṇa — The Vaiṣṇava form of divine love. Texts: *Bhāgavata Purāṇa*; Jayadeva’s *Gīta Govinda*; living congregational singing (*kīrtana*). This function was not supplied by Lakṣmī-as-fortune. Oṣun belongs only if Lucumí is already the house. Do not add a second orisha onto a Hindu Friday.

Durgā — Protective goddess who meets the asura in the *Devī Māhātmya*. Public form: Durgā Pūjā / Navarātri. Hanumān is another Hindu protective current and is not used here, to keep one subject per function.

The intermediate state and consciousness transference (*bar do* / *phowa*) — Tibetan training for dying and the post-death interval. The English “Tibetan Book of the Dead” refers to *Bar do thos grol* cycles. Transference (*phowa*) is transmitted. Reading a translation is study. Performing transference without authorization is not this curriculum. Egyptian funerary books (Amduat; the Book of Coming Forth by Day) are a separate historical corpus. They may be studied. They are not this function.

Thoth (Egyptian Ḏḥwty) — Scribe of the gods, associated with writing, the moon, and recording the weighing of the heart. He is not the Egyptian underworld path (Osiris, the Amduat, Anubis as psychopomp). Greco-Egyptian Hermes-Thoth and Crowley’s *Book of Thoth* are later receptions. Not practiced here as death-navigation.

Vajrasattva — The purification deity in tantric Tibetan Buddhism. The hundred-syllable mantra is the ordinary preliminary-practice form. A painted scroll (thangka) is the traditional support. Jewish return and repair (*teshuvah*) is an analogue in another family, not this seat.

Kabbalah — Jewish esoteric teaching of emanation and return. Ten emanations (sefirot), twenty-two paths, the middle line between loving-kindness (*ḥesed*) and severity (*gevurah*), and the husks (qlippoth). Study texts in that order of seriousness: *Sefer Yetzirah*; *Bahir*; *Zohar* (Pritzker English if needed); Luria. Fortune is a crib for English readers. Golden Dawn path-working is another institution.

## 4. RELATIONSHIP AMONG THE SEATS

Sequence, not fusion — Hindu work may begin with Gaṇeśa’s name, then Lakṣmī on her day. Èṣù is not placed on Lakṣmī’s cloth. Kālī is not used as a wealth-goddess. Durgā is not used as a dissolving blade. Kabbalah is a diagram you study, not a Friday rite on the same cloth.

Public before initiatory — Friday Lakṣmī worship requires no guru. Consciousness transference (*phowa*) and Lucumí priesthood do. Śrīvidyā initiation is not implied by owning a Lakṣmī image.

Two “door” functions — Gaṇeśa removes obstacles so a work can start. Èṣù governs which road opens. English “door” is one word. The jobs are not.

Two “death” functions — Kālī cuts identification while you are alive. The intermediate-state and transference practices train the actual dying. Thoth is neither.

## 5. LAKṢMĪ — THE ONLY PRACTICE IN FORCE

Support — A four-armed Lakṣmī image (*mūrti*) on a dedicated surface. A sculpted mound of coins at the feet is ornament, not an offering vessel.

Required implements — A red or yellow cloth as the seat and garment (*āsana* / *vastra*); a ghee lamp (ghee, wick, fire); one circulating coin of real currency as an offering of value; a lighter. Optional: a 108-bead counting garland (*mālā*).

Not required — Salt; additional deities; crystals; black candles; a second coin-bowl.

Procedure (Friday evening, and optionally a short daily form):

1. Wash the hands. Wipe the image and the surface. Spread the cloth. Seat her on it.
2. Place one real coin on the cloth in front of her.
3. Light the ghee lamp.
4. Optionally salute Gaṇeśa by name, even without his image. Then recite: *Om Śrīṃ Mahālakṣmyai Namaḥ* — 108 times with a counting garland, or 11 without.
5. Remain seated. The condition of the practice is cleanliness and thanks, which is also the condition named in the Śrī literature for Śrī remaining.
6. Extinguish the lamp, or let a tea-light burn out. If food was offered, it is eaten afterward and not left to spoil.

This is ordinary Hindu household worship. It is not a temple installation (*pratiṣṭhā*). It is not a sixteen-service priestly liturgy. It is not Śrīvidyā initiation. The seed-syllable *Śrīṃ* is Lakṣmī’s. The name-mantra above is the standard householder form.

## 6. SUPPORTS FOR LATER SEATS (not to be purchased as a set)

Gaṇeśa — Name first; image later.

Śiva — A small *liṅga*.

Kālī — An image that faces you, on her own cloth.

Counting garland — 108 beads, when counting becomes regular.

Kabbalah — A notebook used only for that study; Fortune as English on-ramp; then *Zohar* and *Sefer Yetzirah*; a Tree of Life diagram used as a worksheet, not as Lakṣmī’s backdrop.

Vajrasattva — A painted scroll (thangka), and a transmitted mantra.

Èṣù — Only inside instruction from a Lucumí house. Color-symbolism from a private note is not authorization.

Death-training — A translation of *Bar do thos grol* as study; consciousness transference (*phowa*) only from a holder of that transmission.

Timer — A dedicated bell, if the phone is a distraction. Not a requirement of Lakṣmī worship.

Incense — Optional. Sandalwood is conventional for Lakṣmī. Frankincense is not required.

## 7. BODY DIAGRAM (if a single garment is later used as a mnemonic)

Constraint — No element should reverse-image-search to a named deity.

Left arm — Navigation: a curve (Gaṇeśa), a crossing of two lines at a style-break (Èṣù), a single vertical (Śiva), none of them iconic.

Chest — Lakṣmī as an eight-pointed star collapsed into other geometry; ten unmarked points in the places of the ten emanations (sefirot); the middle line of the Tree along the sternum.

Back — Kālī as a multi-armed seated form in dark lotus geometry; a four-fold vajra-shape at the sacrum (Vajrasattva); an eye with a horizontal line at the nape may remain as design. It does not reinstate Thoth as the death-practice.

## HOW A CASE RUNS

1. Name the function (wealth, obstacle, crossroads, love, protection, death, purification, ascent) and whether the claim is a map, a study-text, or a practice.
2. Name the family in its own language (Śākta, Trika, Vaiṣṇava, Vajrayāna, Kabbalah, Lucumí). If the only name you have is a private stack-title, you do not yet have a subject.
3. Ask whether that family actually assigns this function. If not, the assignment is decorative.
4. Ask whether the form you are about to do is public household practice or an initiatory act. Do not perform the second as if it were the first.
5. If the word “witchcraft” appears, specify: English criminal statute, Wicca, census write-in, commercial spell-market, or a mislabel of an African-diaspora house. Those are different institutions.
6. Summary form: “This is a [map / study / public worship / initiatory] claim; the subject is [formal name]; the family is [family]; the function it does not have is [wrong job].”

## BOUNDARY NOTE

This curriculum does not confer initiation (*dīkṣā*), Lucumí priesthood, Vajrayāna empowerment, or rabbinic standing in Kabbalah.

It does not replace *Śrī Sūkta*, the *Zohar*, *Devī Māhātmya*, or *Bar do thos grol* when those texts are the point.

It does not substitute for livelihood, accounting, or political-economy study. Those remain maps. Lakṣmī remains the wealth-deity of this curriculum.

It does not authorize a single mixed rite in which every family is invoked at once.

Commercial “manifestation,” salt bowls, and one-altar-for-all-gods packaging are retail. They are not Śrīvidyā, not Kabbalah, and not Lucumí.

## BEST STARTERS

Lakṣmī (now) — The image already in hand; cloth; ghee lamp; one coin; *Om Śrīṃ Mahālakṣmyai Namaḥ*. *Śrī Sūkta* when a hymn is wanted.

Śiva — *Śiva Sūtras*, with a Trika commentary, not a poster.

Kālī — A facing image; the ten-wisdom-goddess / Kālīkula current as the named current.

Durgā — *Devī Māhātmya*.

Rādhā–Kṛṣṇa — *Bhāgavata Purāṇa*; congregational singing (*kīrtana*).

Gaṇeśa — The first-invocation custom; an image when one is actually used.

Death — *Bar do thos grol* as a book; consciousness transference (*phowa*) only from a teacher.

Vajrasattva — The hundred-syllable mantra as transmitted.

Kabbalah — Fortune only to learn English names of the ten emanations (sefirot); then *Sefer Yetzirah* and the Pritzker *Zohar*.

Èṣù — 508 U.S. 520 for the United States legal fact of the religion; practice only in a house.

Wicca, as a different subject — Pagan studies and the institution-map of crime, church, census, and consumer law. Not this curriculum.
