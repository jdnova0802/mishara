# Medium / attention waves

**Not cycles of contention. Not a tweet. Not “the algorithm radicalized me” as the whole file. Not Nisaba.**

**Subject:** *medium theory* (Innis, McLuhan) + **platform attention cycles**. Which plots **clip**. The same movement looks different on pulpit vs For You. This file is the **pipe**, not the claim and not the table of states.

**Job of this file:** who still posts, who still watches, what the ranker **predicts**, which animals the apps actually are. Casual users leave; polarized partisans keep posting.

**Plot leak:** ranker as **sole** cause. **Stricter file:** sorting + who still posts + predicted watch.

A dissertation is **one numbered medium + one periodization fight + one historical file + one DNR/Pew/ANES table + one method**. Date live pulls `YYYY-MM-DD`. Skip one viral week as a new crest. Update each new DNR (annual) + Pew platform notes. Change Live lines only.

## 0. Theory and unit of analysis

**Mechanism stack (this machine):** a medium cheapens some **acts of attention** (copy, reach, reply, watch) → new gatekeepers (priest, printer, editor, scheduler, ranker) → publics form **in that grain** → old publics look like “the news” only from the previous medium. Innis: space- vs time-biased media. McLuhan: the medium is the environment, not a moral. Later: two-step flow (Katz/Lazarsfeld) → broadcast effects tradition → selective exposure → **platforms** (graph) → **For You** (predicted watch, graph optional).

**Unit of analysis for every numbered crest below** (not Lasswell **acts of claim-making**. This lock is for **attention**):

1. **Who holds the file** — priest, printer, editor, scheduler, ranker, creator
2. **What is cheap** — copy, simultaneous reach, reply, clip, predicted watch
3. **Public grain** — parish, republic of letters, national evening, niche slot, graph, For You
4. **Who still shows up** — casuals leave; partisans and professionals remain
5. **Date** — introduction vs **majority use** vs residual (radio didn’t die)
6. **Where / language** — DNR is many markets; ANES is US. Don’t globalize a US app.
7. **Receipt** — **history** (Innis, Starr, Wu, Darnton) **and** **table** (DNR, Pew, ANES, BSA). A Semafor week is a **news file**, not a census.
8. **Falsifier** — “the algorithm radicalized me” with empty sorting/who-posts cells
9. **Grain** — a viral week ≠ a crest; a platform ≠ a worldview; a creator ≠ an electorate
10. **Institutions** — church, Stationers, BBC/FCC, platform ToS, ad market. Not an SMO.

If 1–5 will not fill, it is a story. If 1–5 fill and 7–8 will not, it is a **clean story** (a TED about the feed).

## 1. Oral / manuscript — priests hold the file

1. **Who holds the file:** clergy, scribes, memory specialists.
2. **Cheap:** speech in a room; manuscript is **dear**.
3. **Public:** parish, court, monastery. Time-biased (Innis).
4. **Who shows up:** the literate few + the assembled flock.
5. **Date:** not a start year — the residual never ends (sermon, lecture).
6. **Where:** don’t write “the West” as the whole oral world.
7. **Receipt:** *History* — Innis · Ong, *Orality and Literacy* · Clanchy, *From Memory to Written Record*. *Table* — literacy rates (late, sparse).
8. **Falsifier:** treating pulpit as “pre-modern” while TV preachers exist.
9. **Grain:** a sermon is an event. Manuscript culture is a **medium**.
10. **Institutions:** church, chancery.

**Method owed:** who could copy. Comparison: Islamic/Chinese manuscript worlds — not an extra tweet.

---

## 2. Print / pamphlet (~1450–)

1. **Who holds the file:** printer + licenser; then more printers than police.
2. **Cheap:** copy. Exit the old church; **new churches police**.
3. **Public:** republic of letters, pamphlet storms, Anderson’s print-capitalism.
4. **Who shows up:** the literate; oral remainder still majority for centuries.
5. **Date:** ~1450 intro; Reformation/scientific journal as **uses**; majority literacy much later.
6. **Where:** Europe first as **this** numbering; other xylography traditions are comparison, not a skipped people.
7. **Receipt:** *History* — Eisenstein, *The Printing Press as an Agent of Change* (**fight**: Johns, *The Nature of the Book*) · Darnton · Starr. *Table* — book output, literacy.
8. **Falsifier:** “Gutenberg caused modernity” as a single cell. Cheap copy is real; policing is real.
9. **Grain:** 1450 is intro. 1520s pamphlets are a **use**.
10. **Institutions:** Stationers, Index, postal.

**Method owed:** Eisenstein vs Johns as the periodization fight you actually read.

---

## 3. Mass press / radio (~19th c.–1930s)

1. **Who holds the file:** editor, wire, then state/spectrum.
2. **Cheap:** daily national reach; simultaneous **ear**.
3. **Public:** national “the news.”
4. **Who shows up:** mass literacy + ad model (Wu).
5. **Date:** steam press / telegraph / radio. 1930s radio politics is **use**, not a new number.
6. **Where:** US/UK path is not the only one (state radio elsewhere).
7. **Receipt:** *History* — Schudson · Starr · Barnouw (radio). *Table* — circulation, radio households.
8. **Falsifier:** hypodermic “radio made dictators.” Dictators used radio; the medium is not the party.
9. **Grain:** a broadcast ≠ a regime.
10. **Institutions:** AP/Reuters, BBC, FCC.

**Method owed:** household penetration. Comparison: BBC vs US commercial vs state radio.

---

## 4. Broadcast TV — one living room, few channels, shared evening

1. **Who holds the file:** scheduler + three-network-class oligopoly (US grain).
2. **Cheap:** moving image in the evening.
3. **Public:** shared national now. “The news” as a **time**.
4. **Who shows up:** almost everyone, passively.
5. **Date:** postwar; residual still huge for older DNR cohorts.
6. **Where:** US three-network vs UK duopoly vs other state TV.
7. **Receipt:** *History* — Williams, *Television* · Gitlin · Hallin, *The Uncensored War*. *Table* — Nielsen-class; DNR TV-news share by age.
8. **Falsifier:** “TV homogenized America” without Hallin/Sartori **systems**.
9. **Grain:** an evening newscast is a slot. The crest is **few channels**.
10. **Institutions:** networks, regulators, advertisers.

**Method owed:** share of evening vs later fragmentation. Don’t date this crest to a meme.

---

## 5. Cable / talk / 24hr — niches, still a schedule

1. **Who holds the file:** niche scheduler + talk host as proto-creator.
2. **Cheap:** more slots, still a **clock**.
3. **Public:** niches, not For You. Partisan TV is **sorting** you can name (Prior, *Post-Broadcast Democracy*).
4. **Who shows up:** people who **want** politics; others leave to entertainment niches (Prior’s key cell).
5. **Date:** 1980s–2000s US; other countries differ (sky/satellite).
6. **Where:** US Fox/MSNBC grain is not the UK.
7. **Receipt:** *History* — Prior · Wu. *Table* — cable ratings; Pew news-source.
8. **Falsifier:** blaming “the algorithm” for a **schedule** world.
9. **Grain:** 24hr is a format. Not a ranker.
10. **Institutions:** cable bundles, talk-radio syndicators.

**Method owed:** Prior: **choice** fragments the inattentive. That cell survives into platforms.

## 6. Platform / feed (~2004–)

1. **Who holds the file:** graph + ranker (**who you follow** + score).
2. **Cheap:** publish, reply, share. Break and ratio as political acts (**X grain**).
3. **Public:** networked; not one living room.
4. **Who shows up:** casuals leave; polarized partisans **keep posting** (ANES affect; Pew). Operational fact of this whole file.
5. **Date:** ~2004+ (FB); Twitter 2006. Residual: FB as **kin + leftover politics**.
6. **Where:** US graph ≠ WeChat ≠ WhatsApp publics.
7. **Receipt:** *History* — Gillespie · Benkler · Tufekci. *Table* — Pew platforms · ANES https://electionstudies.org/
8. **Falsifier:** ranker as sole cause. Empty **sorting** and **who still posts** = plot leak.
9. **Grain:** a trending week ≠ the crest. The crest is **graph + score**.
10. **Institutions:** platforms, ad auctions, ToS, app stores.

**Live adjacent:** X: politics-as-reason-to-use leads other apps (Pew/DNR — **re-pull**). Remaining posters more affectively polarized (**ANES**). Party clip dumps to mid-size influencers = **delivery tactic**, not a worldview.

**Method owed:** poster vs lurker. If you only sample posters, you invent a country.

## 7. For You / short video (~2018–)

1. **Who holds the file:** predicted watch; **not** (primarily) who you follow.
2. **Cheap:** clip; swipe; watch-time.
3. **Public:** entertainment-first; politics **hitchhikes** (TikTok animal).
4. **Who shows up:** 18–24s social-first, not site-first.
5. **Date:** ~2018 TikTok-class; Reels/Shorts copy. **Not** a new crest every viral week.
6. **Where:** DNR markets differ; global-majority creator news is often **higher** than Europe.
7. **Receipt:** *History* — still thin; don’t fake Innis. Use DNR methods chapters. *Table* — below.
8. **Falsifier:** “For You radicalized me” without **entertainment-first** and **hitchhike** cells. Also: creators **replace** newsrooms — DNR 2026 says mostly **alongside**.
9. **Grain:** a sound is an event. Predicted watch is the crest.
10. **Institutions:** ByteDance/Meta/Google short-video stacks, ads, ghost-creator mills.

**Live (do not mash years).**

**Social-first 18–24s; attention to creators vs brands:** Reuters Institute, *Understanding young news audiences* (24 Mar 2026), secondary analysis of **DNR through 2025**: 18–24s social-first; among social/video **news users**, **51%** pay most attention to creators/personalities vs **39%** to traditional news brands (55+ is flipped). https://reutersinstitute.politics.ox.ac.uk/understanding-young-news-audiences-time-rapid-change

**Creator reach (DNR 2026, global average, 48 markets):** **27%** some news from **news-focused** creators; **46%** from creators of **any** type; **3%** live on creators **alone**; most use them **alongside** brands. https://reutersinstitute.politics.ox.ac.uk/digital-news-report/2026/dnr-executive-summary

**Ghost creators:** paid faces, AI scripts, ad-revenue mills — Semafor 2026 as a **news file**. Re-check; not a DNR census.
