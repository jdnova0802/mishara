# Narratives field guide

A **decoder**. Not a tweet. Not a posting register. Not a second banner.

If a section would change the owned sentence, skip it in the composer. Edgy-legitimate, gender war, AI doom, sacred/villain/loyalty/restoration **as a costume** stay off posts. Those words live here as **spotting tools**.

---

## 1. What a narrative is (operationally)

A narrative is not “a lie.” It is a **meaning machine**:

**events → selected facts → moral plot → identity → action**

It answers, usually without saying so:

- What matters?
- Who is good / bad / naïve?
- What does my pain mean?
- What must be done?
- Who are my people?

If a story does those five jobs, it is doing narrative work — whether it is true, half-true, or false.

**Pain can be real while the plot is overfitted.** Literacy is not contempt.

---

## 2. The mobilizing skeleton (detector, not a law of nature)

Many **mass** narratives that move crowds use this spine:

1. **Sacred value** — something treated as non-negotiable
2. **Villain class** — a type of person/institution, not a tradeoff
3. **Proof from pain** — anecdote or statistic treated as total confirmation
4. **Loyalty test** — nuance = betrayal
5. **Promised restoration** — if our side wins, moral order returns

**Use this to spot recruitment.** Do not use it to write. Forcing every story onto this spine makes you see villains everywhere — including in a sentence that doesn’t have one.

**Counterexample (coordination without a camp):**  
“If a stranger can’t open it, it didn’t happen.”  
Exam, receipt, clock. No villain class. The pain (you can’t prove the act) can still be real. That is **operational fact**, not a mobilizing movie.

**Package that often accompanies the skeleton** (when it *is* a camp):

1. Your pain is legitimate  
2. It’s not mostly your fault  
3. There is a clear enemy  
4. Belonging is available if you pick a side  
5. History bends when your side wins  

That package beats policy memos because it converts confusion into **identity + mission**. Some people are in it because the memo never showed up. Don’t confuse **capture** with **stupidity**.

## 3. Two different powers of narrative

### A) Deep myths (stabilize)

Slow, mostly invisible. Feel like common sense.  
Examples: Progress, Meritocracy, Markets-as-morality, Technology-is-neutral, The-self-as-project.

### B) Mobilizing conflicts (energize)

Fast, tribal, sticky. Create camps.  
Examples: gender wars, culture wars, nationalism, elites-vs-people, AI doom/salvation.

Deep myths keep systems running.  
Mobilizing narratives organize attention and loyalty.  
**Platforms preferentially amplify the second.** That is why X is a different animal than email, LinkedIn, or a well-known JSON. Your owned posts refuse the second on purpose.

## 4. Narrative categories

| Category | Job | Feels like | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Deep myths** | Make the world feel natural | “Just how it is” | Progress, Meritocracy, Growth = good |
| **Mobilizing conflicts** | Create sides and energy | “Wake up / fight” | Gender war, woke vs anti-woke |
| **Identity scripts** | Tell you who you are | “People like us” | High-value, ally, patriot, victim class |
| **Scarcity dramas** | Explain who gets scarce goods | “The market is rigged” | Dating, housing, attention, jobs |
| **Panic–salvation** | Demand purification now | “Emergency” | Moral panics, apocalypse politics |
| **Hidden-hand** | Reveal offstage puppet masters | “I’m initiated” | Deep state, globalists, “they” |
| **Platform narratives** | Win the feed | “Everyone’s talking about this” | Clip wars, ratio cycles |
| **Operational facts** | Coordinate without a camp | “Can you open it?” | Receipt, exam, clock, bind/stop |

Most viral fights are hybrids: **scarcity + identity + panic**, selected by **platform** incentives.

**Operational facts can still be *used* as myths** (if you add a villain and a restoration). Don’t.

## 5. Deep myths most people swim in

1. **Progress = more** — growth/speed/new = good by default  
2. **Self as brand/project** — identity as something to curate and optimize  
3. **Meritocracy** — outcomes mostly earned; luck/structure underweighted  
4. **Technology is neutral** — tools as innocent; design/incentives ignored  
5. **Markets as morality** — price/demand treated as justice  
6. **Safety through control** — more surveillance/rules/metrics = safety  
7. **Freedom as consumer choice** — options multiply while agency may shrink  
8. **Permanent crisis weather** — urgency as default mode  
9. **Expertise vs vibes as rival religions** — credential worship vs distrust-as-virtue  
10. **The future will save us later** — defer hard costs into innovation hope  

**Test:** if removing the story would make the world feel suddenly optional, it was a deep myth.

Your public line sits against **#4** without joining **#6** as a camp: the tool is not innocent, and “more control theater” is not the product. The product is **a stranger can open whether the act counted**.

## 6. Current mass-moving narratives (map of the feed, not a menu)

Do **not** drill these as “what to speak this week.” Score them with §8 if they show up in a thread. Then **reply only if there is already a case** (an act, a bind, counsel, coverage). Do not hunt gender/culture/doom as the five.

Each *mobilizing* row below can be scored: sacred → villain → proof → loyalty → restoration. That is **their** movie.

| Fight | Scarce good under it | Skip unless |
|---|---|---|
| Gender / dating war | sex, care, respect, commitment, status | never an owned post; not a reply hunt |
| Culture war | dignity vs speech / “reality” | same |
| Race / identity grievance | dignity, safety, belonging, jobs | same |
| Class / elites vs people | housing, wages, medical, bailouts | reply only if the case is an act/cover/exam |
| Nationalism / decline | continuity, borders, industrial base | same |
| Immigration (twin movies) | order vs hospitality | same |
| AI salvation vs AI doom | jobs, uniqueness, safety, abundance | **adjacent heat** — still no camp; reply only if an agent **acted** |
| Health / “they lied” | body, kids, trust | same as gender: not our five |
| Climate emergency vs hustle | survival vs energy cost | reply only if a live grid/commit case |
| Attention / meaning crisis | focus, real life | never the campaign |

**AI doom/salvation** is the feed next to you. The owned sentence is not in that fight. A thread where an agent **deleted / wired / bound / ignored stop** is a **case**. A thread about pause vs accelerate is **mobilization**. Don’t confuse them.

## 7. Gender war as status-market story (literacy only)

Under the slogans, much of it is scarce goods: **sex • care • respect • commitment • future family • public status**.

Apps made comparison brutal. Many people meet the same system as “I am underchosen” or “I am overburdened / unsafe.”

Each side’s clip becomes the other side’s proof. The conflict is also a **content engine**.

**Usually missing from both movies:** winner-take-most apps, economic delay of pair-bonding, porn/feed expectation distortion, collapse of community matchmaking, wanting incompatible goods (freedom *and* devotion).

**Deep story underneath:** “My romantic outcomes are a verdict on my human value — and someone stole the fair verdict.”

That is why it outperforms tax policy. **It is not an X lane.** Followers, badges, and “spawning” attention after a status bump are **scarcity + visibility**, not a gender-war post.

## 8. Field checklist: spot a narrative in the wild

Score any viral fight. If **4+** hit, you are mostly watching mobilization:

1. Sacred value named as absolute  
2. One villain *class* (not incentives/tradeoffs)  
3. Personal pain offered as total proof  
4. Loyalty test (soft disagreement = betrayal)  
5. Moral movie ending (our win restores the world)  
6. Status payoff (belief makes you feel sharper/awake/superior)  
7. Platform fitness (clips, dunks, slogans, ratios)

### One-minute decode

1. What scarce good is this really about — sex, commitment, respect, safety, money, belonging, attention?  
2. Whose status rises if this frame wins?  
3. What tradeoff is being erased?  
4. What would remain true if the villain class disappeared tomorrow?  

If #4 still leaves most of the pain intact, the narrative is doing heavy lifting.

**Extra question for this shop:** is there an **act** (bind, wire, delete, deploy) a stranger could be asked to open? If no, it is not one of the five replies.

## 9. How narratives capture intelligent people

Smart people are not immune. They are often better at **rationalizing** a frame they already emotionally joined.

- **Identity first, evidence second**  
- **Selective empiricism**  
- **Moral glam** — feeling brave/compassionate for holding the line  
- **Enemy clarity addiction** — ambiguity feels like weakness  
- **Audience capture** — feed/friends punish defection  
- **Trauma → theory** — one wound becomes a total worldview  
- **Literacy capture** — using this guide as a costume (“I see the matrix”) which is just another status payoff  

Intelligence helps you win arguments **inside** the narrative. It does not automatically help you notice you are inside one.

## 10. How to use this without becoming owned by it

1. **Steelman both sides’ sacred values** before naming a villain.  
2. Separate **pain** (often real) from **plot** (often overfitted).  
3. Prefer **incentive explanations** over pure character explanations.  
4. Watch for **loyalty tests** — that is where thinking ends.  
5. Ask what would still be true after your preferred purge.  
6. Hold a private “maybe” longer than your side allows in public.  
7. Track what the narrative *gives you* (status, belonging, absolution). If the gift is large, scrutinize harder.  
8. **Do not post the decoder.** Owned X stays the receipt sentence. This page never ships as a thread.

You don’t need nihilism (“all stories are fake”).  
You need literacy: **stories are how humans coordinate — and how humans get recruited.**

---

## 11. Pocket definitions

- **Deep myth:** background story that feels like nature  
- **Mobilizing narrative:** conflict story that creates a crowd  
- **Identity script:** story of who “we” are  
- **Scarcity drama:** story about who gets limited goods  
- **Panic–salvation:** contamination + purification plot  
- **Hidden-hand:** offstage mastermind plot  
- **Platform narrative:** whatever the feed selects for attention  
- **Operational fact:** coordination rule that can be checked without joining a camp  

---

## 12. One-line summary

**Narratives turn pain into plot, plot into people, and people into power.**

The most important ones are either invisible (deep myths) or irresistible (mobilizing conflicts). Literacy means naming both while they work on you — **and not turning the naming into a third camp on X.**
