## Killed: the ESOP closing — mutualism at a shareholder price

**ERISA § 3(18)(B) and § 408(e), as restated on the Unified Agenda.** https://www.reginfo.gov/public/do/eAgendaViewRule?RIN=1210-AC20&pubId=202510 RIN 1210-AC20, publication id 2026. The agenda restates § 3(18)(B): for an asset other than a security for which there is a generally recognized market, adequate consideration means the fair market value of the asset as determined in good faith by the trustee or named fiduciary pursuant to the terms of the plan and in accordance with regulations promulgated by the Secretary of Labor. Section 408(e) is the prohibited-transaction exemption that lets the plan buy employer securities only on that condition. SECURE 2.0 Act § 346(c)(4)(B) tells the Secretary, with the Secretary of the Treasury, to issue formal guidance for acceptable standards and procedures for that good-faith fair market value.

The same agenda entry: stage is “Proposed Rule Stage.” Timetable action is “NPRM,” date “11/00/2026,” and the Federal Register cite is blank. “CFR Citation: Not Yet Determined.” “RIN Data Printed in the FR: No.”

**DOL fact sheet, 16 January 2025.** https://www.dol.gov/agencies/ebsa/about-ebsa/our-activities/resource-center/fact-sheets/notice-of-proposed-rulemaking-relating-to-application-of-the-definition-of-adequate-consideration EBSA has kept an ESOP National Enforcement Project since 2005. The fact sheet says investigations found repeated instances where a shareholder profited from an inflated valuation at the expense of the plan’s participants. The Department’s remedy in those cases was a process agreement with that case’s trustee. The fact sheet says the agreements, unlike a regulation, “are tethered to the specific facts and parties involved in each case.” The agreements that are public require the trustee to refuse a valuation advisor who previously worked for the seller, the sponsor’s deal team, or the party structuring the transaction, and to get that refusal in writing. One text: https://www.dol.gov/agencies/ebsa/about-ebsa/our-activities/enforcement/esop-agreement-appraisal-guidelines-horizon

The fact sheet also describes a proposal dated the same day. That proposal is not the rule. The 2026 agenda has no Federal Register cite for it.

**One line:** this is the intersection. Mutualism is the plan buying the firm for the workers. Shareholder primacy is the selling owner being paid a price. The statute says the price is adequate only under regulations, and the agenda says those regulations are not in the Federal Register. The determination that still closes the sale is the trustee’s good faith. A stranger’s copy of the appraisal would be the company’s nonpublic value. That is the first answer under criterion 4. A receipt that said only “the appraiser had not worked for the seller” is the written confirmation the settling trustees already have to obtain, and the Secretary already has the job of making that a rule.

**Object-per-hit:**

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Who attests | The trustee or named fiduciary, by the good-faith determination in ERISA § 3(18)(B). The appraiser’s report is the input. On the public process agreements, the trustee must also hold the appraiser’s written confirmation of no seller-side engagement. |
| What act depends on it | The plan’s purchase of employer stock. Without adequate consideration, § 408(e) does not exempt the purchase. |
| Who already gets paid to check | The trustee, as the fiduciary. EBSA, through the enforcement project the fact sheet dates to 2005. The Secretary, for the regulation the statute and SECURE 2.0 both assign, now scheduled as an NPRM in November 2026. |

**Seat:** assigned. Erra asks whether the plan should buy. Mishara asks whether the participants were harmed by the price. Neither question opens a seat in front of the trustee. Do not build a valuation mouth while the November 2026 NPRM is the Department’s own clock.

## Killed: the USMCA preference — the duty changes before anyone asks for the certificate

**19 C.F.R. § 182.11 and § 182.12, text on the eCFR page read 20 October 2026.** https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-19/chapter-I/part-182

Section 182.11(a): an importer may claim USMCA preferential tariff treatment, including an exemption from the merchandise processing fee, “based on a written or electronic certification of origin, as specified in § 182.12, completed by the importer, exporter, or producer.”

Section 182.11(b): the claim is made by putting the letters “S” or “S+” in front of the HTSUS subheading on the entry summary.

Section 182.12(a): the importer who makes that claim “must submit, at the request of CBP, a copy of the certification of origin.” The certification is not a document the entry summary has to carry.

Section 182.12(f)(1): the importer, exporter, or producer may complete the certification based on information the certifier has, or, if the exporter is not the producer, on “reasonable reliance on the producer’s written representation.”

Section 182.12(f)(2): “CBP may not require that an exporter or producer complete a certification of origin, or provide a certification of origin or written representation to another person.”

Section 182.12(d): a responsible official or authorized agent with knowledge of the facts signs and dates it.

The importer “is responsible for the truthfulness of the claim and of all the information and data contained in the certification of origin.” An incorrect claim is outside 19 U.S.C. § 1592 penalties if the importer promptly corrects it, pays the duties, and submits the statement the rule describes.

**19 C.F.R. § 182.72.** A claim “will be subject to such verification as CBP deems necessary.” CBP may use a questionnaire or a visit to the exporter or producer in Mexico or Canada. The applicability line in § 182.71 was amended by CBP Dec. 24-18, 90 FR 6489 (17 January 2025). The verification itself remains “as CBP deems necessary.”

**One line:** free trade is the preference. The mercantilist half is the word “originating.” The write that takes the money is two characters on the entry summary. The certificate can be the importer’s own, it can rest on the producer’s representation, and it is produced when CBP asks. That is a postponed check, not an empty one. CBP is the verifier. A product that held the producer’s cost file would fail criterion 4. A product that refused to transmit “S” until a stranger had re-done the origin analysis would be a second certifier the rule does not require, in front of a claim the rule already assigns to the importer and a verification it already assigns to CBP.

**Object-per-hit:**

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Who attests | The importer, the exporter, or the producer, by the certification of origin. The importer may rely on it and is responsible for the claim. |
| What act depends on it | Preferential duty, and the merchandise-processing-fee exemption, at entry, by the “S” or “S+” prefix. |
| Who already gets paid to check | CBP, under § 182.72, when it decides a verification is necessary. The importer, who must keep the records for five years and correct a claim the importer comes to believe is wrong. |

**Seat:** assigned. This is the Gate question — does the duty write complete — and the answer in the rule is that the prefix completes the claim. Do not build a pre-entry origin halt.

## Not an object: developmental award × competition review

**GAO-26-107882 and GAO-26-109121** already supply the CHIPS disbursement facts in `BUCKET_A_PE_HUNT_2026-10-20.md`. Commerce disburses after milestone proof. Those reports do not contain an antitrust clearance, an HSR waiting period, or a Federal Trade Commission countersignature as a condition of disbursement.

A 2024 policy paper recommends that CHIPS allocation be done in consultation with FTC antitrust enforcers. https://www.economicliberties.us/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/20240117-AELP-IndPolSeries-CHIPS-Paper_v4-1.pdf That is an argument, not a rule. Ordoliberalism would want a competition check before the state picks a champion. No instrument in the fixed corpora makes the disbursement depend on that check. Verra’s question — did both rails clear — has only one rail here. There is no second attestation to staple, and no gap to sell.

## What this pass refuses to call an invention

- A worker-ownership price check. The plan may buy only at adequate consideration. The trustee determines it. The Secretary owes the regulation and has it on the November 2026 agenda with no Federal Register cite. The appraisal is not a stranger’s file.
- A pre-entry halt on a USMCA “S” prefix. The certificate is completed by the importer, the exporter, or the producer, submitted when CBP asks, and verified when CBP decides to verify.
- A competition countersignature on a CHIPS disbursement. No GAO report states that the money waits on one. A paper that wishes FTC were in the room is not a rail.

The intersection worth remembering is the clock, not a new brand. One tradition pays at the write. The other tradition’s check is either not yet a regulation, or it runs only when the agency asks. In both live cases the statute already names who occupies that later check.
