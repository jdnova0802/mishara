# Bucket A — institutionalist and developmental-state pass (20 Oct 2026)

**Verdict:** no unoccupied seat. The structures this lens predicts are real, and each one already names who attests, what payment or credit depends on the attestation, and who is paid to check it. Do not open a new mouth off this pass.

Method: `BUCKET_A_METHOD.md` criterion 4, then object-per-hit (who attests / what act depends on it / who already gets paid to check), plus the acquisition-check and the “statute already assigns this seat” check. Time box: instruments and reports from 2024 forward. Real cite or cut. No market-size figure is stated below, because none was collected.

The source of candidates is the institutionalist and developmental-state reading, not a fresh GAO/OIG/DOJ docket sweep for its own sake. Douglass North: outcomes follow the rules that define property and who may enforce them. Chalmers Johnson: a developmental state steers selected industries and has to be able to condition the support. Peter Evans: state–business ties work only with autonomy; the failure mode is capture, where the state cannot discipline the firm it is inside. Ha-Joon Chang: industrial policy is a conditional bargain, not a grant that survives whatever the firm later does. A candidate counts only when that structure leaves a stranger-checkable act with nobody already paid to check it.

## CHIPS manufacturing incentives — Commerce pays after proof of the milestone

**GAO-26-107882, published 11 December 2025.** https://files.gao.gov/reports/GAO-26-107882/index.html (text read 20 Oct 2026). Scope is awards from September 2024 through July 2025. The report says it does not include amendments after July 2025.

As of July 2025, Commerce had awarded 19 companies $30.9 billion in direct funding for 40 projects, plus $5.5 billion in loans to two of them. Award documents identified 161 milestones, from November 2024 through October 2033, covering construction, equipment qualification, and minimum production. Companies had submitted completion reports for 24 of those 161 milestones. Commerce had also received 11 workforce-activity disbursement requests. Commerce had reviewed and verified 18 of 35 disbursement requests and had disbursed $6 billion. Officials told GAO that Commerce disburses after proof of milestone completion, as reimbursement of costs the company has already fronted, and that Commerce may require partial or full repayment if milestones and other terms are missed.

**GAO-26-109121, published 6 August 2026.** https://files.gao.gov/reports/GAO-26-109121/index.html (text read 20 Oct 2026). Since July 2025, Commerce awarded nine further projects, 49 projects across 24 companies in total, and amended awards for 14 companies. As of April 2026 the body of the report says Commerce had disbursed $13.1 billion of $31.2 billion in direct funding awards. The highlights print $31.5 billion for the same $13.1 billion and the same “approximately 42 percent.” Eleven awardees had been paid for 24 completed project milestones. About half of those were done by the anticipated date; all of them were done by the contractual due date. Nine other milestones were past the anticipated date and not yet due. GAO excluded Intel’s milestones from that count because of the August 2025 amendment, discussed below.

The same report’s R&D finding is a plan Commerce does not yet have. Commerce canceled awards representing $7.8 billion of the $11 billion appropriated for the advanced-microelectronics R&D activities, including the Natcast award for the National Semiconductor Technology Center, and GAO says Commerce has no detailed plan or timeline for reestablishing the NSTC, the National Advanced Packaging Manufacturing Program, and the Industrial Advisory Committee in line with the statute. That recommendation is addressed to Commerce. It is not a third-party verification seat.

**Commerce OIG, OIG-25-021-I, issued 3 June 2025.** https://www.oig.doc.gov/wp-content/OIGPublications/OIG-25-021-I.pdf The CHIPS Act funds the Office of Inspector General to assess eligibility, whether funds are used as required, whether recipients carried out worker and community-investment commitments, whether recipients were told what counts as a violation, whether the Secretary notified Congress of violations, and whether the Secretary reviewed the listed foreign-expansion exceptions.

**Commerce OIG, OIG-26-030-A, issued 24 September 2026.** https://www.oig.doc.gov/wp-content/OIGPublications/OIG-26-030-A-SECURED.pdf Objective: whether recipients met 15 U.S.C. § 4652 eligibility. OIG reviewed six agreements (TSMC, Intel, Micron, GlobalFoundries, Infinera, Corning), about $22.6 billion in direct funding and $5 billion in loan guarantees, and found the six eligible. NIST’s controls gave reasonable assurance on eligibility. OIG made no recommendation and named three risks it will keep monitoring: more than $36.3 billion of $36.4 billion announced was awarded inside ten weeks; three recipients received exceptions to use equipment from suppliers with ties to China; four of the six filed school or customer support letters with notably similar language. The report also restates the statutory clock: OIG oversight of use of funds, worker and community commitments, violation guidance, congressional notice, and foreign-capacity review is due no later than four years after the first disbursement. That is a dated duty of the Inspector General.

**One line:** the buyer of a milestone file benefits only if that file stays with Commerce. Outsiders are not the party the disbursement waits on. First answer if the product sells the nonpublic request. Second answer if the product is “Commerce can check before it pays” — and Commerce already does. Kill as a mouth.

**Object-per-hit:**

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Who attests | The awardee, in a milestone-completion report or a disbursement request. |
| What act depends on it | Commerce’s disbursement. GAO-26-107882: disbursement follows proof of milestone completion, and missed milestones can be clawed back. |
| Who already gets paid to check | The CHIPS Program Office, before it pays. The CHIPS Act pays Commerce OIG to assess use of funds and worker and community commitments. GAO has already read the award files twice. |

**Seat:** assigned. Do not build a CHIPS milestone mouth, a clawback mouth, or a “copied support letter” mouth. OIG already wrote the letter problem down.

## Intel, 27 August 2025 — the capture shape, with the checker still named

This is the Evans case, and it still fails the empty-seat test.

**Intel Form 8-K, amendment dated 27 August 2025, filed 29 August 2025.** https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/50863/000005086325000135/intc-20250827.htm and exhibit https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/50863/000005086325000135/a08272025form8-kex101.htm

The implementing amendment deletes Schedule B, the disbursement milestone schedule, and replaces it with “[Reserved].” It also reserves Annex D (program requirements), Annex E (Davis-Bacon), Annex F (reporting), and Annex G (workforce funding). Section 3: Commerce disburses the released funds after a Funding Obligation and a completed Disbursement Request for Released Funds whose certification is true. The form of request asks for $5,695,000,000. The 8-K states the company certified it had spent at least $7.865 billion in eligible project costs, and that on 27 August 2025 it received the $5.695 billion and issued Commerce 274,583,000 shares plus a warrant for up to 240,516,150 shares. Article 6 of the direct funding agreement does not apply to that disbursement request. Workforce-policy requirements were removed except as required by law. What remains, on the 8-K’s own list: no CHIPS funds for dividends or buybacks, no change-of-control with a foreign entity of concern, the foreign-capacity restriction, the joint-research and licensing restriction, and repayment remedies in Commerce’s hands if those remaining duties are breached.

GAO-26-109121, read against that filing: the August 2025 amendment removed project milestones not yet achieved; each Intel project is treated as complete once Intel has incurred eligible costs equal to the full award, $7.865 billion; Commerce found that Intel had already incurred those costs and treated the projects as complete at amendment; Commerce no longer requires progress reports on the removed milestones.

North’s point lands here. The state took an equity claim in the firm and, in the same bargain, accepted the firm’s certification in place of the milestone schedule a stranger could have been shown later. That is the embedded-autonomy failure: the steward is now an owner. It is not an unoccupied seat. Commerce is the party who found the costs incurred. OIG’s use-of-funds duty still names the Inspector General. GAO has already separated Intel from the other awardees in public and said it will keep examining the projects.

**One line:** a product that re-audits the $7.865 billion after Commerce accepted it is selling a second look at a file the counterparty already closed, or selling the file itself. The first of those is not an open statutory seat. The second fails criterion 4.

**Object-per-hit:**

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Who attests | Intel’s authorized officer, on the Disbursement Request for Released Funds. |
| What act depends on it | Release of the remaining $5.695 billion, and Commerce’s finding that the projects were complete. |
| Who already gets paid to check | Commerce, as the contracting party and now as shareholder. Commerce OIG, on the statute’s use-of-funds duty. GAO, which already reported the deletion. |

**Seat:** assigned to Commerce. Do not sell an independent “did Intel meet the deleted milestones” mouth. The milestones were removed by the parties. Restoring them is not a vacant verifier’s job.

## Transferable production credits — the seller attests, the IRS registers, the buyer has to hold the file

**IRS Publication 5884 (Rev. 7-2026), Elective Pay and Transfer Election Pre-Filing Registration User Guide.** https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/p5884.pdf A transfer of a credit under section 6418, which the guide covers together with the CHIPS and clean-energy credits, requires the transferor to register and receive a registration number. The election is valid only with that number, on a timely return.

**IRS transferability FAQ, Q2 and Q4, updated 25 April 2024.** https://www.irs.gov/credits-deductions/elective-pay-and-transferability-frequently-asked-questions-transferability Eligible credits include the advanced manufacturing production credit, section 45X. The seller has to complete pre-filing registration, give the buyer the registration number and the required minimum documentation, sign the transfer-election statement, and file. The buyer claims the credit from that package.

This is Chang’s bargain moved into the tax code: the state pays for a chosen kind of production, and the payment is a credit someone else can buy. The property in the credit is clear enough to be sold. The check on whether the production happened is the registration plus the return, not an empty public mouth.

**One line:** the buyer of the credit benefits by being able to show the registration number and the required documentation. That is the second answer, and the IRS already runs it. A product that sold the production file to someone who is not the buyer or the Service would be the first answer.

**Object-per-hit:**

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Who attests | The transferor, in the pre-filing registration and on the return that makes the election. |
| What act depends on it | The buyer’s claim of the transferred credit. No valid registration number, no valid election. |
| Who already gets paid to check | The IRS, which issues the number and examines the return. The buyer already has to keep the documentation the FAQ tells the seller to hand over. |

**Seat:** assigned. Do not build a §45X substantiation mouth.

## DOE fixed-amount awards — the verification method is a field on the milestone

**OCED, Fixed Amount Awards.** https://www.energy.gov/oced/fixed-amount-awards (page read 20 Oct 2026). Payment is organized by milestones. Each milestone states a number, a description, a verification method, a payment amount, and a completion date. The verification method is how the recipient shows the task is done: reports, vouchers, training sheets, subcontracts, permits, photos, videos, or another means OCED can analyze. OCED is the analyzer.

**2 CFR § 200.201(b),** fixed-amount awards. https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-2/subtitle-A/chapter-II/part-200/subpart-C/section-200.201 Payment is tied to the milestone agreed in the award. At the end, the recipient certifies to the federal agency that the project was completed, or identifies what was not, and the award is reduced for work not done. OCED’s October 2024 terms note updates citations after the Uniform Guidance revision effective 1 October 2024. It does not move the check off the Grants and Agreements Officer.

**One line:** the stranger who can check is the awarding office, by a method written into the award. Second answer, and the seat is OCED. Kill.

**Seat:** assigned. Do not build a parallel verifier for an OCED milestone whose verification method already names OCED.

## The corporatist report that was waived, and the wage report that was not

**DOE memorandum, 5 June 2025, Kenneth Michael Goff and Derek Passarelli.** https://www.eere-pmc.energy.gov/PMCRecipient/RefDocs/Waiver%20of%20Diversity,%20Equity,%20and%20Inclusion,%20Community%20Benefits%20Plans,%20and%20Justice40%20Reporting%20Requirements.pdf Effective immediately, DOE waives DEI, Community Benefits Plan, and Justice40 reporting on IIJA and IRA awards, and on annual-appropriation awards that carried those reports. The waiver lists the Updated Community Benefits Plan, the Community Benefits Report, Boosting Domestic Manufacturing, Training Outcomes, Good Jobs Outcomes, Permanent Jobs, Equity and Justice, and Pathway to Net Zero. The same page says the waiver does not apply to Davis-Bacon reporting, including payroll in LCPtracker and the DBA Compliance Report.

That is the corporatist bargain in the form the prompt asked about: a state–firm–community package whose “did you do it” report is no longer required. The duty was deleted. There is nothing left for a stranger to verify, and nothing to sell a mouth against. Do not build a Community Benefits Report product for a report the memorandum tells recipients to disregard.

What stayed is the wage side. **DOE, Davis-Bacon Act Requirements for IIJA recipients.** https://www.energy.gov/infrastructure/davis-bacon-act Section 41101 of the IIJA puts Davis-Bacon on IIJA construction. DOE determines coverage and the wage determination, reviews certified payrolls, and sends a semiannual compilation to the Department of Labor. **LCPtracker.** https://www.energy.gov/infrastructure/weekly-dba-payroll-tracking-lcptracker DOE contracted LCPtracker for weekly certified payrolls. It is free to IIJA recipients. A waiver from the software is only for a recipient who cannot use it, and that recipient still submits weekly paper payrolls to the agency financing the work.

**One line:** a Community Benefits file has no remaining act depending on it at DOE. A certified-payroll file is checked by DOE, through a system DOE already pays for. Both fail as an open seat. The first because the report is gone. The second because the seat is filled.

**Seat:** wage check assigned to DOE and, for the wage determination and the semiannual rollup, coordinated with the Department of Labor. LCPtracker is the occupied tool.

## New York Green CHIPS — the participant reports to ESD, at ESD’s discretion

**Excelsior / Green CHIPS amendments, ESD text of 5 NYCRR.** https://esd.ny.gov/sites/default/files/Excelsior-Regs-Green-CHIPS-Amendments.pdf A Green CHIPS community plan and a Green CHIPS sustainability plan are submitted in the Department’s form and approved at the sole discretion of Empire State Development. The community plan holds the worker and community commitments. The sustainability plan holds annual milestones and the greenhouse-gas commitments. A Green CHIPS report goes to the Department not less than annually and states performance against those milestones and commitments.

**Office of the State Comptroller, 2024-F-19, issued 15 April 2025.** https://www.osc.ny.gov/state-agencies/audits/2025/04/15/oversight-select-high-technology-projects-follow Follow-up of *Oversight of Select High-Technology Projects* (2017-S-60, issued 21 August 2020). Of that report’s three recommendations, one was implemented and two were partially implemented. The follow-up’s program description names Green CHIPS, and Micron’s planned megafab, as current ESD activity. The objective of 2024-F-19 is implementation of the 2020 recommendations on the projects that audit covered. It does not open a third-party seat on the annual Green CHIPS report. The attester is the participant. The reader is ESD.

**One line:** a product that explains ESD’s approval, or sells the community-plan file ESD has not published, benefits because outsiders do not have that file. First answer. Kill.

**Object-per-hit:**

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Who attests | The Green CHIPS participant, in the annual report and in the plans ESD may approve or not. |
| What act depends on it | ESD’s approval of the plans, and the participant’s continued standing in a program that pays on those commitments. |
| Who already gets paid to check | Empire State Development, which approves the plans at its sole discretion and receives the annual report. The State Comptroller already audits ESD’s high-tech oversight as a follow-up of the 2020 report. |

**Seat:** assigned to ESD. Do not build a Green CHIPS community-plan mouth.

## What this pass refuses to call a gap

- A CHIPS disbursement verifier. Commerce already verifies before it pays, and the statute already pays the Inspector General to assess use of funds and worker and community commitments.
- An Intel milestone restorer. The August 2025 amendment deleted the schedule, Commerce found the $7.865 billion already incurred, and Commerce took the shares. That is capture with a named counterparty, not a vacant mouth.
- A National Semiconductor Technology Center plan. GAO told Commerce to write one.
- A §45X or other §6418 credit file. The IRS registers it and the buyer has to hold the documents.
- An OCED milestone checker. The verification method is a term of the award, and OCED is the party who applies it.
- A Community Benefits or Justice40 report after the 5 June 2025 waiver. The report was waived. Davis-Bacon payroll was not, and DOE already reviews it through LCPtracker.
- A New York Green CHIPS monitor. The annual report runs to ESD. Selling ESD’s nonpublic file fails criterion 4.

No candidate from this lens cleared criterion 4 and also had an unassigned seat. The bar did not move.
