CORE LINE
"Economics" is not one subject.
It splits by SCALE (zoom), FIELD (topic), METHOD (how you prove), and SCHOOL (what you assume).
Political economy is the lane that refuses to pretend prices exist without power.

IN THIS REPO
PE (default in Gate chats) = political economy
Private equity = say "private equity" or "PE firms" explicitly (finance industry)

═══════════════════════════════════════
0) HOW TO USE THIS MAP
═══════════════════════════════════════
1. Pick a question (wages? inflation? empire? eviction?).
2. Find its FIELD.
3. Notice which SCHOOL is smuggling assumptions.
4. Ask the political-economy overlay: who is paid, forced, or status-rewarded?
5. Open primary series / docs before vibes.

═══════════════════════════════════════
1) BY SCALE (zoom level)
═══════════════════════════════════════
MICROECONOMICS
- Households, firms, prices, single markets
- Classic questions: supply/demand, elasticity, monopoly pricing

MACROECONOMICS
- Whole-economy aggregates: GDP, inflation, unemployment, interest rates, cycles
- Classic questions: recessions, stimulus, monetary policy

MESOECONOMICS (less common label)
- Industries, regions, sectors between firm and nation

═══════════════════════════════════════
2) CORE APPLIED FIELDS (topic lanes)
═══════════════════════════════════════
LABOR ECONOMICS
- Wages, unions, unemployment, discrimination, migration of workers

PUBLIC ECONOMICS / PUBLIC FINANCE
- Taxes, spending, deficits, welfare, public goods

MONETARY ECONOMICS
- Money, banking, central banks, inflation, payment systems

INTERNATIONAL ECONOMICS
- Trade, tariffs, exchange rates, capital flows, balance of payments

DEVELOPMENT ECONOMICS
- Growth, poverty, institutions, aid, industrial policy in poorer countries

INDUSTRIAL ORGANIZATION (IO)
- Firms, competition, monopoly, antitrust, platforms as markets

FINANCIAL ECONOMICS
- Asset prices, risk, corporate finance, market microstructure

HEALTH ECONOMICS
- Insurance, hospitals, pharma incentives, care rationing by price/policy

EDUCATION ECONOMICS
- Schooling, human capital, returns to credentials

URBAN / REGIONAL / HOUSING ECONOMICS
- Cities, rent, zoning, agglomeration, transport

ENVIRONMENTAL / RESOURCE / ENERGY ECONOMICS
- Pollution, climate, commons, oil/minerals, energy transition

AGRICULTURAL ECONOMICS
- Food systems, land, commodities, rural development

LAW AND ECONOMICS
- How legal rules reshape incentives (tort, contract, crime, property)

BEHAVIORAL ECONOMICS
- Psychology breaks "perfectly rational agent" models

INFORMATION ECONOMICS
- Asymmetric information, signaling, contracts, lemons markets

INSTITUTIONAL ECONOMICS
- Rules, property rights, organizations, enforcement

POLITICAL ECONOMY
- Power + interests + policy: who gets what, and who can force/paper it
- Closest to Gate Part 0

COMPARATIVE ECONOMIC SYSTEMS
- Capitalism, socialism, mixed regimes, varieties of capitalism

ECONOMIC HISTORY
- Past economies via archives + data (often the most honest PE cousin)

ECONOMETRICS
- Statistical methods used to test economic claims (a method field)

═══════════════════════════════════════
3) BIG SCHOOLS / TRADITIONS (worldviews)
═══════════════════════════════════════
These are assumption families — not course catalog folders.

CLASSICAL
- Smith / Ricardo / Marx era: value, growth, distribution, class

NEOCLASSICAL
- Optimization, supply/demand, equilibrium, marginal analysis

KEYNESIAN / NEW KEYNESIAN
- Demand shortfalls, sticky prices/wages, role for active policy

MONETARIST
- Money supply discipline; inflation as monetary phenomenon emphasis

AUSTRIAN
- Subjective value, capital structure, skepticism of central planning/fine-tuning

MARXIAN
- Class, exploitation, accumulation, crisis tendencies

INSTITUTIONALIST (old)
- Habits, law, orgs, technology — markets embedded in institutions

NEW INSTITUTIONAL
- Transaction costs, property rights, contracting (Coase / North / Williamson)

PUBLIC CHOICE
- Politicians and bureaucrats as self-interested actors (Buchanan et al.)

MMT (contested)
- Reframes constraints of currency-issuing governments; argue methods hard

FEMINIST / STRATIFICATION ECONOMICS
- Gender, race, care work, power inside "the market"

COMPLEXITY / EVOLUTIONARY
- Economies as evolving systems; path dependence; not always equilibrium

═══════════════════════════════════════
4) METHOD SPLITS (how claims get made)
═══════════════════════════════════════
THEORY
- Models, proofs, thought experiments

EMPIRICAL / APPLIED
- Data, natural experiments, RCTs (esp. development), historical series

STRUCTURAL / CALIBRATED MODELS
- Bigger models matched to moments in data

QUALITATIVE / ARCHIVAL / CASE
- Documents, orgs, process tracing (common in political economy + economic history)

Rule: method before number (same as Gate stats twin).

═══════════════════════════════════════
5) WHERE POLITICAL ECONOMY SITS
═══════════════════════════════════════
Political economy is not "economics but angry."
It is economics with the power variables left in:
- State force and legality (violence pyramid Layer 1-3)
- Class and ownership (oligarchs, rentiers, labor)
- Institutions and ideology (rules + stories)
- International hierarchy (hegemony, empire, dependency debates)

If micro says "price rose,"
PE asks "who could make that price stick — and who eats it?"

9) HARD LINE
═══════════════════════════════════════
A school is a lens, not a church.
A field is a zoom, not a personality.
If someone says "economics says," ask: which field, which school, which method, whose interest?

ONE LINE
Economics is a toolbox; political economy is the layer that keeps power inside the toolbox instead of pretending it isn't there.
