## 3. Definitions — learn in this order

One line each. Desk examples live in §4.

**Internalize top → bottom.** Do not skip ahead to AM Best / docket until Tier 1–3 are solid. §8 drill follows this same order.

### Tier 1 — Gate sentence (say these first)

**MAY** — What they say is bound (file, deck, MGA, CUO).  
**CAN** — What a stranger can verify from instruments that actually exist.  
**Instrument** — A checkable written thing that exists outside the meeting (policy, authority, LOC, treaty, guaranty, dec page). Not a deck slide. Not “they told us.”  
**Instruments** — Plural of instrument; the set of checkable papers behind a bind.  
**Stranger-verify** — A person with no inside access can check the instrument.  
**Bind** — Coverage in force: this risk, this paper, this authority, this time.  
**Named bind path** — One program / one paper / one miss. Not “your whole book.”  
**Paper** — Whose policy form and legal name the insured is buying.

### Tier 2 — Issue chain (quote → force)

**Quote** — Price/terms offered. Not bound.  
**Binder** — The short written proof a bind happened; not the same as a quote.  
**Issue** — The moment the policy/binder is put in force.  
**Dec page** — The first page of the policy: names, limits, dates, paper.  
**Insured** — The customer the policy covers.  
**Carrier** — The legal insurer on the declarations page. Who the insured sues.

### Tier 3 — Who holds the pen

**MGA** — Managing General Agent. Vendor contracted to underwrite/bind/administer on the carrier’s paper. Not the insurer.  
**Producer** — The broker/agent who sold the account. May or may not be the MGA.  
**Program** — One MGA + one carrier paper + one set of rules for a book of business.  
**Book** — The set of policies on that program.  
**Program agreement** — The contract that creates the pen, collateral rules, QS, and kill switches.  
**Authority / pen** — Written list of what the MGA may bind (line, limit, state, dates).  
**Own paper** — That carrier keeps the risk on its own capital.  
**Front** — Licensed carrier puts its name on the policy; most risk is supposed to sit behind it. Front still owes the insured if the back fails.  
**CEO** — Chief Executive Officer. Runs the company.  
**CUO** — Chief Underwriting Officer. Owns bind quality.  
**CURO** — Chief Underwriting and Reinsurance Officer (Lucas’s title). Still a CUO-class desk.  
**CFO** — Chief Financial Officer. Sends deficit/collateral letters.

### Tier 4 — Fire-four money papers (memorize before mail)

**Collateral** — A named instrument meant to pay this beneficiary if a stated trigger hits. Not “they have money.”  
**Beneficiary** — Who the instrument pays (usually the carrier).  
**LOC** — Bank’s irrevocable promise to pay the beneficiary on a proper draw, up to a set amount.  
**Draw** — Beneficiary demands the bank pay on the LOC.  
**Honor** — Bank actually pays the draw. Fail to honor = collateral was not live.  
**Swap** — New instrument replaces the old; old is returned, expired, or undrawable.  
**Replacement as extra** — New instrument accepted *and* the old one is still drawn. Same obligation counted twice.  
**Top-up** — Extra collateral because losses grew. Real. Not the same as replacement-as-extra.  
**Trust / funds withheld** — Other collateral forms: cash sitting in a trust or held back by the carrier.  
**Guaranty** — Written promise (often the MGA’s parent) to pay the carrier what the program agreement says is due. Promise ≠ cash.  
**Reinsurance** — Insurance the *carrier* buys so someone else takes part of the carrier’s risk.  
**Reinsurer** — Takes a share of the *carrier’s* risk. If they leave, the carrier still owes the insured.  
**Treaty** — Reinsurance on a whole book.  
**Facultative** — Reinsurance on one risk.  
**QS (quota share)** — Reinsurer takes a fixed % of premium and the same % of losses.  
**Excess-of-loss** — Reinsurer pays only above a number. Not QS.  
**PNOC** — Reinsurer’s written notice: we cancel, effective [date].  
**Deficit** — After contract math, one side owes the other cash.  
**Deficit letter** — Carrier (often CFO) stating that amount is due now.  
**Bordereau** — The report of premiums/losses used to do that math.  
**Captive** — An insurer owned by the people whose risk it covers. Often sits behind a front.  
**Capacity** — Who is actually taking the risk/limit behind the paper.  
**Capital** — Money the carrier must hold against claims.  
**Non-admitted asset** — Asset regulators may not count as capital. SteadPoint’s stuck collateral sits here in the AM Best writeup.

### Tier 5 — Funnel and money (how you sell)

**Desk** — The person/inbox who can bind or stop a bind.  
**Door** — The published email you actually send to.  
**Intake** — A general door (Partnerships@, tmaner@) that may forward inside.  
**Bounce** — Mail server rejects the address. Then use the fallback door.  
**CC** — Extra recipient sees the mail. Don’t CC press or a guessed CEO.  
**Published** — On their site or a checkable filing. Not LeadIQ.  
**Fire** — Dated, sourced miss (docket / AM Best quote / their site).  
**Test** — Old CUO four. Curiosity mail. Do not send.  
**Silence** — No reply. End.  
**Follow-up** — You email again after silence. Forbidden on this batch.  
**Ignore** — Allowed close of the cold email.  
**Forward once** — They send it to the right desk. You still don’t chase.  
**Operator-local** — Your clock, not UTC. Tuesday 9:00 is *your* 9:00.  
**Memo** — The written 72h REVIEW deliverable. Email/PDF. Not a call.  
**REVIEW** — Their reply word. You write a free 72h memo. $0.  
**Deposit** — Optional $2,500 to go past the free memo.  
**Full review** — Paid $5–8k. Quit bar when it *clears*.  
**Retainer** — Optional $10–40k/mo if they keep you. Invoice to the carrier, not take-home.  
**Invoice** — They were billed. Not cash.  
**Clears** — Money is in the Nisaba account, not promised.  
**Stripe** — How they pay. Fees come out.  
**CHARGE-only** — Live Gate sells. No fake “we’ll bill later” except the free 72h front.

### Tier 7 — Source language (after the miss is clear)

**Reserve** — Money the carrier estimates it will need for claims. Not a deficit. Not cash paid.  
**ADR** — Adverse development: claims costing more than the reserve. Not a Gate bind miss.  
**RBC** — Regulatory capital ratio. Not a Gate bind miss.  
**AM Best** — Rating agency for insurers. A downgrade is not automatically a bind miss.  
**FSR** — Financial Strength Rating (e.g. A- , B++).  
**Under review negative** — AM Best may cut the rating further. Still not a bind miss by itself.  
**Q2** — Second quarter (Apr–Jun).  
**Docket** — The court’s public file for a lawsuit (e.g. SDNY 1:25-cv-10463).  
**SDNY** — U.S. District Court, Southern District of New York.  
**N.D. Ill.** — U.S. District Court, Northern District of Illinois.  
**As alleged** — What the complaint claims. Not a verdict.  
**Counsel** — Their lawyer. If they can’t find your source in five minutes, cut the line.

### Tier 9 — Code (also §6; last)

**Cursor / agent** — Writes code. You direct. You narrate before it runs.  
**Scaffold** — It drafts; you check.  
**Function** — Named block: inputs, outputs, side effects.  
**Side effect** — It changes something outside its return (disk, network, DOM).  
**Variable** — A named value.  
**Loop** — Repeat.  
**Conditional** — If this, then that.  
**Frontend** — What the browser shows.  
**Backend** — What the server does.  
**Database** — Where records live.  
**API** — Request in, response out (status + JSON).  
**JSON** — Structured text the API speaks.  
**Status** — Number on the response (200 ok, 404 missing, 500 server broke).  
**URL** — Address you hit.  
**Env / secrets** — Config that is not in the repo. Never hardcode a key.  
**Hardcoded** — Secret or config typed into source. Bad.  
**Validation** — Check stranger input before using it.  
**Error handling** — What the code does when it fails.  
**List / dict / set** — Ordered items / named fields / unique items.  
**Schema** — Agreed shape of the data. Changing it hurts.  
**SQL** — SELECT / WHERE / JOIN enough to read what the agent wrote.  
**Commit** — A saved snapshot.  
**Branch** — A parallel line of work.  
**Merge** — Combine a branch.  
**PR** — Pull request: ask to merge. Review before it lands.  
**Weld** — Shove unrelated files onto a product PR. Don’t.  
**N+1** — Loop that hits the database once per row instead of once.  
**Auth** — Who is allowed to hit this. Missing auth = open door.  
**Don’t trust input** — Validate what strangers send.  
**SQL injection** — Stranger’s text treated as a database command.  
**XSS** — Stranger’s text treated as page code.  
**HTTPS / DNS** — Encrypted site / name → server.  
**System design** — Sketch pieces and where data lives — only for a product you already have.
